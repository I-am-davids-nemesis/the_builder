### The first goal, as the handoff paraphrased it

Character profiles complex enough to:

1. Drop a character into any situation and predict **how they will react**.
2. Know **the voice they use**, with voices distinct enough to tell characters apart.

The design principles, as the handoff recorded them from the writer:

- **Stories are more interesting when characters have both internal and external tensions.** Every character needs both.
- **Series-level tension must last.** Each book resolves only small pieces, and the root conflicts stay open.

### The idea for the book, by October 9

A new novel that **follows the plot of an existing book** but tells it differently:

1. **A different narrative technique.** The original lives inside Tor's head. This book doesn't. Tor's traits show through action, dialogue and other people's reactions.
2. **No naive filter.** In the original, Tor's preconceptions hide the real world, and the reader learns the truth only as he does. Here, mysteries stay hidden through **which scenes are shown and who is present**, and the reader may sometimes know more than Tor.
3. **More description.** Places, objects, magic, bodies in motion, weather, food, light. Every setting does work. Places are pressure.
4. **Distinct characters.** Cover the speaker tags and a reader should still know who is talking.

The original's plot beats are kept. Everything else (prose, structure, scene construction, interiority, description) is new. The source is mined for facts and never imitated. Each fact is labelled (canon), (assumed) or (invented).

### The story underneath (the original's premise, from the blurbs)

Tor Baker, a baker's son from the deep woods, studies magic on a special scholarship from the king. He is a **builder**, one of those who create the magic that makes the kingdom of Noram work. His first project might fail, and taking unscheduled time off to work on it risks expulsion. The project draws the attention of very powerful people. The world he thought he knew is a lie, and he is caught in noble intrigue that began before he was born. His innocence is a liability: a few simple mistakes could put him on the gallows.

### What was still open at the start

These were the decisions the October 1 handoff asked for. Answering them shaped the book, and `journal.md` should record how each was settled.

1. The roles of Rolph, Patricia ("Trice"?), Sarah (the canon "Sara"?), Dorian and Clark.
2. Faithful to canon, or a reimagining where invented material is fine.
3. A name for the builder instructor, and for the headmaster.
4. The Missouri–Mississippi feud as the series spine, or something else.
5. How far into the series to plan: book 1, the first nine-book Tor arc, or beyond.
6. **Which narrative mode** replaces the deep internal POV. The candidates were third person with shifting viewpoints, an omniscient narrator, and a camera-like external narrator. Both camera and rotating third were drafted. Rotating third won, and camera moved to its own branch on October 30.
