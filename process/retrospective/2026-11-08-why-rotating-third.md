# Why rotating third, and what it's for

_Written by Claude on November 8, 2026. It follows up on `2026-11-08-meta-story.md`, which noted that no reason was recorded for choosing rotating close third over the camera narrator on October 27. The writer answered that question the same day. This file records the answer and what it changes in the story of the story._

## The answer

In the writer's words:

> One of my beta readers liked it more than the camera as it provided a little insight into the characters.

So the choice came from outside the conversation with Claude: a reader. That makes it the first beta reader to appear anywhere in the records, and it fits the wish in the writer's November 8 journal entry to have captured "all the conversations I have had with people about the book." The meta-story guessed that Green's arrival on the same day had something to do with the choice. It didn't, or at least that wasn't the reason given. The deciding reason was a reader wanting "a little insight."

## The tension the writer named with it

The writer called the choice "interesting" and said it "could lead to a long term problem," because the book has a goal beyond itself:

> One goal is to be able to take a book written in this way, and be able to repurpose it and retell the story in different ways. One way is from different POV; another way is to convert it into a screenplay -- which will require a lack of narration, descriptions that are visual, storyboarding (probably with sketches rather than photorealistic images).

And what that goal asks of the characters:

> But, it does seem important to gather the characters as fully fleshed out people, having a POV, a continuity, a motivation, sufficient (but not too much) consistency.

This is new to the records. Nothing in `engine.md`, the journal or `AGENTS.md` names repurposing as an aim. `AGENTS.md` only says the user "may regenerate scenes and chapters from consistent viewpoints, but not yet," which now reads as a first glimpse of it.

## How it changes the story so far

Seen through this goal, several turns in the meta-story look less like separate decisions and more like one direction:

- **The October 1 seed was already about this.** The first request was never a novel. It was character profiles complete enough to "drop a character into any situation and predict how they will react" and to know their voice. That is the same requirement as "fully fleshed out people" who can be carried into another POV or onto a screen. The book has come back to its seed.
- **The camera experiment wasn't a dead end.** The camera narrator was close to the screenplay's needs: no narrated thought, everything visible. Its nineteen days of drafts sit on the `camera` branch. The meta-story called it "boxed up rather than thrown away," and that now matters more than it seemed to.
- **Rotating third already leans toward the screen.** Tor's viewpoint is scarce, his thoughts are never narrated, and his inner life shows through muttering, his body and the room. No Ancient ever holds the viewpoint. Most of what the reader knows about Tor and Green would survive losing the narration.
- **The "little insight" is what wouldn't survive.** The beta reader liked rotating third for what the viewpoint characters notice and feel. A screenplay has no narration for that, so every insight would need a visible or audible counterpart. Today's dinner hints show where the problem lives. Hale notices that Burks's silk is royal-guard purple, and Tor doesn't. On the page that's Hale's noticing. On screen it needs a shot of the silk and a shot of Hale's eyebrows.
- **"Sufficient (but not too much) consistency"** fits the reaction rules in the codex, and the rule that a character acting against them has to earn it. The writer's "not too much" is new. It warns against characters so predictable that a retelling has nothing to discover.

## Where this should be recorded

This retrospective can only describe the turn. The decision itself belongs in files outside `process/retrospective/`, which a normal panel session can update:

- `engine.md`: Form (or Promise), with an entry under Changes dated November 8: the book is written so it can be retold from other viewpoints and adapted as a screenplay.
- `journal.md`: a Writer or Claude entry giving the beta reader as the reason rotating third won, pointing back to the October 27 entry.
- `AGENTS.md` §3 or §4, if the writer wants drafting to follow from it (see the questions).

**Questions:**

1. **Recording the goal:** should repurposing (other POVs, a screenplay) go into `engine.md` as part of the book's form?
   - Default: Yes, under Form, with a Changes entry for November 8 and a journal entry naming the beta reader.
   - Option: Keep it in `ideas.md` as a later project, not a constraint on this draft.
2. **Insight with a visible twin:** when a viewpoint character notices or feels something, should drafts also give it something a camera could see (a gesture, an object, a line)?
   - Default: Yes, as a soft rule: aim for it, and flag scenes where the insight can't be shown.
   - Option: No; keep the prose free, and solve it scene by scene at adaptation time.
3. **The camera branch:** should it be treated as a resource for the screenplay, not just an archive?
   - Default: Yes; note in `AGENTS.md` that the camera drafts of chapters 1–5 are reference for visual adaptation.
   - Option: Leave it as an archive; a screenplay would start fresh from rotating third.
4. **Not too much consistency:** should the codex track where each character is allowed to surprise, alongside their reaction rules?
   - Default: Yes; add a short "where they break pattern" line to each main character's profile when next revised.
   - Option: No; the existing rule that breaking pattern must be earned is enough.
