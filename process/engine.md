# Engine

## Premise

Tor Baker is a baker's son from the deep woods. He is twenty but looks about fifteen, and he studies at the Lairdgren School on a royal scholarship. The school is in the kingdom of Noram, on a far-future, post-collapse Earth. He is a builder, one of the rare people who create new magic rather than copy it. Each thing he builds draws more powerful people to him: merchant houses, a royal guard who suspects him, the crown, and eventually the Ancient who runs Noram. The people closest to him are not what he takes them for. His roommate Rolph is the prince, his friends Trice and Sara are training at a spy school, and the "servant" Burks is Green. Tor misreads how all of them see him, and his innocence could put him on the gallows. The book follows the plot beats of P.S. Power's *The Builder* (2012) and tells them in its own way.

## Central question

The material never states it as a single question. These are the questions the drafts keep open:

- Who is Tor really, and who arranged his upbringing? The codex settles him as a clone of Count Lairdgren (Green), a series-long secret the book only hints at.
- Who is moving against him, in a plot that began before he was born?
- Will his naivety about nobles cost him before he understands his place among them?

The writer should name which of these the reader is meant to keep reading for.

## Engine

- **The loop** (`AGENTS.md` §5): Tor builds something, the build causes complications, and the complications give him his next idea. Chapter one runs the loop once. Each later turn raises the stakes:
  - school rules (the drying field, missed classes);
  - money and merchant houses (ch. 2);
  - the law and treason (ch. 3);
  - weapons that matter to the crown (the shield and flying, ch. 4);
  - a whole county (the Ford water line, ch. 7–9).
- **One turn where the complication wins:** in chapter three Tor is too spent to build. The next idea is left empty on purpose.
- **The perception gap:** Tor misreads how others see him. He fears nobles who like him, takes proposals as jokes and declared friendships as quips. The reader sees the mismatch.
- **Secrets around him:** Rolph's royalty, the spy school, Wensa's guard duty, Green, Trice's rage field, the rage epidemic. These stay hidden through scene selection and through who is present, never through a naive narrator.
- **Wants that pull against each other:**
  - Tor quietly makes things for people and doesn't care about the money.
  - Kolb pushes him to build.
  - His friends and their houses have interests of their own in his work.
- **Places are pressure.** Each setting squeezes a different tension:
  - the school: rules and rank;
  - the capital: open politics;
  - the beach house: what can't be said at school;
  - the town: money and ordinary lives;
  - Two Rivers: Tor's past.
- **Series rule:**
  - Every character always has one internal and one external tension.
  - Each book resolves about one thread, advances two or three, and opens one.
  - Root conflicts stay open.

## Promise

- **Genre and series:** a magic-school fantasy of builds and noble intrigue, the first book of a series.
- **Tone:** as light and funny as possible. The danger is real, but the comedy comes from:
  - the gap between how Tor reads a situation and what is actually happening;
  - physical comedy;
  - the calm of everyone around him.
- **The joke is never on Tor's intelligence.** He is brilliant and wrong about people.
- **Running jokes:** escalating numbers, and Tor's courtesy out of all proportion to the danger.
- **Rich, specific description.** Every setting does work.
- **Distinct voices.** Cover the speaker tags and the reader still knows who is talking.
- **Content:** non-sexual throughout. The original's sexual content is dropped.
- **Payoff:** each book closes small, concrete questions, such as the shield working or the river running, and leaves the root conflicts open.

## Form

- **Narration:** rotating close third. The viewpoint shifts scene by scene or section by section.
- **Tor holds the viewpoint only when he is alone or something traumatic happens to him.** Otherwise the scene goes to someone watching him.
- **No Ancient ever holds the viewpoint.**
- **A secret-keeper may hold the viewpoint** if the scene stays on action, with only word choice hinting that something isn't quite honest.
- **Tor's thoughts are not narrated.** They show through behavior, his muttering, his body and the room.
- **Idiom:** American.
- **Structure:**
  - Chapters are folders of numbered scene files, compiled into whole chapters and the book.
  - Chapters 1–4 and 6–9 are drafted, chapter 9 through scene 5.
  - The chapter 5 outline still has an empty "What the chapter must do."
- **Target length:** not stated.
- **Not yet settled:**
  - how far the series is planned (books 2–5 are not planned);
  - whether the book is faithful to canon or a reimagining (still listed as open in `AGENTS.md` §8).
