## 1. Project goal (in the user's words, paraphrased)

Build a set of character profiles for *The Builder* that are complex enough to:

1. Drop a character into any situation and predict **how they will react**.
2. Know **the voice they use**, with voices distinct enough to tell characters apart.

Design principles the user stated:

- **Stories are more interesting when characters have both internal and external tensions.** Every profile needs both.
- **Series-level tension must be maintained long term.** Each book resolves only small pieces of the overall story tension; root conflicts stay open.

### Cast the user listed

- **Main characters:** Tor, Rolph, Patricia, Sarah, Dorian, Clark (son of Count Missouri).
- **Supporting characters:** David (fifth son of Count Mississippi), Count Mississippi, Carl (combat instructor), Miriam (accounting professor), the school headmaster, the builder instructor (unnamed), Sophia (meditation instructor).
- **Places:** the school, the town around the school, Two Rivers (Tor's hometown), the capital, a beach resort house, a city of Gallaia.

### Narrative approach and source-text rules (added October 9, 2026)

This book will **not** be told from deep inside Tor's head, even though the original is. Source text is mined for characters and facts, never for prose style.

**How to use source text (the user will paste the opening pages)**

- Extract who characters are: traits, dispositions, relationships, and how they behave.
- Extract world and plot facts, marked **(canon)**.
- Ignore the original's tone, sentence style and narrative voice; don't imitate them.

**What the original does, and what we keep or drop**

- The original opens with a little description, then drops straight into Tor's internal perspective.
- That gives direct examples of his personality. **Keep the traits; drop the delivery.** Here they show through action, dialogue and other characters' reactions.
- It also hides the real world behind Tor's preconceptions, so the reader learns the truth only as he does. **We don't use that device.** The mysteries need another way to stay hidden.

**Consequences for the bible** (Claude's inference, not yet confirmed)

- Reaction rules and voice carry more weight, since Tor's thoughts won't be narrated.
- Tor's self-doubt must be visible from outside: apologies, deflections, overwork.
- With no naive filter, secrets stay hidden through scene selection and who is present. The reader may sometimes know more than Tor (dramatic irony).

**Open question:** which narrative mode replaces it — third person with shifting viewpoints, an omniscient narrator, or an external camera-like narrator?

## 5. The character bible (full content)

### 5.1 How to use it

Each profile is built so you can drop a character into a scene and predict both what they do and how they sound.

**Profile template (main cast):**

- **Role** — who they are to Tor and to the plot.
- **Want vs. need** — what they chase, and what would actually fix them.
- **Internal tension** — two things they believe that can't both win.
- **External tension** — the person, faction or rule pushing on them.
- **Reaction rules** — if/then triggers for predicting behavior.
- **Voice** — diction, rhythm, tics, and words they never use.
- **Sample lines** — calibration for the voice.
- **Series slice** — what book 1 resolves and what it leaves open.

**Tension rule:** each book resolves one small, concrete piece per character (a question answered, a relationship shifted) while the root conflict survives. If a book resolves a character's root conflict, that character's arc is over.

### 5.2 Tor Baker (Torrance Green Baker)

Tor is a genius who believes he is a nuisance, and every scene with him should test that belief.

- **Role:** Scholarship builder student from Two Rivers; the kingdom's hidden piece in a game older than he is. (canon)
- **Want:** To be useful, to not cause trouble, and to make his family proud. (assumed)
- **Need:** To accept that his worth doesn't depend on being useful to others, and to ask who he is, not just what he can make.
- **Internal tension:** Deep conviction that he is small and ordinary vs. mounting evidence that he is extraordinary. He resolves the contradiction by assuming there's been a mistake. (canon-adjacent)
- **External tension:** Nobles and older powers maneuvering around him; school rules he keeps breaking by accident; a family history that doesn't add up. (canon)

**Reaction rules**

- **Praised** → deflects to luck, a teacher, or the materials; changes the subject to the work.
- **Asked for help** → says yes before the sentence ends, then overcommits.
- **Threatened himself** → goes quiet and polite; fear shows as extra courtesy.
- **A friend threatened** → the meekness vanishes; he solves the problem with frightening, disproportionate competence, then apologizes for the mess.
- **Handed a technical puzzle** → forgets food, sleep, and the people in the room.
- **Faced with noble social customs** → embarrassment, literal-mindedness, and a strong wish to be somewhere else.

**Voice**

- Diction: plain, rural, respectful; "sir" and "ma'am" by reflex, even to peers who outrank him.
- Rhythm: short hedged sentences that stretch into long, precise run-ons the moment he talks about building.
- Tics: "I'm sorry," "I didn't mean to," "It's not really anything," baking metaphors from home.
- Never: boasts, swears casually, or uses a title as a weapon.

**Sample lines**

- "It's not really anything. The pattern just wanted to go that way, so I let it."
- "I'm sorry, sir. I didn't know that was against the rules. I didn't know there were rules about it."
- "Please step back from her. I'd hate for this to get worse." (said quietly, which is the warning)

**Series slice:** Book 1 answers "Can Tor actually build?" (yes) and "Is he in danger?" (yes). It leaves open who he really is, who arranged his life, and whether his humility is his own.

### 5.3 Rolph

Rolph is Tor's loud, loyal shield who is quietly afraid he'll only ever matter as Tor's friend.

- **Role:** Tor's closest friend at school; a noble's son whose mother, Connie, takes an interest in Tor. (Connie is canon; the rest is assumed)
- **Want:** To be the one who protects Tor and shows him how the world really works.
- **Need:** To find a talent or purpose that is his own, not borrowed from his family or his friend.
- **Internal tension:** Genuine love for Tor vs. envy of how easily Tor surpasses everyone. He'd die before admitting the second.
- **External tension:** Family expectations and his mother's agenda. He suspects Connie's warmth toward Tor is partly strategy, and he's not sure he minds.

**Reaction rules**

- **Tor insulted** → steps physically in front of him, big and grinning, before anyone else reacts.
- **Tor achieves something huge** → celebrates loudest in public; goes quiet for a while in private.
- **Asked about his own magic or future** → jokes, changes the subject, or starts a fight.
- **Family politics surface** → plays dumb, but catches every detail and reports it to Tor later.

**Voice**

- Diction: casual noble slang, nicknames for everyone ("Little Tor," "Baker").
- Rhythm: fast, overlapping, answers his own questions.
- Tics: teasing Tor about his prudishness; claps shoulders; "Trust me."
- Never: speaks formally unless he's truly angry. Formality from Rolph is a red flag.

**Sample lines**

- "Trust me, Baker, nobody here is as scary as they want you to think. Well. Almost nobody."
- "You built that in a week? Of course you did. Of course you did."
- "My lord, I believe my friend has finished speaking with you." (cold and formal; he's about to swing)

**Series slice:** Book 1 proves Rolph's loyalty under pressure. It leaves open his envy, his own purpose, and whose side his family is really on.

### 5.4 Patricia ("Trice")

Patricia sees the political board more clearly than anyone Tor's age, and she can't decide whether he's a friend or her best move.

- **Role:** A sharp noble student fluent in court maneuvering; Tor's guide to power and occasionally its instrument. (assumed; later-series readers mention a character called Trice, possibly her)
- **Want:** Leverage. To be the person who saw Tor's value first.
- **Need:** To trust one person without calculating the return.
- **Internal tension:** Real affection for Tor vs. a worldview in which affection is a liability. She tells herself the fondness is part of the strategy.
- **External tension:** A family that expects her to deliver an advantage, and rivals (Clark's house, possibly Mississippi) who see Tor as a prize to be claimed.

**Reaction rules**

- **New information** → files it, reveals none of it, and asks one more question than seems polite.
- **Tor is naive in public** → rescues him smoothly, then lectures him in private.
- **Caught in a manipulation** → doesn't deny it; explains why it was the right call.
- **Genuinely moved** → gets brisk and changes the subject to logistics.

**Voice**

- Diction: precise, courtly, vocabulary of trade and debt ("owe," "cost," "position").
- Rhythm: clipped statements; rhetorical questions that are really instructions.
- Tics: calls Tor "Master Baker" with ironic formality; "Think, Tor."
- Never: says "I don't know." She says "Not yet."

**Sample lines**

- "Think, Tor. Why would a count's son eat lunch with a baker's boy? Because he wants something. Find out what."
- "Of course I used you. You were useful. That's not the same as not caring."
- "Not yet. Give me a day."

**Series slice:** Book 1 establishes that Patricia will protect Tor when it counts. It leaves open whose interests she serves, and whether she'll ever stop calculating.

### 5.5 Sarah

Sarah is the person who treats Tor as a person rather than a phenomenon, which is exactly why her secret, if she has one, would hurt most.

- **Role:** Tor's steadiest friend and the emotional center of his school life. (assumed; readers name a "Sara" as a fan-favorite companion for Tor)
- **Want:** A life that's hers, not decided by her family's rank or by the storm around Tor.
- **Need:** To stop shrinking herself to keep the peace. She and Tor share that flaw.
- **Internal tension:** Wanting to stay close to Tor vs. seeing that closeness to him is dangerous and draws attention she never wanted.
- **External tension:** Lower standing than Patricia or Clark's circle (assumed), so she's the easiest person for Tor's enemies to pressure.

**Reaction rules**

- **Tor is overworking** → physically removes the tools and puts food in front of him.
- **Snubbed by a noble** → polite and unbothered on the surface; cries later, alone.
- **Tor is in danger** → runs toward it, not away, even without a plan.
- **Asked to choose between Tor and her family** → stalls, bargains, and tries to find a third option. That indecision is her main flaw.

**Voice**

- Diction: warm, direct, everyday words; uses Tor's name often.
- Rhythm: gentle questions, then one blunt sentence.
- Tics: "Tor. Look at me." / teasing that never stings.
- Never: flatters or uses titles to gain ground.

**Sample lines**

- "Tor. Look at me. When did you last eat something that wasn't a crust?"
- "You don't have to fix everyone. Some of us just like you."
- "I'm scared too. I'm still coming."

**Series slice:** Book 1 makes Sarah the reader's trusted anchor. It leaves open her family's position, the cost of standing beside Tor, and anything she hasn't told him.

### 5.6 Dorian

Dorian is the charming rival builder who needs Tor to be beatable, and gradually learns he isn't.

- **Role:** A gifted, well-connected fellow builder student; the class star until Tor arrives. (assumed)
- **Want:** Recognition as the best builder of his generation, and the patronage that comes with it.
- **Need:** To value the craft more than the applause, which Tor, oddly, can teach him.
- **Internal tension:** Admiration for Tor's work vs. resentment of what it does to his own standing. He copies Tor's ideas and hates himself for it.
- **External tension:** A sponsor (house unconfirmed; Mississippi is a strong candidate) who invested in him and expects results.

**Reaction rules**

- **Tor succeeds publicly** → gracious compliment in public; late night in the workshop, trying to replicate it.
- **Tor fails** → visibly relieved, then ashamed of the relief; may quietly help.
- **Offered a shortcut by his sponsor** → takes it the first time, refuses the second. That's his turning point.
- **Flattered** → soaks it up; flattery is the easiest lever on him.

**Voice**

- Diction: polished, witty, a little theatrical; technical terms used to impress.
- Rhythm: balanced sentences with a punchline at the end.
- Tics: "My dear Baker," backhanded compliments, dismissing things as "quaint."
- Never: admits he's confused. He says "Interesting" instead.

**Sample lines**

- "My dear Baker, it's charming. Quaint, even. Does it do anything?"
- "Interesting. Walk me through the second lattice. Slowly. For the others."
- "I took it from you. The idea. I'm telling you because I'd rather you heard it from me."

**Series slice:** Book 1 establishes the rivalry and one moment where Dorian could betray Tor and doesn't, or does and regrets it. It leaves open his sponsor's hold on him and whether he becomes a true ally.

### 5.7 Clark, son of Count Missouri

Clark is an honorable antagonist: he opposes Tor on principle, and his principles eventually force him to Tor's side.

- **Role:** Heir to the county of Missouri; top of the combat class and the school's social order. (assumed)
- **Want:** To uphold his house's name and the proper order. Commoners on scholarship upset it.
- **Need:** To separate honor from rank. The most honorable person he meets is a baker's son.
- **Internal tension:** A rigid code of fairness vs. a class prejudice that code can't justify. Every time Tor behaves well, Clark's worldview cracks a little.
- **External tension:** His father's long rivalry with Mississippi, and pressure to win the school's social contests as a proxy for that feud.

**Reaction rules**

- **Tor breaks etiquette** → corrects him coldly, in public.
- **Someone cheats against Tor** → intervenes on Tor's side, furious that he has to.
- **Loses to Tor** → formal acknowledgment, no excuses; then trains twice as hard.
- **Mississippi's people are involved** → suspects them first, sometimes wrongly. That's his blind spot.

**Voice**

- Diction: formal, military, full titles; uses "one" instead of "I" when making rulings.
- Rhythm: short declaratives; no filler words; long pauses.
- Tics: "That is not done." / addresses Tor as "Scholar Baker."
- Never: jokes at someone else's expense, or lies outright.

**Sample lines**

- "Scholar Baker. That is not done. One does not address a countess's daughter by her given name."
- "The match was fair. You won it. I have nothing further to say."
- "Step away from him. If this is settled, it is settled with me."

**Series slice:** Book 1 shifts Clark from contempt to grudging respect. It leaves open whether he'll break with his father, and what Missouri's role is in the older plot.

### 5.8 Supporting characters (all details assumed)

Each gets one internal tension, one external tension, one reliable reaction, and a voice recognizable in a single line.

#### David, fifth son of Count Mississippi

- **Role:** Has no inheritance and no expectations, so he has time to watch everything; the school's best source of gossip.
- **Internal:** Resents being irrelevant vs. enjoys the freedom it gives him.
- **External:** His father may use him as a disposable go-between to reach Tor.
- **Reaction:** Offered a secret → trades it for a better one.
- **Voice:** Wry, self-deprecating, conspiratorial asides. "Fifth son. Nobody poisons the fifth son. Very restful."

#### Count Mississippi

- **Role:** A patient political operator, and a prime suspect in the plot that predates Tor's birth.
- **Internal:** Believes his scheming keeps Noram stable vs. enjoys the game for its own sake.
- **External:** The rivalry with Missouri, and the crown's growing suspicion.
- **Reaction:** Crossed → smiles, forgives openly, and remembers forever.
- **Voice:** Genial, folksy, grandfatherly, with menace underneath. "Sit, son, sit. Nobody's in trouble. Yet."

#### Carl, combat instructor

- **Role:** A retired soldier who judges students only by what they can actually do.
- **Internal:** Contempt for nobles who play at war vs. protectiveness toward the kids who'll one day fight one.
- **External:** Noble parents who demand their sons win.
- **Reaction:** Tor gets hurt → no sympathy in class, quiet extra lessons after.
- **Voice:** Short imperatives, soldier's slang. "Again. Feet. You're small, so be fast. Again."

#### Miriam, accounting professor

- **Role:** Teaches builders the business side of magic; the first adult to tell Tor his work is worth money.
- **Internal:** Believes numbers are honest vs. knows exactly how nobles cook their books.
- **External:** Tor's constant underpricing, which she treats as a moral failing.
- **Reaction:** Tor gives work away → makes him write down its true value, in ink.
- **Voice:** Dry, precise, ledger metaphors. "Humility is lovely, Mr. Baker. It is also a line item. Yours is costing the kingdom."

#### The headmaster

- **Role:** An institutional survivor who knows more about Tor's origins than he admits.
- **Internal:** Duty to protect Tor vs. duty to the crown that placed him there.
- **External:** Noble families threatening the school's funding over Tor's rule-breaking.
- **Reaction:** Tor breaks a rule → punishes the letter of it, protects the spirit.
- **Voice:** Measured, formal, answers questions with questions. "Is that what happened, Mr. Baker? Or is that what you'd like the record to say?"

#### The builder instructor (name TBD)

- **Role:** Master of the craft and a purist about method.
- **Internal:** Pride in tradition vs. awe at Tor's impossible, untraditional work.
- **External:** Must certify Tor's projects, putting their reputation on the line.
- **Reaction:** Sees an unorthodox design → forbids it, then asks to see it again privately.
- **Voice:** Technical, grudging, precise. "That shouldn't hold. Why is it holding? Don't touch it."

#### Sophia, meditation instructor

- **Role:** Trains students' mental discipline; the first to sense something odd inside Tor's mind.
- **Internal:** Duty to report what she senses vs. fear of what would happen to Tor if she does.
- **External:** Pressure from whoever built Tor's hidden mental structures to look away.
- **Reaction:** Senses a wall in Tor's mind → stops the session, calmly, and never explains why.
- **Voice:** Slow, spacious, questions and silences. "Breathe. Where did that thought come from, Tor? Was it yours?"

| Rolph | Loyalty proven under pressure | His envy; his own purpose; his family's side |
| Patricia | She'll protect Tor when it counts | Whose interests she ultimately serves |
| Sarah | Established as Tor's safe harbor | Her family's position; anything she hasn't told him |

**Pacing rule of thumb:** per book, resolve about one thread outright, advance two or three, and open one new one. That keeps the ledger roughly the same size while the stakes rise.

## 5.12 Canon from the opening pages (added October 9, 2026)

The user pasted the book's opening pages, to be used **for character and fact mining only**, not style. Findings:

**World and school (canon)**

- The school is the **Lairdgren School**. A tower bell by the main courtyard rings the hours day and night; time is counted in bells.
- Students wear brown trousers and tunics. There's a student store and isolation chambers for building work.
- **King Richard Cordes** ordered Tor's scholarship but doesn't know him. Hook: Cordes is also the one Ancient who didn't take a color name.
- Combat class is mixed boys and girls. Tor sees nobles as prone to drinking and playing.

**How magic works (canon)**

- **Copying** from a template: about 1 in 60 people can do it, even untrained. Copy work runs the realm's infrastructure.
- **Novel builds** are rare; even builder students seldom try; professionals may need dozens of attempts.
- The builder must understand the idea very clearly. A sigil on a focus (cheap ink on unfinished wood) is a mental map that holds focus.
- Building takes a deep working trance that can last days and scrambles perception afterward. Ink drying unusually fast is a good sign.
- Daily meditation classes train lighter versions of the same states.

**Tor's first project (canon):** a field on a wood square that makes water leave cloth and fibers in an area, so laundry dries in moments. He spent days in trance and missed classes; untested at the opening.

**People**

| Character | What the text shows | Bible impact |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Tor | Poor, from the woods; sees himself as ugly and below his station, but proud of his intelligence. Rarely smiles, keeps to himself, compulsively follows tradition and respects elders, mutters to himself. Once babbled nonsense to test whether people listened. | Profile updated |
| Rolph | Roommate for two years; listens when Tor talks magic. Tor "had been told" Rolph is from a merchant family. Not frivolous. | Profile updated; merchant story treated as possible cover |
| Terlee | Tor's oldest sister: very shy, occasionally does a happy dance | New Two Rivers family member |
| Maria | First year: Tor asked her out; she screamed he was poor, ugly, above his station. He hid in his room for weeks. | New minor antagonist; root of his romantic self-doubt |
| Petra | Tall, dark-skinned, bright smile; beats Tor in combat class but kindly; he can't recall her name | New supporting character: a combat classmate who is kind to Tor |
