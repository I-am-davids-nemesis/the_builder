# Chapter plan

Our chapter plan: what each chapter must do, and the planning fields from `AGENTS.md` §8. Plot facts come from `codex/canon-notes.md`; when the original's beats are written up, they go in `outline/plot-beats.md`.

## The book's engine

The book runs on a loop, and chapter one has to show it once, in small, so the reader learns to expect it (user's direction, October 9, 2026):

1. **Tor builds something.** A practical problem catches his attention and he solves it with magic.
2. **The build causes complications.** It works too well, breaks a rule, costs him something, or draws the wrong kind of attention.
3. **The complications give him his next idea.** He answers trouble with another build, and the loop starts again at a larger scale.

Notes for drafting:

- Each turn of the loop should **raise the stakes**: from a private inconvenience to school rules, then money, nobles, and the crown.
- Because we don't narrate Tor's thoughts (`AGENTS.md` §3), **step 3 must be visible**: muttering, a sketch on scrap wood, a question to the building instructor, a sudden silence at the dinner table while the others keep talking. The reader should see the idea land before Tor explains it.
- The loop is Tor's, but **other characters supply the complications** and react to the builds. That's where their voices and tensions get room.
- Keep the routine (chapter one) as the baseline the loop disrupts. Later chapters can skip the routine or break it, because the reader already knows its shape.

### The laundry scene, beat by beat

All beats are canon (user, October 9, 2026) unless labeled.

1. **Tor washes clothes** to have wet laundry for the test. He's soaping and scrubbing; the water suds up.
2. **Davie is at the next tub, mystified.** His water won't suds up and he can't see why, because he doesn't know laundry takes soap. He's grumpy about having to do it at all, and afraid someone will notice he doesn't know how.
3. **Tor stops to help.** He teaches Davie basic laundry and lends him soap, *before* testing his field. Something small someone else needs comes ahead of the thing he has spent thirty hours on. This is the scene's main character beat for Tor.
4. **Dorgal comes in** and teases Tor about being a washerwoman.
   **Davie keeps his head down.** The mockery he was afraid of lands on Tor instead, and he stays out of it.
5. **Tovey comes in** to see how this new magic works, and **a crowd follows him**, because whatever Tovey is interested in, people want to see.
6. **The test:** the field dries the laundry in moments. (Whether it happens before or after Tovey arrives is assumed; after gives it the biggest audience.)
7. **Everyone wants one.** Tovey wants it for his mother. The crowd all want copies.
8. **Trice and Sara arrive**, after Tovey.
9. **Trice gets Sara to estimate what it's worth.** Value depends on what the magic is made on and how long it lasts. Tor's is cheap wood and paint, so it's worth far less than one on good material, but still **30 gold**, a significant sum.
10. **Tor can't give it to anyone.** He has to turn the prototype in for testing. He promises nothing, but **assumes he'll be making copies for all of them.**
11. **Rolph comes by** shortly after Trice and Sara, and gets Tor out of a social situation he isn't equipped to handle.

**Staging notes** (invented):

- **Davie's ignorance has to be visible, not explained.** He's working hard and getting nowhere: scrubbing, wringing, holding a shirt up to the light, glancing at Tor's suds and then his own flat gray water. The reader should work out what's missing a beat before Tor says it.
- **Tor's help comes the way his reaction rules say:** yes before Davie finishes asking sideways; no mockery; a practical lesson, possibly with a baking or home comparison. He shouldn't make it a kindness Davie has to thank him for, because that's what lets Davie accept it.
- **The scene keeps getting bigger.** One noble who needs help, then a rival who mocks, then Tovey and his crowd, then Trice putting a price on it. A private test of a laundry trick turns into a public event with a market value, which is the book's engine in small.
- **Dorgal and Tovey are a voice contrast in one scene.** Dorgal jokes at Tor's expense; Tovey never does (`tovey.md`, "Never").
- **Davie keeps his head down** while Dorgal teases Tor. He was just helped, and doesn't speak up for the person who helped him. It shouldn't be played as villainy; it's fear, and the reader should see it cost him something (a glance away, a shirt folded too carefully).
- **The perception gap, staged.** Tor is afraid of Tovey and treats him as a threat that could strike out at any slight: extra courtesy, careful words, keeping his distance. Tovey is not disdainful and not hostile; he's curious and wants a gift for his mother. Since we don't narrate Tor's thoughts, the gap shows as a mismatch between Tor's behavior and what Tovey actually says and does. The reader sees both; Tor sees only his fear. (See `codex/characters/tor.md`, the perception gap.)
- **The price is a character beat for Trice and Sara.** Trice asks; Sara answers. Trice knows the question that matters and who in the room can answer it. Sara's answer, broken down by material and how long it lasts, shows knowledge the reader didn't expect from her.
- **30 gold lands differently on each person.** To Tovey and the crowd it's a purchase. To Tor, a baker's son, it's probably more than his family sees in a long time (assumed), and he made it out of scrap. His reaction (disbelief, an apology, an urge to explain that it's only paint) is the scene's comic and painful peak.
- **Turning in the prototype is the hook.** Demand without supply: everyone wants one, he has one, and it isn't his to give.
- **The unspoken promise has to be visible.** Tor never says he'll make copies; he assumes it. With no narrated thoughts, the reader sees it from outside: he counts heads under his breath, asks someone how many students board at the school, looks at the wood square as a quantity, or mutters a figure of days. Nobody in the room hears a promise. The reader understands he has made one to himself. This is the engine's step 3, and the start of chapter two's trouble (how long would that take, and who said he had to?).
- **Another case of the perception gap.** The crowd want to buy; Tor hears an obligation. Nobody asked him for free copies for everyone; he sets himself that task. (invented framing of canon)
- **Rolph's rescue ends the scene.** He's the friend who knows how to do what Tor can't: talk his way out of a room full of nobles. His arrival should feel like air coming back in. Use his reaction rules (loud, physical, joking) and let him leave with Tor before anyone pins Tor down.
- **Sara's appraisal shows her background before anyone states it.** She prices the field as someone who has handled the best magic the continent has to offer and grew up around the capital. The reader can infer a merchant house from how she talks about cost and material. (invented staging of canon)

**Why the laundry scene works** (craft notes; invented):

- **It introduces magic through need.** The reader sees what the field is for in the hands of someone who can't manage without it, before anyone explains how building works.
- **It shows the class order through an inversion.** The noble can't do laundry and the baker's son can, which sets up the culture clash without a word of exposition.
- **Davie and Tor are both outsiders, in opposite ways.** Davie hides that he doesn't know something; Tor hides that he knows a great deal. Both fear being laughed at.
- **Reaction rules collide.** Davie asks for help sideways and won't admit ignorance; Tor says yes before the sentence ends and calls the field "not really anything". Neither will say what's actually happening, and the scene's humor and tension come from that.
- **It shows who Tor is before it shows what he can do.** He helps with the soap before he tests the magic, so the reader meets his character first.
- **The laundry is a setting the reader can see:** wet wool, cold water, a bar of soap and the suds it makes (or doesn't), a tub, steam, a painted square of cheap wood set down among the dripping shirts, and then a room too full of people in brown tunics.

### The room, beat by beat

All beats are canon (user, October 9, 2026) unless labeled.

1. **Rolph "rescues" Tor** from the laundry and brings him back to their room. People follow along, hoping for free things; the two of them shut the door on them.
2. **The lock.** The room has a magic lock: it identifies the room's occupants and magically lifts a simple door bar on the inside. Tor **copied** it from a chest of Rolph's, a magic lockbox **full of gold** that sits in the room.
3. **Rolph's interest in Tor's magic.** He is very interested in Tor and his work, in what looks like innocent envy: he once spent hours staring at the room lock.
4. **Rolph wants a copy for his mother** too.
4a. **Rolph invites Tor home** to visit his family **in the capital** over the next school break. Tor hesitates because he doesn't want to be a burden on his friend's family during their holiday. He has no idea the family is interested in him. (canon, user)
5. **Rolph explains prestige.** Among nobility, having new magic before anyone else is a mark of significant prestige.
6. **The idea.** Rolph says it would be nice if the field worked on fruit and vegetables, since crop losses are a frequent topic at home.
7. **Tor goes off into a fugue** working out exactly how that would work and what he'd change to turn a clothes drying field into a food drying field. He goes to his desk and starts sketching. Rolph watches, patiently amused.
8. **Tor remembers he's in the middle of a conversation** and apologizes.
9. **The rest of the day:** Tor eats and recovers from the long trance, now and then making notes on the new idea. Rolph reads (studying, presumably) and keeps him company. It's a **normal rest day**, so he misses no further classes. (decided by the user; not confirmed against the original)
10. **That night** Tor falls asleep dreaming about the new project, wakes, and furiously scribbles notes that make perfect sense to him and are indecipherable to anyone else.
11. **Next morning** he gets an early start, to show the original field to his professor for testing. (This opens the last third.)

**Staging notes** (invented):

- **The closed door is a scene change in pressure.** The laundry was public and full of rank; the room is private, and the only place Tor isn't performing. The crowd outside, hoping for free things, is the laundry's demand following him home. Let it be heard (voices, a knock, the bar dropping) before it's shut out.
- **The lock does two jobs.** It shows the reader what copying is (Tor took an existing magic and reproduced it) next to the novel build of the drying field. And it puts Rolph's chest in view: a magic lockbox full of gold, in the room of a boy from a "merchant family." The narration shouldn't comment on it. Tor doesn't find it strange, so nobody in the scene says it's strange; the reader can.
- **Rolph's hints stack, and none is an admission.** The lockbox of gold; a mother who'd want new magic as a mark of status; "among nobility," said like someone who knows from inside; crop losses as dinner-table talk at home, which sounds like land and tenants more than trade. Each has an innocent explanation. Together they make a pattern the reader can see and Tor can't. (Track in `codex/reader-knowledge.md`.)
- **Rolph's envy should read as innocent on the surface.** Hours staring at a door lock is funny and a little sad: he wants to understand what Tor does and can't. The profile's darker version (envy he'd never admit) stays underneath. (`rolph.md`)
- **The invitation is the book's first open door to Rolph's family.** Tor's only worry is being a burden, which is the perception gap again: he thinks he'd be an imposition on a family that has been asking about him. Rolph keeps his mother's eager questions to himself. "You could lose a regiment in it" is true and tells Tor nothing. (staging invented)
- **The prestige line explains the laundry crowd after the fact.** The reader now understands why Tovey's interest drew everyone, and why Tovey and Rolph both think of their mothers: new magic is a social currency. Tor hears it as information, not as pressure, which it is.
- **The fugue is the engine visible.** We don't narrate the idea. We show Tor stop mid-sentence, go to the desk, the scratch of the pen, Rolph's amused patience, the bell passing, and then Tor surfacing to apologize. The apology is his reaction rule (`tor.md`); Rolph's patience is the friendship.
- **The recovery is the trance's cost, shown in the body.** Hunger, stiffness, eating more than he means to, falling asleep early. Rolph's quiet reading beside him is the first calm the chapter gives the reader.
- **The night notes pay off later.** Make them concrete on the page: cramped lines, sigil fragments, arrows, words run together. Someone (Rolph in the morning, or the professor later) should try to read them and fail.

### The next day, beat by beat

Beats canon (user, October 9, 2026) unless labeled.

1. **An early start.** Tor arrives early at his **building instructor's office** (he seems to be a special projects instructor) and shows him the new device. The instructor pulls him straight to the washing house to test it.
   **The dunking**, played for comedy. The instructor takes Tor to the washing area and **dunks him in a washing tub**, then uses the field to show it dries Tor's clothes without harming Tor himself. **He states his reason out loud:** a field like this has to be shown safe for use around people. **What he doesn't say, and the reader never hears,** is that he's already reasonably sure it is: Tor told him he'd tested it, and students are never as careful around new magic as they should be, so it has surely been used near people already.
   **He never comments on the quality of the work.** The instructors assume it's top-notch and never tell Tor so.
   **He keeps the device** and sends Tor off to classes, reminding him to explain his absence the week before as **a building exercise he was doing for the instructor's class**.
2. **Meditation with Doris.** She doesn't react much. She barely talks at all; each day she brings in a new item for the class to focus on. Tor thinks he should be excused for his absence, since he spent the time meditating while he built the field.
2a. **Magic theory.** The theory instructor is positively gleeful to see Tor, and tells him he's already spoken with Tor's building instructor.
3. **The cafeteria, midday.** Tor meets Rolph for lunch. When they're nearly done, Kolb, head of combat, which takes up the afternoons, walks over and **quietly** promises retribution, in the form of extra intensive workouts, for daring to miss his class.
4. **Rolph tries to support Tor**, but Tor can hardly hear him; he's terrified of what Kolb is going to do. Nothing will stop Kolb from taking his "revenge."
5. **Tor leaves the cafeteria and heads out to combat class, afraid.** **The chapter ends here.** Combat class opens chapter 2.

**Staging notes** (invented):

- **This is the routine chapter one promised.** The last third is the school morning at full speed: the bells, the walk across the courtyard, one class after another, then the cafeteria. Keep each class to a short beat so the day feels like a machine Tor has just been fed back into. Each instructor gets one recognizable line, not a scene.
- **The dunking is comedy.** Play it for the laugh: the brisk walk to the washing area with Tor not knowing why, the splash, Tor surfacing mid-sentence, the instructor already reaching for the field, the steam, Tor standing bone-dry and bewildered with his hair still sticking up. Physical comedy works without anyone's thoughts, which suits our mode.
- **The stated reason, and the unstated one.** The instructor says it plainly: a field like this has to be shown safe for use around people. That's the reason the reader gets. What stays hidden is that he already expected it to be safe, so the dunking was less of a gamble than it looks. On the page it should look rough, even reckless. An **unnamed student bystander** is shocked and appalled that a professor would do that to a student (suggested by the user); their horror sets up the laugh and pushes the reader toward "reckless." Don't narrate the instructor's thoughts.
- **Tor takes it in stride.** He thinks it's a fair test, maybe even a sensible one, and apologizes for dripping. His calm against the bystander's horror is part of the joke.
- **No praise, and nobody notices.** The instructor never says the work is good. To the instructors it's obviously top-notch and not worth saying; Tor hears the silence as a pass at best. Keep it ambiguous whether that's the school's culture or Tor's inability to hear praise (see the perception gap in `tor.md`). Don't resolve it.
- **The scene answers a safety question nobody asked.** Is magic that pulls water out of cloth safe near a body made of water? The reader can see the stakes after the fact, once Tor is fine.
- **Vitality fields, maybe.** The chapter may mention, separately from the dunking, that telling living from non-living tissue is one of the fundamentals of a builder's education: every living thing has a vitality field, stronger and more complex the more complex the creature, and human fields naturally resist simple fields. (canon world fact; including it in chapter one is optional, per the user) A natural home is the **magic theory class** later that morning, as one line of a lecture Tor half-hears while still damp. Never connect it to the dunking on the page. A reader who puts the two together has worked out more about the instructor than anyone says; one who doesn't has lost nothing. (placement invented)
- **It's the laundry scene's mirror.** Yesterday Tor stopped to help someone with wet clothes; today someone with power soaks his. Same tubs, same field, reversed roles.
- **Expulsion is Tor's worry and nobody else's.** Advanced building is so rare that one such builder per cohort is a triumph for the school, and everyone knows it without being told. So when Tor brings up expulsion (to the instructor, to Rolph, to Trice), people take it as a joke, a poor one, and move on, or laugh politely. Nobody explains why it isn't a risk, because to them it's too obvious to say. The reader should piece it together from the reactions: the instructor's blank look, Rolph's grin, a change of subject. It's the perception gap again (`tor.md`), played for comedy. Use it two or three times in the chapter at most; the joke is that it keeps not landing.
- **The handover is the first build leaving his hands.** Whatever the building instructor says or doesn't, the field is no longer Tor's. The copies he silently promised and the food field in his notes both now wait on someone else's verdict.
- **Kolb's threat is quiet, and that's what makes it frightening.** He doesn't make a scene in the cafeteria; the rest of the room can carry on eating. The quieter he is, the more the reader believes him. (`kolb.md`)
- **The ending turns the engine's cost into a cliffhanger.** The chapter's triumphs (the test, the price, the new idea) were all about building; the bill arrives from the one part of the school that doesn't care about building. Tor's genius has no answer to Kolb. End on the walk out, not the confrontation: Tor leaving the cafeteria, the bell, the path toward the combat grounds, his fear in his body (a dry mouth, a too-careful pace, Rolph's hand on his shoulder dropping away). Combat itself waits for chapter 2.
- **Rolph's limits show.** He rescued Tor at the laundry; here he tries and can't. It's the first time the reader sees something Rolph can't fix, which matters for his envy and his want to protect Tor (`rolph.md`).
- **Tor's reaction should fit his rules:** threatened himself → quiet and polite, extra courtesy (`tor.md`). He shouldn't argue, and probably thinks he deserves it. The fear is real even though he wasn't afraid of the dunking: he's calm about magic and terrified of people.
- **Is it revenge?** The quotation marks leave room: Kolb's "revenge" may also be training he thinks a small, trance-prone builder needs. From outside, the two look the same, which suits a mode without Tor's thoughts. (Decide later; see `kolb.md`.)

### 3. The routine and its pace

- Mark time in **bells**. Establish the day's fixed points: rising, morning classes (building/special projects, meditation with Doris, magic theory), meals in the cafeteria, **afternoon combat with Kolb** (canon, user), the store, lights out, so the reader can feel when Tor is late, absent, or skipping. (classes and afternoon combat canon; the rest of the order assumed)
- The routine has a rhythm: **short, regular beats** for the school day, set against the **long, timeless stretch** of the trance. The contrast between the two is the chapter's pacing.
- Show the routine **once, briskly**, as Tor rejoins it. The middle third takes him out of it (a day of recovery in his room), so the routine's clearest showing is the **last third**: the early start, the bells, the building instructor, the morning classes, and the cafeteria, with afternoon combat waiting for chapter 2. (canon, user)
- Use the routine to place characters: who sits where at meals, who partners whom in combat, who is in building class with Tor. Rank shows through the routine. (`codex/places.md`: the school pressures rules and rank.)

## Chapter 2

Beats are from the user's plot summary of the original's chapter two (October 9, 2026): plot points only, not the original's tone or wording (`AGENTS.md` §2).

### What the chapter must do

1. **Pay chapter one's bill.** Kolb's "revenge" arrives and turns out to be a build assignment with a deadline: a shield in two weeks.
2. **Turn the engine twice, faster.** The drying field leaves Tor's hands and becomes **money**: a dangerous merchant, then Sara's house, then Rolph's rush order. The stakes rise from school rules (chapter one) to money and merchant houses.
3. **Show Tor's friends are more than they seem.** Trice and Sara fight like professionals, wear black, and belong to a "special school." Rolph's mother has a birthday the same week as the queen's.
4. **End on a cliffhanger:** the strange numbness, Trice down in the dark courtyard, Tor swinging a sack of copper at a huge attacker who turns out to be Tovey.

### Tone: light and funny

The user wants the book **as light and funny as possible** (October 9, 2026). The original's chapter two is heavy with Tor's dread; ours keeps the dread visible but plays it for comedy. The comedy comes from the gap between how Tor reads a situation and what's actually happening (`codex/characters/tor.md`, the perception gap), and from physical comedy, which suits modes without narrated thought.

- **The joke is never on Tor's intelligence.** He's brilliant and wrong about people. The reader laughs at the mismatch, and likes him for it.
- **Danger stays real, but people around Tor treat it as routine.** Kolb's casual lethality, the knife at the throat, the powder: everyone else thinks this is a normal afternoon. Tor's courtesy under threat is funny because it's so out of proportion with how calm the others are.
- **Escalating numbers are a running joke.** Sara priced the field at 30 gold in chapter one. The merchant offers 2. Sara offers 12, or 20 on metal. Rolph's order works out to 11 each. Tor thinks every offer is wildly generous. The reader remembers 30.
- **The ending is the one place the chapter turns serious**, and it still has a sack of copper squares in it.

### Combat class, beat by beat

All beats canon (user, October 9, 2026) unless labeled.

1. **Kolb at the pell.** Kolb is in the practice yard hitting a **pell** (a great log buried halfway in the ground) so hard everything seems to shake. He's surprisingly relaxed with Tor.
2. **The demonstration.** Kolb tells Tor a single blow like that could kill someone without armor or a magical shield field device. Then he draws a **cutter**, not a small tool but a **four-foot blade**, and slices cleanly through the pell.
3. **The assignment.** Two weeks to build a new shield that withstands **both** kinds of attack (the blow and the cutter) for **at least twenty minutes**.
4. **The warm-up.** Kolb sends Tor to run to the nearest town and back, about **ten miles**. Tor doesn't mind: Kolb always tells him his best way to survive a fight is to run away.
5. **The run.** Tor is low, and thinks about leaving school, then loses himself in the shield problem and runs an extra half hour out before turning back, faster.
6. **Back at the yard:** students in the usual brown, and **two in black**, which marks students of the **special school**. They turn out to be **Trice and Sara**.
7. **Sparring, unarmed.** Kolb assigns them to spar with Tor. Trice first: she goes straight for a kick to the groin. Tor dodges, but he can't run, because he's been assigned to spar. She's skilled, especially at kicks, but tries a showy **spinning wheel kick**; Tor gets inside it and takes her to the ground.
8. **The knife.** Tor is winning the grappling when there is suddenly a **knife at his throat**. He freezes for a second, then almost instantly throws out a **field that pushes everything away from his body**.
9. **Sara mistakes it for a shield device** and tells Trice to back off; the knife won't help.
10. **The powder.** Trice gets up and throws a **white powder** at him. It doesn't get through the field, but it blinds him, and he realizes he **can't breathe**. He drops the field, noting that it has to let air in but keep dust out.
11. **The complaint.** Sara and Trice complain that Tor has a shield. Kolb laughs it off: Tor doesn't have a shield; he's working on the project Kolb set him, and now that Kolb knows it will likely work, he'll have to come at it even harder in two weeks.
12. **The rest of the afternoon:** Sara and Trice take turns attacking Tor.
13. **Evening:** Tor goes back to his room to finish the food drying design and start thinking about the shield.

**Staging notes** (invented):

- **The open is a fake-out.** Chapter one ended on dread. Chapter two opens on Kolb being pleasant, which is worse. Let him be chatty, even friendly, while explaining how easily he could kill a person. The bigger the pell's shudder, the calmer his voice.
- **The cutter is description work.** It's magic: an infinitely thin blade that passes through any physical thing without resistance (canon, user). Show it, don't explain it: the blade vanishing when it turns edge-on, the swing that doesn't slow, no sound, the mirror-smooth cut face, the top half sliding off a moment later. Give the reader the object Tor will have to stop.
- **The twenty minutes is the joke's punchline.** Kolb says it last, like an afterthought. Tor's reply should be courteous and appalled (`tor.md`: threatened → extra courtesy).
- **Ten miles is home ground.** Kolb sends it as punishment; Tor treats it as the one part of combat he's good at. Kolb's own advice (run away) said back to him, or about to be, is the joke. Someone, Kolb or a classmate, should notice he's not suffering.
- **The run is where the despair goes.** We don't narrate Tor's thoughts, so the low point shows outside: he slows at a milestone on the road, looks back toward the school, then starts muttering about fields and doesn't notice the town go by. The extra half hour is seen from the turnaround: he's well past where he should have turned. He comes back faster, which says the mood broke.
- **The black uniforms are the reader's first clue** that Trice and Sara aren't what chapter one suggested. The special school is a spy school with assassination training (canon, user), and **none of that is revealed**: no name for it, no explanation, nobody's thoughts about it. Fighting dirty in "unarmed" sparring is as far as the hint goes. In chapter one Sara priced magic; here she fights. Nobody explains the special school. Tor recognizes them late, which is a small comedy of its own (he's busy being afraid of Kolb).
- **Tor's fighting has to read as surprisingly competent.** He's small and fast; he gets inside the wheel kick and puts Trice down. The reader should see he's better than he thinks, and so should Trice. (Perception gap again: Tor thinks he's losing the whole time.)
- **The knife is the turn.** One beat of stillness, then the field. It looks like a **noble's hereditary field**, and Tor is controlling it, which almost nobody has ever shown (canon, user). Nobody says that in this chapter. Sara reads it as a device; Kolb knows better and laughs. Trice may look at Tor a beat too long. That's all the reader gets. Narrate the field from outside: dust and grit sliding away from him in a ring, Trice's knife hand pushed back, Sara's reaction. Don't explain how he did it; nobody on the page understands it yet either.
- **The powder is physical comedy.** A white cloud hanging around him in the shape of the field, Tor inside it, blind, then visibly not breathing, then the field dropping and the powder landing on him all at once. He comes out white and coughing, and possibly apologizing.
- **The idea lands visibly.** Coughing, he mutters what's wrong with the field (air in, dust out). That's the engine's step 3, out loud.
- **Kolb's laugh is the scene's release.** He's delighted, which means the test just got harder. Trice and Sara learn that what beat them wasn't a device, which the reader should notice more than Tor does. Kolb's delight should carry a hint that he's seen something rarer than a good student project.
- **The afternoon of being attacked by turns** is a montage: short, rhythmic, funny. Trice tries something new each time; Sara is plainer and harder to read; Tor apologizes when he hits either of them.

### The merchant, beat by beat

All beats canon (user, October 9, 2026) unless labeled.

1. **Pulled from class.** Next day, Tor's building instructor pulls him out of building class and takes him to his office, where a **dangerous-seeming merchant** is waiting: **Douglas Sorvey** (same family name as Dorgal). (spelling assumed; the user wrote "Sorve")
2. **The offer:** **two gold** for each clothes drying device Tor can produce.
3. **Tor hides behind Sara**, saying he's already agreed to have Sara represent him. (He hasn't yet; she makes it true that evening.)
4. **Sorvey is agreeable**, and offers to pay for any new projects Tor comes up with.
5. **Tor can feel how dangerous the man is** and is reluctant to deal with him.
6. **Sorvey leaves his card** and takes his leave.
7. **The instructor is clearly unhappy** that the merchant he brought in was turned down. He suggests it isn't a bad idea to **pay off the person doing the testing**. Tor thinks this is a natural, sensible idea, not a bribe solicitation.

**Staging notes** (invented):

- **The danger has to be visible without narrating Tor's senses.** Sorvey is polite and still; Tor goes very polite and very careful (`tor.md`: threatened → courtesy). Specifics: Sorvey never blinks first, his hands are a fighter's under a merchant's rings, he's taken the instructor's chair. The reader can sense what Tor senses from the way Tor stands.
- **Two gold is a lowball the reader can catch.** The reader heard Sara say 30. Tor, being Tor, thinks two gold is generous and refuses anyway. That's the scene's comic core: he says no to a bad deal for the wrong reason.
- **"Sara represents me" is half a fib** (user). Half of it is Tor's honest reading of the laundry: Sara priced the field, Trice steered the talk, and he came away half-believing something had been settled. The other half is a shield he grabs because Sorvey frightens him. Play both: he says it a little too fast, then visibly tries to work out whether it's true. It becomes true by nightfall, which is the joke.
- **The Sorvey name lands on the reader, never on Tor.** Douglas is **Dorgal's father** (canon, user). Everyone else knows it, so nobody says it; Tor doesn't connect them. The reader should catch the name and Tor's blankness. Douglas doesn't mention his son. (The reader's confirmation can come later, from someone who assumes Tor already knows.)
- **The instructor's hint is the funniest line in the scene, said dryly and heard innocently.** He suggests paying the tester; Tor agrees that's only fair, since testing is work. Neither says who the tester is. Whether Tor actually offers money later is open (a callback opportunity).
- **The instructor's displeasure is a new note for him.** In chapter one his rough methods hid sound reasons. Here his motive looks plainly mercenary, and that may be the truth, or not. Keep it ambiguous (`builder-instructor.md`).

### The visit, beat by beat

All beats canon (user, October 9, 2026) unless labeled.

1. **Trice and Sara come to Tor's room** while he's working on the shield, dressed up for the visit. Tor doesn't notice, believing they can't possibly be interested in him, least of all after beating him up yesterday.
2. **They argue against the Sorvey house** and for the **Debri** merchant house, Sara's family.
3. **Sara's offer:** **12 gold** per drying device, **20** if they can get it on a **metal plate**. Tor thinks it's a very good idea.
4. **Tor worries he won't live to deliver**: he expects Kolb to kill him in two weeks when the shield is tested.
5. **Trice's joke:** they have every confidence he'll survive; they overheard Kolb telling the head of their department he can do it, or he wouldn't have set it. The school rarely kills students; it's bad for enrollment. (The joke is canon; the original's wording stays out of our drafts. Write our own version.)
6. **Tor takes it as Kolb keeping him focused.** The threat still feels real to him.
7. **Sara becomes his sales agent**, for a **10%** cut. Whether Tor thinks he gets the 10% or Sara does is unclear. (canon ambiguity; see staging)
8. **No card.** Sara has nowhere to carry a calling card in that outfit, so she tells him Rolph or Tovey knows where to find them.
9. **After they leave**, Tor is flabbergasted at the idea of asking a count to play messenger for him; he takes it as a cruel joke.

**Staging notes** (invented):

- **Content guardrail (`AGENTS.md` §9).** The source has the girls dressed to seduce. Ours: they're dressed for an evening out, not for a dormitory. The comedy is entirely Tor's: he notices nothing, or notices something irrelevant (that Sara's sleeves are too thin for the cold corridor, that there's nowhere to keep a card). No description dwells on bodies; Tor's obliviousness is the whole joke.
- **The reader gets the context; Tor doesn't.** Rolph's grin, a passing student's double take in the corridor, or Trice's visible annoyance that the effort was wasted can tell the reader the girls came prepared to charm, without anything sexual on the page.
- **Trice and Sara work as a pair, in their own voices.** Trice argues the politics (who Sorvey is, who benefits, `patricia.md`); Sara names the numbers and makes it personal (`sara.md`). Run the cover-the-names test here.
- **The enrollment joke is Trice's**, delivered dry. Tor hears reassurance about an assassination policy. The reader hears a joke. Tor's response should be sincere and grateful.
- **The 10% stays ambiguous on purpose** (user): a joke now, a source of tension later. Tor agrees warmly to a number without being sure which way it runs, and nobody on the page settles it. Sara doesn't correct him. Keep the dialogue genuinely ambiguous, so neither reading can later be called a misreading of the text.
- **"Ask Rolph or Tovey" is a perception-gap bomb.** Tor treats asking Tovey (a count) to fetch two girls for him as an obvious cruel joke. Neither girl thinks it's a joke. The reader should also notice they named **Rolph** in the same breath as a count, which Tor doesn't.
- **Sara's metal plate is the next idea arriving from outside.** She says "20 if it's on metal," and Tor's eyes go to the materials. That's the engine's step 3 coming from another character.

### Rolph's order, beat by beat

All beats canon (user, October 9, 2026) unless labeled.

1. **Rolph comes back late from the pub** with news: people are very interested in the drying device.
2. **He wants his mother to get the first ten** so she can hand them out to her friends, which would help sales enormously.
3. **Tor plays grumpy**, claiming (truthfully) he doesn't have the materials. He'll obviously do it.
4. **Rolph offers 55 gold**, half up front on the assumption that ten will cost 110.
5. **The rush:** his mother's birthday is in a week. Tor: "Like the queen's birthday? Do we get that day off?" Rolph chuckles: yes, just like the queen. He needs them fast to catch the **express mail**, three days to the capital.
6. **Tor decides to do it tonight.** He has money now, so he buys **a hundred copper squares** (five inches on a side) and **acid** for etching at the school store.

**Staging notes** (invented):

- **The queen line is the chapter's biggest piece of dramatic irony.** Rolph's mother is the queen (canon, user). Tor asks the question as a joke; Rolph answers it truthfully, as if joking back. The narration must not wink. This is the strongest hint yet, and it should go past as fast as it can. In rotating third, Rolph's viewpoint must stay on action and word choice (the secret-keeper rule, `AGENTS.md` §3).
- **Tor's grumpiness is a performance, and a new note for him.** He's teasing Rolph, which he only does with Rolph. It's the clearest sign of their friendship in the chapter, and the reader sees him agree before he says he will (he's already counting squares).
- **Rolph's pub mood helps.** He's loud, a little merry, delighted with his own salesmanship. His enthusiasm sets up the scale (ten, then a hundred) that Tor takes on alone.
- **The 110 gold is another escalating number.** Tor, who was offered 2 gold that morning, is handed 55 tonight.
- **The day names.** The source says "next Friday"; our calendar so far uses numbered days ("fourth day," "fifth day," "rest day"; `canon-notes.md`). Use the numbered form unless the user prefers otherwise.

### The night build, beat by beat

All beats canon (user, October 9, 2026) unless labeled.

1. **The method.** Tor etches the design on the copper with acid. **The sigil is a guide**, not what makes the magic work: he imbues the object with the field's pattern; the sigil keeps his place and shows what the object is for.
2. **All hundred squares** get the design. Some don't come out perfectly; he decides to **give those away**: to Davie, the laundry, other scholarship students.
3. **He splits them**: half stay in his room, half go to a school **workroom**.
4. **Batches of ten**, a couple of hours each, harder and harder to concentrate on: copy work is boring, and it's late.

**Staging notes** (invented):

- **Copying is the opposite of chapter one's trance.** The trance was timeless; copying is counted. Mark it in bells and batches: ten done, the bell, ten more. The boredom is visible: Tor talking to the squares, losing count, a yawn that interrupts a sigil.
- **The acid and copper are description work**: the green bloom on the metal, the smell, the hiss, stained fingertips, the bright lines where the design bites in.
- **The giveaways are the copies he promised himself in chapter one**, quietly kept. Davie first. It's his reaction rule in action, and it's unremarked.
- **The workroom setting** should be empty and cold at that hour, so the walk back through the courtyard is late and dark.

### The courtyard, beat by beat

**Structure** (user, October 9, 2026): we don't yet know what set off Trice's field or why she and Tovey are out so late. The likeliest answer is a **flashback in chapter three** that shows how the fight began. Chapter two **catches the fight mid-action, from Trice's viewpoint, with no cause**: she doesn't know why Tovey is like this, and she never learns that she caused it. In the camera mode, the same section opens on the two figures mid-fight with no lead-in.

All beats canon (user, October 9, 2026) unless labeled.

1. **The numbness.** Walking back with the finished squares in a sack, Tor's leg goes numb, as if he'd sat on it during the build. The numbness spreads. He's afraid he's done something to himself. After a moment of panic he keeps walking, hoping to walk it off.
2. **A fight in the courtyard.** One figure is **huge** (too dark to see who); the other is smaller and faster (still bigger than Tor) and keeps pointing a **device** at the big one, making him stumble back. The smaller figure has clearly been hit several times; they're staggering as badly as Tor.
3. **Tor is about to run**, then hears the smaller figure cry out: a girl. He recognizes **Trice's** voice.
4. **He has to help**, or at least get between them so she can run, even if it costs him a beating. All he can think, staggering toward them, is that she'd better have a good reason to be out fighting this late.
5. **The big man knocks Trice down** with a punch, sparks flying around him as he advances on her.
6. **Tor swings the sack of copper squares** at him to distract him, hitting him several times before the man notices and turns.
7. **The numbness is stronger when the man looks at him.**
8. **It's Tovey**, Count Thompson. Tovey stops looking at Tor, and attacks. **The chapter ends.** (end point assumed: the summary stops here)

**Staging notes** (invented):

- **The numbness must be shown, not explained.** A leg that doesn't land where he puts it, fingers that won't close on the sack's neck, Tor talking to his own foot. The comedy (blaming himself for sitting wrong) should tip into fear as it spreads.
- **"She'd better have a good reason" is the one funny line in the scene**, and it's Tor's: he mutters it while lurching toward danger. It's his friend-threatened reaction rule (`tor.md`) arriving in its least heroic form.
- **The sack of copper is the chapter's thread tied off as a weapon.** Rolph's order, Sara's metal, the night's work: all of it swung at a giant's back. The sound of it should be specific (a flat clank, squares sliding inside).
- **The sparks and the device** are unexplained magic seen from outside. Don't label them.
- **Trice's viewpoint is honest.** She can hold the viewpoint without hiding anything from the reader, because she doesn't know about her field. What she sees: Tovey not himself, the numbness she's fighting through, her device, how badly this is going. She can't say why it started (the cause is held for chapter three), so her section begins in motion: mid-strike, mid-stumble.
- **What's really happening** (canon, user; **not on the page**): the numbness is Tovey's hereditary field, which comes out when a high noble is enraged. Tovey is in a blind rage because Trice's own hidden field, which drives nobles to rage and which she doesn't know she has, triggered. Drafts must be consistent with this without stating it: Tovey's face should look wrong (blank, not angry in his usual way), and his numbing field should be strongest at whoever he's looking at.
- **The reveal reverses the perception gap.** All book, Tor fears Tovey without cause. Here, for the first time, Tovey attacks him. The reader knows (from chapter one and `tovey.md`) that this isn't who Tovey is, so the reveal should land as wrong, not as confirmation. The numbness getting stronger when Tovey looks at Tor is the clue.
- **End on the attack beginning**, not on the blow.
