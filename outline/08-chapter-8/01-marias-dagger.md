# Marias Dagger

- **Summary:** story points from the original's version of this scene (canon unless marked). Where our codex has already changed a detail, our version wins and is noted.
  1. **The name lands.** Sara asks "who?", and Tor can't tell if she's scared or angry; she's already understood "Doretta" is a type, not a woman's name. Trice sits up and asks, low and dangerous, whether it was Maria. Tor is reminded these pampered girls could still beat him in a fight.
  2. **Rolph takes the floor.** He stands and moves to the far side of the fire to face both girls, and avoids Tor's eye. Tor wants this never told; Rolph tells it anyway.
  3. **The roommate.** Rolph sets it up as a fireside tale: first year, the heir to the throne given a roommate by the dean's choice, a baker's son from nowhere, a quarter his size and far cleverer. Rolph expected a room and servants of his own and was put out, then liked Tor within a day. Tor's thick accent was gone in days, perhaps hours.
  4. **The asking.** At Rolph's urging, Tor asked a girl from one of his classes to the spring student party (ours: the school dance). Rolph didn't take her for peerage and assumed the worst she'd do was plead a prior engagement or offer a friend. Tor wasn't after anything more than a dance; Rolph calls that sweet.
  5. **The flowers.** Rolph coached the words himself. Tor approached her after dinner with an arrangement he'd spent the afternoon on, meticulous the way he is with everything; Rolph says the queen would have accepted it gladly and Tor could earn a living arranging flowers in the capital.
  6. **Rolph's pause.** He drinks and goes quiet a long time, looking away, pensive and a little sad. Then: the invitation was perfect, inside every rule, leaving her a hundred graceful ways out. Still angry after nearly two years: she could simply have told him no.
  7. **Tor flinches.** Sara winces. Trice touches Tor's arm and he jumps away, then feels bad about it. Sara keeps her eyes on Rolph and pointedly never looks at Tor. Tor dreads the details; these two are nearly the only girls outside his family who don't seem to want to flee him.
  8. **What Maria did.** She screamed at him for minutes in front of everyone: worthless, dirty, diseased, beneath her (ours: poor, ugly and above his station, in the cafeteria before most of the school; see `codex/characters/supporting/maria.md`). Older girls finally dragged her out.
  9. **What Tor never knew.** Maria then ran a rumor campaign making Tor the villain of everything. Tor didn't hear it because he didn't leave the room for nearly three weeks; the school nearly sent him home, and Rolph asked his parents to intervene, which they did on his word alone. Staff feared Tor would harm himself if he heard the rumors. Rolph won't repeat them even now. Tor is surprised there was more; he only remembers the glares.
  10. **Kolb.** Kolb started training Tor to fight because of this, chose him personally, and no one put him up to it. That's why Tor will never be let off. (Rolph's aside to Tor; Rolph reveals it in this scene, user, November 1, 2026; see `codex/characters/supporting/kolb.md`.)
  11. **Maria's exit.** Maria was eventually recalled home; Rolph assumed her family had found out and sent her away in shame.
  12. **Trice connects it.** That's why Tor looked so upset at the dinner; she's amazed Maria showed her face. Sara doesn't place her (a palace servant?). Trice: Maria married Count Ward. She calls Maria a vile thing and compares her unfavorably with Ursula. Maria would never have dared if she'd known who Tor would become; that's why there are rules. What if Tor decided to make war on her family, or on Ward?
  13. **Tor's war.** Trice hugs him awkwardly from the side; he enjoys it and thinks she's being silly. Comic run: at best he could trip Ward; a handful of Ward's soldiers would finish him while the rest held a picnic and eating contests. Yet Rolph says the king and queen have taken the idea seriously.
  14. **Rolph retells the dinner.** Both men in combat rage, Tor standing between them taking Ward's blows meant for Rolph, aura blasting uselessly off the shield like a mountain in the way. Then calmly talking about who could marry Ursula, in Rolph's old court livery, so that to Ward and Thorgood it was as if the king had ordered them to heel. Then he walked off, not winded, holding no grudge, though he had every right.
  15. **Tor's reading.** Tor snorts: Ward didn't notice him; Maria didn't recognize him or didn't care, and he can see why, since he isn't important.
  16. **Rolph and Sara.** Rolph sits back down beside Sara, close enough that they touch. Tor smiles at it, and files it under things women won't feel about him; Maria taught him that. He has his work, and now his friends. Rolph stood by him back then as a "merchant's son"; knowing he's the prince makes it weigh more.
  17. **"Who's Doretta?"** Tor changes the subject: he keeps hearing the name. Trice, gently, tells the short version like a story for children. Trice tells the tale and Rolph gives the moral, paying off her "Not tonight" from chapter 7 (user, November 1, 2026).
  18. **The tale.** A hardworking shopkeeper in a big city befriends a regular customer and one day proposes to her in the street. She's minor nobility (a knight's daughter, depending on the teller) and spurns him in public for reaching above himself. Humiliated, he sells the shop and joins the army to die with honor; instead he becomes a fine soldier, saves the prince's life and becomes his friend (Trice looks between Tor and Rolph and snorts), rises to general and then count (ours: Count Wylde). Twenty years on he finds her married to a baron, kills the baron in battle and everyone in the barony, her whole family, and leaves her on a small island to starve and think about what her cruelty cost.
  19. **The moral.** Rolph drinks and passes the bottle. Darkly: it never happened; it's a lesson for children that you're polite and kind whoever you think you're dealing with. "Don't be a Doretta" is a saying every noble child hears many times a month. Some still fail.

## Beats

1. **"Who?"** Sara, sharp. Doretta is a thing you are, not a name. Trice, low: "Was it Maria?" Tor looks like both doors are guarded.
2. **Rolph stands** across the fire, not looking at Tor. "Rolph. Don't." "A story's got to be told properly or it goes off. Like milk." He drinks from Walt's milk crock all through.
3. **The roommate.** Twenty, a closed carriage, expecting his own room; a second bed and a boy reading a sigil book for the third time. "Lots of people know where Two Rivers is." "Name one." "Mother." The window catch; the accent gone in hours.
4. **The asking.** The girl by the window in theory. Rolph said ask her; he'd have sworn she wasn't peerage; the worst is a full card or a friend. Only a dance. "I could have put him in a glass case."
5. **The flowers.** Two evenings of coaching; Tor in the wood at midnight. The queen would have kept them. Sara: "What was in it?" Tor: celandines, blackthorn, early primroses, ivy. "It wasn't very much."
6. **Forty breaths.** Rolph drinks and stares at the sea. At lunch, in the cafeteria: the invitation was perfect. "Or she could just have said no. That would have been *fine*."
7. **Tor jumps.** Trice's hand on his arm; he jumps, apologizes, gives the arm back. Sara keeps her eyes on Rolph so nobody looks at Tor. "What did she do?"
8. **The screaming.** Poor, ugly, dirty, catching; six minutes; two fourth-year girls walk her out. "And he just stood there. With the flowers."
9. **More.** "There was more?" Weeks of stories; nineteen days in the room; bread on the desk; the letter home already written. Rolph wrote to his mother that night; she wrote to the headmaster. A man on the stair. He won't repeat it.
10. **Kolb.** First-years get an under-instructor; Tor got Kolb, every afternoon. Not a muddle in the roll: Kolb came to the room door and asked if Tor could run. Running, dodging, going left. "You aren't exactly a big job."
11. **Howard Turnbull.** Her family sent for her before exams; Rolph assumed disgrace, "or married off to Howard Turnbull." Tor: "Oh! I know who he is now." Everyone laughs.
12. **Countess Ward.** Trice: the candle-wax dinner explained. Sara: "Maria who?" "She's a countess." Trice admired the snub and is furious with herself; Ursula's worth ten of her. "What if you made war on her family?"
13. **The picnic war.** Trice's stiff sideways hug. Tor: a hundred and twelve squares and Davie, home for supper; trip Ward, two men sit on him, the rest have an egg and spoon race. "It's not a war if it's one person and a picnic." Rolph: Mother and Father aren't laughing. "He tried. For about a quarter of a bell."
14. **The dinner retold.** Rolph doesn't remember most of it ("You're not driving"), so he tells it from Trice, his mother and a frank guard. The mountain; the floor like a leaf; asking for a husband for Ursula in the king's falcon; Thorgood and Ward brought to heel; walked off with no grudge.
15. **Tor snorts.** Ward didn't know he was there; Maria didn't know him, "it's not as if I'm *anybody*."
16. **Rolph by Sara.** Shoulder to shoulder; she knows the sum and doesn't move. Tor smiles like a man outside a lit window. "You wrote to the *queen*. When I thought you were a merchant's son." "She was only my mother then, as far as you knew." "I know. That's what I mean."
17. **"Who's Doretta?"** Trice: "I said not tonight. It's tonight." Nurse's voice.
18. **The tale.** Ribbons; a no in the street, said kindly, in front of the neighbors; a soldier who meant to die; the prince's friend (Trice looks from Tor to Rolph and snorts); Count Wylde; twenty years; everyone, the mill, the dogs; a rock with one tree.
19. **The moral.** The crock is empty; Rolph passes it anyway. "Don't be a Doretta." Sara: merchants have it too; Doretta's father goes bankrupt. "Some of us… still go and stand up in a cafeteria."
20. **The goat.** Tor: "I wouldn't put a *goat* on an island." Trice: "We know. That's rather the trouble. Nobody else does." She takes the crumbled biscuit from his fist.
