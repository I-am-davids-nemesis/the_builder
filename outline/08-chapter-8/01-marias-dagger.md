# Marias Dagger

- **Summary:**
- “Who?” Sara asked, sounding scared... or maybe angry, Tor couldn't tell. What he could tell was that she seemed to know instantly that Doretta wasn't the actual name of the girl at all, which had to be true, because Tor had never known a woman by that name in his life. Trice sat up from where she'd been sitting, her eyes going wide. “Was that... Maria?” Her voice was soft and low, deadly sounding. It reminded Tor that no matter what else these girls might be, rich and pampered things that couldn't even cook a basic meal or probably start a fire without special gadgets or devices, they were both deadly in their own way. They could kick his butt anyway. That was dangerous enough, for the time being. The Prince stood all the way up and moved to the other side of the fire so that he could address both girls at the same time. “Yes.” He looked at Sara particularly, avoiding Tor's gaze altogether. Probably because now that Tor got what he was referencing, he really didn't want the topic discussed. The most embarrassing moment of his life shouldn't become a fireside tale. Ever, if he had his way in the matter. Apparently, he didn't though since the Prince kept talking. “It was in our first year, about halfway through. I'd been given a roommate of all things. Can you imagine that? Me? One that the dean himself had decided no one would ever expect the heir to the throne of Noram to be placed with. Making the boy nearly perfect in a way. I think, it’s just possible, that I may have also been put with him to learn true humility, too. I mean, here I was, lord high of the mighty and who was he? Just some little backwoods boy, the son of a baker, with a fraction of my education, a quarter my size... and about eight times my intelligence. I was, naturally, a little put out at first, since I'd expected my own room, and personal servants. Maybe a spare girl or two brought in a few times a week for comfort and relaxation, not some country lad underfoot all the time. But... after the first day I found that I kind of liked Tor, who seemed smart and kind, even if he did come from a place that I'd never heard of, and talked of baking a little too often... He had a really thick accent then too, which he got rid of in... days. Hours, possibly. It was amazing to see happen. How he changed so fiercely, in what seemed like moments.” Rolph smiled. “Anyway, he decided, at the urging of myself, to ask a girl that he'd noticed in one of his classes to the student party at the beginning of spring. She was a pretty thing, not a grand beauty like either of you, of course, but nice enough looking. I didn't recognize her as peerage and really, what was the worst she'd do when he asked? Tell him she already had a date or let him down easy, perhaps? Or, if desperate enough, she might arrange for a friend to go with him instead, which would still give him a date at least and after all, he thought he could be interested in her, not that they were true loves, destined to be together for all eternity. He wasn't even trying to get her into bed. How cute and innocent is that? Besides... why would she ever even consider saying no? I really figured that even if she had another date, she'd drop the other fellow and pick Tor instead. Maybe I'm biased because he's my friend but...” Rolph had both girls staring at him, and milked it for all it was worth, spinning it as if a popular tale rather than the most embarrassing moment of Tor's life. So far anyway. “So, steeling himself after practicing the right words, coached by me, so you know they were perfectly correct, I didn't let him go in sounding like a backwoods hick or anything, he approached her after dinner one night, and handed her a very nice arrangement of flowers that he'd spent most of the afternoon on. You know how he is with magical projects, but understand this, he’s like that with everything he does. That means it was meticulously done, it looked, well, my mother wouldn't have thought twice about receiving such as a gift and would have called it well done, and she can be picky about flowers. It was more than good enough for a school girl. Maybe too good? He could probably make a living doing flower arrangements in the Capital, with very little improvement. It was that good. Seriously. Elegant. Delightful. If he'd given it to me I probably would have agreed to go with him to the party. You two probably would have done him in your dorm room without waiting to hear what he was asking for... and really, it's not even an insult to suggest you would. It really was that nice.” He drank again, doing it deeply, not speaking for nearly two minutes, looking off into the distance instead. His face seeming both pensive and slightly sad. “So, he approached her and started saying the needed words. That was done perfectly. Smoothly. A simple invitation to consider going to the party with him if she wasn't already engaged or busy that night. No presumption that anything more would happen or hint of anything untoward. Really, it was well crafted inside the rules. Leaving her a hundred ways out if she didn't want to go with him. She could have said that she couldn't go due to a previous arrangement, or that she was engaged otherwise. She could have offered a friend that she felt would suit him better, you know the obvious tricks one might use, or even...” Rolph took a huge breath and continued, obviously angry, even after nearly two years. “Or she could even have simply told him no.” Sara winced and Trice reached out and touched Tor's arm, which caused him to jump away from her a little bit. He instantly felt bad about it, since she was just trying to be nice, but it had startled him, being unexpected. “What...” Sara looked at Rolph hard, not looking toward Tor at all. Her gaze felt conspicuous in its absence. “What did she do?” Tor dreaded this next part. He could only hope that Rolph wouldn’t say the words in front of these two. They were practically the only girls not related to him that didn't try and run away screaming. No one had ever actually run away, of course, but close enough. The Prince grimaced. “She... was vile. Evil to him, for simply asking if she'd consider possibly, maybe, going to a party, in public, with him. She screamed, called him names, said he was worthless, dirty and probably had diseases she didn't want to catch. She accused him of having sex with animals and probably men to boot. As if anyone would care on that score? She went on and on like that for at least six minutes, and would have probably kept going in that vein to this day if some older girls hadn't pulled her from the room by force. That was... bad. But what she did next...” This caught Tor by surprise, there was more? He didn't remember anything else, except everyone turning on him for a while after that. Glaring at him mostly. “She started a campaign of rumors, one in which Tor was the villain of everything wrong that could be imagined. He didn't know about it at the time because he wouldn't leave the room for nearly three weeks. They almost had to kick him out of school then, and send him home. I actually had to contact my parents to intervene for him, in fact. Which they did without question, Tor, based only on what I'd told them before. For a while, the faculty was afraid he'd kill himself if he knew what was being said about him. I won't repeat any of it, even now. Let's just say that the reason Kolb started training Tor to fight and protect himself was directly related to that... and no Tor, it wasn't a mistake, and no one put him up to it. Kolb chose you for training personally. That's why you're never getting out of it now, even after everything has died down. I think he views it as a personal challenge. You aren't exactly huge after all.” He continued on, telling them how Maria was recalled home for some reason eventually, what, he hadn't known at the time. He'd thought that her family had caught wind of what their daughter was doing and sent her away in shame, at least to another school, if not to live with Howard Turnbull on his country estate. Trice nodded then. “I see. I'd wondered at the meeting why Tor looked so upset. That explains it. I'm surprised she even dared show her face. Can you believe that Sara?” “Maria who? Is she... a servant at the palace? Or...” Blonde hair flopped just a little as she shook her head. Trice fixed here with a gaze that was serious and seemed stern, locking eyes. “No... She married a Count. Count Ward to be exact. Vile bitch. No wonder the Count chose to sleep with Ursula, who isn't half as pretty, instead. That's... well, she wouldn't have tried that if she knew who Tor was going to be, I bet. That's why there are rules about such things. What if Tor decides to make war on her family, over the slight? Or on Count Ward?” Trice moved closer to him and hugged him awkwardly from the side. He wondered what the heck she was talking about, but enjoyed the attention for a bit, even if she was being silly. Him making war was a patently ridiculous thought. Maybe, if fortune were with him, he could be a minor inconvenience to someone like Ward personally. Trip him or something if he caught the man off-guard. Duck down behind him and get someone else to push him over maybe. An actual war was out of the question. His army would just destroy him. That... sounded better than the facts would really allow for, he knew. A few men from his army would destroy him. The rest would probably hold a picnic while they waited for the two or three actually working to finish up. They could hold games and eating contests while they waited. Short of getting his brothers involved, it would just be him against tens of thousands of armed men. That, oddly enough, the idea of Torrance Green Baker personally going after County Ward in retaliation, was a fact not lost on the King and Queen according to Rolph. That Tor might take it in mind to do battle, alone, against the Count and his entire army. “When we fought, the Count and I, both in full combat rage, Tor simply stood between us Sara. Ward pounding him from behind and me trying to work my way around to get at the louse. When I tried to strike, he'd jump in the way, taking the blow instead. We both tried to use direct effect too and were blasting aura the whole time, and it did nothing. It was like a mountain had been placed in front of us for all that we could get at each other. It's the shield he made of course, but no one in the world could really take those away from him. He could just make more if it were ever tried. Notice how even that buffoon Ward stepped into line after that? It was especially impressive when Tor had just been standing there taking a beating intended for me from the man, and started talking, very casually, about who we could get to marry Ursa. Of course, wearing my old court livery also worked out there. To Ward and Thorgood both it was as if the King himself had stood there and commanded us all to heel to his will... Then enforced it, demanding good behavior, since violence would not be allowed that night. Then he just walked away as if nothing happened, not even out of breath, not holding a grudge or even calling us to task for it, which would have been well within his rights.” Tor snorted then. “I’m pretty certain that Count Ward just didn’t notice I was even there. Maria didn’t seem to get who I was. That or she didn’t care. Which... I can see, I guess. It’s not like I’m important or anything.” Moving back to sit down, Rolph brushed at the blanket as if getting some sand off, then sat back, the side of his body touching Sara. Tor smiled. It looked like fun, but if Maria had taught him anything it was that women just didn't like him that way. It didn't matter. He'd learned to be happy with his work. It wasn't the same as being loved, but it was all he really had. Well, that, and his friends now. Rolph really had been there for him, after all. Given the man wasn’t just his friend, but the Prince of their entire Realm, that suddenly seemed a lot more impressive. It had been when he’d simply been the son of a merchant. Now those actions carried even more weight. For Tor, at least. Changing the topic, a bit, he tilted his head. “I’m... Who’s Doretta? I don’t know anyone by that name. I’ve heard it a few times now though.” Trice looked at him and gave him a gentle look. “Oh... that... makes sense, I suppose. It's probably not a story you were told as a child. No one would name their daughter that, now, of course. Let me see here, I’ll try for the short version, since the rest of us all know it. Um...” After a moment, she went on, as if telling a story to a group of children who had gathered around. It was, he had to admit, cute of her. “A long time ago there was a shopkeeper, a humble young man who sold goods in a decently large city. He worked very hard and was successful, being smart and clever. A woman came in regularly and they'd talk, in a friendly way. So, one day, working up his courage, the man finally proposed marriage to her in the street in front of the shop. The woman spurned him publicly, since she was of noble blood, if not very highly placed. The daughter of a Knight or something. It varies depending on who's telling the tale. She told him no, and suggested he should have known better than to reach above himself, and dare imagine he was worthy of someone as grand as her.” She took a deep breath, and then nodded, slowly. “So the shopkeeper, despondent and humiliated, left his shop, selling it off, and then joined the army, seeking his death in combat. That way he could redeem his honor you see. Wash the stain of humiliation away in his own blood. Years passed, and he proved to be a good fighter, eventually saving the life of the Prince himself, who became his good friend...” Trice gave him a significant look, her eyes darting several times between him and Rolph. A soft snort escaping her, she continued. “As unlikely as that always seemed to me... Time passed and the shopkeeper was made a general, then, eventually a Count.” Trice shook her head then, her eyes going dark. “After a time, over twenty years later, he found where the girl lived, her having married a sitting Baron, and took his army to meet her husband in battle, and slaughtered him. Then he killed everyone in the Barony, including the woman's entire family. After that, as she cried for her losses, he had her put on a small island so that she could slowly starve to death, reflecting on how her own actions of spurning him so cruelly had led to all that death and destruction.” Rolph waved the bottle, drinking a bit more then passing it to the others there. Finally the Prince spoke, his voice dark. “It isn’t a real tale, of a thing that actually ever happened, of course. An instructional story for children, only. A warning to all of us that, no matter who we believe we are dealing with in the moment, being polite and kind is the correct path. We even have a saying that is used. Don’t be a Dorretta. We all hear that, a half dozen times or more, each month, as we grow up. Some still fail. But they do know better. Tragic things can happen if you fail that lesson, after all.”
