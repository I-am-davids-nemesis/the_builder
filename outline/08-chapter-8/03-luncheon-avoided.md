# Luncheon Avoided

- **Summary:**
- as they approach the city they see a big commotion outside of the city.  There is a circle of guards and a whole crowd of people around them.  There are also people running out from the city carrying wood planks.
- it isn't clear what is going on at first -- some kind of uprising? riot?
- As they land on the outskirts of the crowd, Tor starts putting it together, what is happening: someone fell down a well.
- Rolph hasn't figured it out yet, and so runs forward and shouts: "situation report!" in that particular tone of voice that you normally only hear coming from Kolb. apparently a large man, flying in and yelling 'situation report!' gets a response from the guards
- Long quote to break down: “Child, boy, seven years old. Tanners’ child. Fell down an old well. Middens, really.” The man didn't seem to care who they were after that, and turned back to shout at the men with the timbers. The man growled in frustration. “Gah! No good. Crap, those sticks won't hold a man. We have to send someone down there. We need thick timbers. Now.” He screamed, sounding a little panicked, suddenly. Tor could see why. The sides of the hole in the ground were already crumbling in. He could feel the field of the earth shifting, even if it looked solid at the moment. The walls were already collapsing. It probably wasn't even all that deep, but if it caved in, the child died. A thin man in red and white dangled over the edge, feet held by three other men, all big and brawny workman types. They pulled the guardsman out and he shook his head. “No good Captain... I can't work my shoulders past the first bend and if I try to dig it starts to hit the child. We can't wrap a rope around a corner neither. Someone has to go down.” He looked the captain dead in the eye and shook his head grimly. If he couldn't do it, as thin as he was, no other man there could either. A woman who looked to be about twenty-five or so, with dark brown hands and smudges of darkness on her face, cried out loudly. It was heartrending. “Simon. My baby!” A swarthy man in stained clothes and a stout leather apron took her in his arms. Both had tears in their eyes. The fellow, stout and strong as he seemed, tried to comfort her, but it didn't seem to be working too well. Of course, it wouldn’t. Her boy was about to die in an old shit pit. No amount comforting could work on her at all, given that. Tor sized up the man who had tried to get to the boy. Thin, like he was, but also far too tall. Nearly as large as Rolph. That made a difference in how big a person really was. He knew that firsthand from fighting practice. Big skinny guys weighed a whole lot more than he did. They were bigger around, too, for all they looked the same. The bones of the shoulder and waist were bigger even if they barely managed to hold any muscle at all. It was a difference in scale. Tor, however, was probably not that much larger than the boy stuck down the hole. Looking around Tor thought they had what he needed. Enough to try and save the boy, anyway. Someone had to try something. Even if it would end in his death. It was, he knew, the part of the man to save the women and children. No matter the cost to himself. That single fact, of course, settled the matter in his mind.
-Tor takes command -- he asks for rope, and for all of the luggage plates to be put on the strongest piece of luggage.  He strips down to his underwear, and asks that the rope be tied around his ankle.  He knows they need to hurry because he can feel the sides of the hole starting to collapse, slowly.
- the man who ties the rope warns him that he better get it off in 10 to 15 minutes, because it is tight enough to cause damage.  Tor asks for a guard with the steadiest hand that can take instructions from him. They get an older grizzled solider over, and he tells them that he is going to go down and try to tie a rope on the boy, and he is going to need to be pulled out once he signals -- he might be screaming during the pulling, but keep going. The man agrees: "Got it"
