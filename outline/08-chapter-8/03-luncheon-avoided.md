# Luncheon Avoided

- **Summary:** the rescue at the midden outside the capital, the same day as scene 2, before the king's luncheon (third bell after noon), which they miss (user, November 6, 2026). Facts from the user's notes and the original's version of the scene; canon unless marked.
  1. **The commotion.** Coming in to land outside the walls (flying inside them is forbidden), they see a ring of guards, a crowd packed around it, and people running out of the city gate carrying planks. From the air it could be a riot or an uprising.
  2. **Tor works it out first.** They land at the edge of the crowd. Tor puts it together before anyone tells him: someone has fallen down a hole.
  3. **"Situation report!"** Rolph hasn't caught up yet, so he runs in and shouts it in Kolb's own drill-yard tone. A very large man dropping out of the sky and barking that gets an answer from the guards.
  4. **The captain's facts.** A boy, seven, a tanners' child, down an old well that's really a midden. The captain stops caring who's asking and turns back on the men with the planks: too thin, they won't hold a man, someone has to go down, thick timbers, now. He sounds suddenly panicked.
  5. **Why he panics.** The sides of the hole are already crumbling. Tor can feel the field of the earth shifting under the look of solid ground. It probably isn't deep, but if it caves in, the boy dies.
  6. **The thin guardsman.** A thin man in the city guard's red and white has been dangling head-first over the edge, three big workmen holding his feet. Pulled out, he reports: he can't get his shoulders past the first bend, digging drops dirt on the child, and a rope won't go round a corner. Someone has to go down. He's the thinnest there; if he can't, no other man can.
  7. **The parents.** The mother, about twenty-five, hands stained dark brown from the tannery and her face smudged, cries out for Simon. The father, swarthy, in stained clothes and a stout leather apron, holds her; both are weeping, and his comfort does nothing.
  8. **Tor sizes it up.** The guardsman is thin but tall, nearly Rolph's height, and Tor knows from fighting practice that tall thin men are wider through the bones of shoulder and hip than they look. It's a matter of scale. Tor is hardly bigger than the boy. He thinks the people around him have what he needs to try.
  9. **What settles it.** Tor believes it's a man's part to save women and children whatever it costs him, even his life. That decides it. (Tor never says this aloud; it shows in how fast he moves, per AGENTS.md §3.)
  10. **Tor takes command.** He asks for rope, and for all the luggage plates to be put on the strongest piece of luggage. He strips to his underwear and has the rope tied round his ankle. He knows they have to hurry: he can feel the sides slowly giving.
  11. **The warning.** The man who ties the rope tells him it's tight enough to do damage; it has to come off in ten or fifteen minutes.
  12. **The steady hand.** Tor asks for the guard with the steadiest hand who'll take orders from him. They bring an older, grizzled soldier. Tor tells him: he'll go down and tie a rope on the boy; when he signals, pull him out, and keep pulling even if he's screaming. The soldier: "Got it." (The soldier works the loaded luggage as the lift; assumed.)
  13. **Down.** He's dangled over the hole and lowered in with a coil of rope. About ten feet down he finds the boy, puts the spare shield on him (Rolph's, which Tor asked for before going down; user, November 6, 2026), and ties the rope round him. He calls to be pulled up.
  14. **The girl.** Then he sees a second child, a girl of three or four, a few feet farther down and in. He calls to wait; they don't, because he told them not to. He rips off his own shield, throws it at her, and tries with everything he has to switch it on from a distance, hoping she's close enough. By now it's completely dark: the soil is coming in on them.
  15. **Rolph's field.** In desperation he tries what he's seen Rolph do (in our codex, Rolph's hereditary field is a line of force, so that's what Tor makes; invented, November 6, 2026): a raw field, an organized sense of movement and connection, between himself and the girl. Filth is rammed into his mouth; his ankle tears and stretches under the rope, and something snaps. He nearly blacks out and can't let himself: he can't let go of the field, or she dies.
  16. **Into the light.** Light all round him; he's up, with the boy, and the soldier starts lowering them to the ground. Tor screams to go higher, he has her, hoping to use the connection to pull her free; it comes out a weak gasp. They're set down, and people surround them and drag him and the boy away from the edge as the pit caves in.
  17. **He won't allow it.** He will not let a child die because he's weak or doesn't matter. He holds out his right hand and remembers the field he made at the beach for moving water from one place to another (chapter 7), and builds one like it for something much heavier: dirt. It has to be far, impossibly stronger, since dirt doesn't flow, and it has to happen at once, because a buried shield gives no air. He believes it isn't possible for someone like him. There's no choice.
  18. **"Move."** He tries to firm his voice and manages a whisper, flat, all emotion gone. Rage comes over him: anger at dirt, at the unfairness of it, stupid or not. He says it again, gasping, trying to shout and failing: "Move. I command you… Move!"
  19. **Rolph screams.** Nobody moves until Rolph screams at them to run. It's not the manliest sound the prince has ever made, but people scatter.
  20. **The fountain.** Earth erupts upward in an instant, high into the air like water from a fountain: a tan cloud of dust and things that should have stayed buried. Last comes the girl, a shiny piece of copper clutched in her fist (Tor's shield), rolling end over end through the air, crying. She hits the ground and stays there as if glued or nailed down, sobbing in a high squeal. The shield did switch on after all (a faint shimmer round her), and nobody, Tor included, ever knows whether the shield or his field saved her (user, November 6, 2026).
  21. **Crying means alive.** Tor sits and holds the boy, his left leg throbbing horribly. He might have cried then; in his relief, he passes out instead.

- **POV / mode:** Rotating third, in three sections (user, November 6, 2026). **Rolph** from the approach until Tor goes over the edge: he watches the smallest person there take command of a guard captain, and the reader sees how scared Tor is through how polite he gets. **Tor** in the pit, alone and in the dark, through the girl and the snap (traumatic; AGENTS.md §3). **Rolph** again from the light: he's the only one near enough to hear the whispered "Move," which is why he's the one who screams.
- **Location:** Outside the capital's white wall, near the river below the city: an old midden among the tanners' yards, which stand outside the walls and downwind for the smell (invented). A ring of red-and-white city guards, a crowd, planks dropped in the dirt, hot afternoon sun (`codex/locations/the-capital.md`).
- **Present:** Tor, Rolph, Sara, Trice. The guard captain; the thin guardsman; the three workmen holding his feet; the man who ties the rope; the grizzled soldier with the steady hand; Simon's parents (tanners); Simon, seven; the girl, three or four; the crowd; people running out with planks. None named except Simon.
- **Reader learns:** Tor reads the earth's field without a device and sees the danger before anyone; Tor gives orders, and grown men take them; he can throw a raw field at a distance, the way Rolph's hereditary one works, and he can move earth by will alone, with no build; Rolph's "situation report" voice is borrowed from Kolb; Tor's left ankle or leg is badly hurt; both children live; the luncheon with the king is missed.
- **Hidden (and how):**

  - **What Tor believes about himself:** that it's impossible "for someone like him," and that he doesn't matter. Shown through what he does, never stated; the gap between that and what the crowd just saw is the point.
  - **Who the girl is, and why nobody missed her:** not raised in this scene; an unnamed woman carries her off. Revealed in chapter 9 (user, November 6, 2026).
  - **That Tor copied Rolph's field:** only Tor knows (his section, in the dark). Rolph feels the earth field gather and can't explain it (user, November 6, 2026).
  - **Whether the shield or Tor's field saved the girl:** never settled for anyone, Tor included (user, November 6, 2026).
  - **What Tor's injury is:** the draft shows only the left foot at a wrong angle; chapter 9 decides (user, November 6, 2026).

## Beats

1. **From the air** (Rolph). The white line of the wall, and outside it a dark knot of people, a ring of red caps, men running out of the gate with planks on their shoulders. Trice says riot; Rolph thinks worse.
2. **Landing.** They drop at the crowd's edge. Tor is already looking at the ground, not the people, and says, quietly, that someone's down a hole.
3. **"Situation report!"** Rolph hasn't heard him. He wades in and bellows it in Kolb's voice, and to his own surprise a captain answers.
4. **The captain.** Boy, seven, tanners', old well, midden really. Then he's shouting at the planks.
5. **The guardsman comes up** filthy and shakes his head: the bend, the dirt on the child, the rope and the corner. Someone has to go down.
6. **Simon.** The mother's cry; the father in his apron holding on. Rolph looks away from them and finds Tor looking at the guardsman's shoulders, measuring.
7. **Tor's orders.** Very polite, very precise, very fast: rope; the luggage plates, all of them, on the strongest trunk; Rolph's button; Rolph's shield; please. He's unbuttoning before anyone answers. Rolph has seen Tor go scarlet at a bare ankle, and he isn't blushing now; that's how Rolph knows.
8. **The knot.** The rope-man's warning, ten or fifteen minutes. Tor thanks him.
9. **The steady hand.** The grizzled soldier, the control put in his fist. "Even if I scream." "Got it." Tor goes over the edge head-first.
10. **Down** (Tor, alone). Dark, the smell, his elbows scraping, dirt ticking down. Ten feet. A small foot; then the boy, warm. Rolph's shield over his head, touched on; the rope knotted under his arms. "Up!"
11. **The girl.** Past the boy, farther in, a smaller face. "Wait!" They don't; he told them not to. He tears his own shield off, throws it, reaches after it with everything he has. The light goes.
12. **Rolph's way.** He makes the field the way he's seen Rolph do it, a holding between himself and her. Dirt in his mouth; the ankle; the snap. He doesn't let go.
13. **Light** (Rolph). The trunk rises out of the ground with Tor upside down under it and the boy roped below him, plastered brown. Tor is saying something; the soldier lowers them; hands drag them away as the pit falls in on itself.
14. **"Move."** Tor sitting up in the dirt with his right hand out over the collapse. Rolph is close enough to hear the whisper, then "I command you," in a voice Rolph has never heard from him.
15. **Run.** Rolph screams at everyone to run, high, and they do.
16. **The fountain.** The ground goes up like a fountain. Dust, old filth and a tiny girl tumbling through it with a copper plate in her fist, landing and sticking flat as if nailed, howling, a faint shimmer round her. Rolph can't tell whether it's the shield or something else; he's never seen a shield hold a body down.
17. **Crying.** Tor holds the boy in his lap and stares at the girl. His eyes fill; then he simply tips over. Somewhere up the hill, the bell rings for the king's luncheon.
