# Dinner Distractions

- **Summary:**
- back at the house, Burks returns to dress Tor for dinner.  Tor is worried he looks terrible; Burks reassures him he doesn't, but Tor thinks he would say that anyway. Oh no, Burks replies, I would definitely tell you so we could fix whatever was wrong.
- Burks dresses Tor in the colors of the king, with the King's crest on his breast (a falcon with wings spread, a shield held in its talons). The emblem is in the Kings colors, silver with a background of purple. The rest of the outfit is black.  Burks explains that wearing the kings symbol like this means that he holds the kings favor. He has to be careful not to make any promises or be rude to anyone at dinner because, wearing the kings favor, it would seem as if the king was being rude, or making the promises.
- Tor brings his extra equalization devices, and a room cooling device. He ensures that he is wearing his newest shield (the fourth version -- he keeps on coming up with flaws in the prior versions)
- Rolph intercepts him on the way to dinner and tells him that he needs to ensure that he doesn't eat first -- its a tradition that the host eats first due to the risk of poison -- so he must ensure that he eats before Ward.  The tradition demands that the person most likely to be poisoned be sat at the far end of the table, and eats last (as a guarantee that he hasn't been poisoned).  Tor doesn't understand -- Rolph claims it doesn't have to make sense, it is just tradition. Tor realizes that he ate first the night before; Rolph tells him it was okay, his mother saw it as him stating that he was going first to demonstrate that there was no poison.  So, tonight the last two to eat will be Ursula and Ward.  Tor again is confused because Ursula had done nothing wrong -- well, it might not seem that way to anyone else, Rolph cautions.  She was the one carrying the device.
- Rolph also cautions Tor not to challenge Ward to a duel.  Tor is flabbergasted. How could he as a commoner just jump up and challenge a Count to a duel? Even if he won he would be put to death.  Rolph tells him that the Count wouldn't accept anyway -- finding some way out of the duel, because with Tor's shield he is practically invincible.  Tor agrees to keep all of his challenges to people he can physically beat, so children and babies for the most part. Rolph laughs, but then sobers, and says he will try to do the same, but it might not be easy.
- when they get to the formal dining room, the queen is already there.  Quavel is at her side.  The queen greets Alphonse and Tor the same way, with an air kiss to the cheek.  Tor is terribly embarrassed.
- The queen asks Quavel to bring a couple of the cooling plates to the dining room to keep it cool.  Tor interrupts and says he brought one already, and he swears it will be enough, even for the large dining room.  He is more worried about the servants who have to go in and out in their uniforms. He says he brought 10 personal cooling devices for them, and asks Quavel to hand them out.  Unfortunately, there are at least 15 staff, and 10 aren't nearly enough. Tor immediately takes his off and hands it over; Rolph chuckles and hands his over to Quavel as well. The queen, seeing this self sacrifice is forced to follow suit.  Tor was still feeling bad that two people wouldn't get them.
- Tor looks around for a chair, but Rolph tells him they won't be seated until all the guests arrive
- Trice arrives, looking beautiful in a dress of light violet, with her dark brown hair up with a few ringlets falling around her face. She greets them with a smile, and asks if the cat is out of the bag.  "hard to hide a whole palace" Connie replies.  She then fills in Trice on the whole situation.  She doesn't feels as concerned as Tor expected her to be, which puzzles him. She hardly shows any reaction until the queen mentions that Tor offered to marry her -- then her eyes fly open.
- Facts from the original's passage (wording not to be reused, `AGENTS.md` §2):
  - The queen confirms Tor's offer and calls it remarkable: he didn't know who Ursula was beyond her engagement to Alphonse, and acted properly with no thought for himself.
  - It can't be allowed yet, because of the class difference, but the offer has weight: nobody hearing of it can say the crown did less than its best for Ursula.
  - They might find Tor a title, so the match would look correct; there are a few empty baronies.
  - Her tone is low and slightly playful. Trice checks that nobody's looking and, with her back to the room, sticks her tongue out at "Aunt Constance" for teasing.
  - Rolph: it may be no joke; by the end of the night it may be all they have to keep the peace. And Tor would make an excellent baron.
  - Trice pauses, takes a slow, very deep breath and, not smiling, agrees that he would.
- Ursula comes in with her father and mother, and introduces them to Tor as "Master Wizard Torrance Baker". The parents are very happy to meet him, feeling his offer and defense of their daughter was quite something.
- Weirdly, to Tor the Count feels like he can't place his age, as if he is only 12, which doesn't match his wizard status.
- “Tor, this is Count Horace Thorgood and his wife, the Countess Marigold. You both know of Tor already, it seems." Rolph introduces Tor to Ursula's parents, stating that Tor and Patricia plan to help them this evening if they can.
- The king comes in; everyone bows (except the queen).  The king mentions that he hopes that things can be resolved quickly because there is a problem in Ford county -- there is a drought that is going to need a lot of planning and work done. He reminds Tor that it is west of the capital, and it is typically dry and warm there, but still next to the Pacifico ocean. So, lots of water, but not water than can be used for irrigation.  the best crop lands are hundreds of miles inland. and they do have recurring droughts; so the plan is to figure out food delivery to Ford (even though food usually goes the other way).  It is something that happens every decade or so.  Tor immediately starts thinking about how he could deal with that kind of problem.
- before he can get too deep into the problem, Trice shakes him out of the trance to keep him focused on the evenings issues.
- Just then Count Ward and his wife enter -- which Trice thinks is pretty bold, almost a declaration that they are untouchable. But, she has a low opinion of Ward's intelligence.
- Tor sees the count, clearly a huge noble, nearly as big as the king, if not quite as proper.  While he is admiring the figure he cuts, he suddenly notices his wife, on his arm, walking together (quite a bit shorter).  It is Maria ( if you recall from the codex, Maria had spurned his request to go to a school dance, and caused Tor to be super depressed for a couple weeks, before she left the school.).
