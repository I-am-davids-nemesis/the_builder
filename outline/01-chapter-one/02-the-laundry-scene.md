# The laundry scene

## The laundry scene, beat by beat

All beats are canon (user, October 9, 2026) unless labeled.

1. **Tor washes clothes** to have wet laundry for the test. He's soaping and scrubbing; the water suds up.
2. **Davie is at the next tub, mystified.** His water won't suds up and he can't see why, because he doesn't know laundry takes soap. He's grumpy about having to do it at all, and afraid someone will notice he doesn't know how.
3. **Tor stops to help.** He teaches Davie basic laundry and lends him soap, *before* testing his field. Something small someone else needs comes ahead of the thing he has spent thirty hours on. This is the scene's main character beat for Tor.
4. **Dorgal comes in** and teases Tor about being a washerwoman.
   **Davie keeps his head down.** The mockery he was afraid of lands on Tor instead, and he stays out of it.
5. **Tovey comes in** to see how this new magic works, and **a crowd follows him**, because whatever Tovey is interested in, people want to see.
6. **The test:** the field dries the laundry in moments. (Whether it happens before or after Tovey arrives is assumed; after gives it the biggest audience.)
7. **Everyone wants one.** Tovey wants it for his mother. The crowd all want copies.
8. **Trice and Sara arrive**, after Tovey.
9. **Trice gets Sara to estimate what it's worth.** Value depends on what the magic is made on and how long it lasts. Tor's is cheap wood and paint, so it's worth far less than one on good material, but still **30 gold**, a significant sum.
10. **Tor can't give it to anyone.** He has to turn the prototype in for testing. He promises nothing, but **assumes he'll be making copies for all of them.**
11. **Rolph comes by** shortly after Trice and Sara, and gets Tor out of a social situation he isn't equipped to handle.

**Staging notes** (invented):

- **Davie's ignorance has to be visible, not explained.** He's working hard and getting nowhere: scrubbing, wringing, holding a shirt up to the light, glancing at Tor's suds and then his own flat gray water. The reader should work out what's missing a beat before Tor says it.
- **Tor's help comes the way his reaction rules say:** yes before Davie finishes asking sideways; no mockery; a practical lesson, possibly with a baking or home comparison. He shouldn't make it a kindness Davie has to thank him for, because that's what lets Davie accept it.
- **The scene keeps getting bigger.** One noble who needs help, then a rival who mocks, then Tovey and his crowd, then Trice putting a price on it. A private test of a laundry trick turns into a public event with a market value, which is the book's engine in small.
- **Dorgal and Tovey are a voice contrast in one scene.** Dorgal jokes at Tor's expense; Tovey never does (`tovey.md`, "Never").
- **Davie keeps his head down** while Dorgal teases Tor. He was just helped, and doesn't speak up for the person who helped him. It shouldn't be played as villainy; it's fear, and the reader should see it cost him something (a glance away, a shirt folded too carefully).
- **The perception gap, staged.** Tor is afraid of Tovey and treats him as a threat that could strike out at any slight: extra courtesy, careful words, keeping his distance. Tovey is not disdainful and not hostile; he's curious and wants a gift for his mother. Since we don't narrate Tor's thoughts, the gap shows as a mismatch between Tor's behavior and what Tovey actually says and does. The reader sees both; Tor sees only his fear. (See `codex/characters/tor.md`, the perception gap.)
- **The price is a character beat for Trice and Sara.** Trice asks; Sara answers. Trice knows the question that matters and who in the room can answer it. Sara's answer, broken down by material and how long it lasts, shows knowledge the reader didn't expect from her.
- **30 gold lands differently on each person.** To Tovey and the crowd it's a purchase. To Tor, a baker's son, it's probably more than his family sees in a long time (assumed), and he made it out of scrap. His reaction (disbelief, an apology, an urge to explain that it's only paint) is the scene's comic and painful peak.
- **Turning in the prototype is the hook.** Demand without supply: everyone wants one, he has one, and it isn't his to give.
- **The unspoken promise has to be visible.** Tor never says he'll make copies; he assumes it. With no narrated thoughts, the reader sees it from outside: he counts heads under his breath, asks someone how many students board at the school, looks at the wood square as a quantity, or mutters a figure of days. Nobody in the room hears a promise. The reader understands he has made one to himself. This is the engine's step 3, and the start of chapter two's trouble (how long would that take, and who said he had to?).
- **Another case of the perception gap.** The crowd want to buy; Tor hears an obligation. Nobody asked him for free copies for everyone; he sets himself that task. (invented framing of canon)
- **Rolph's rescue ends the scene.** He's the friend who knows how to do what Tor can't: talk his way out of a room full of nobles. His arrival should feel like air coming back in. Use his reaction rules (loud, physical, joking) and let him leave with Tor before anyone pins Tor down.
- **Sara's appraisal shows her background before anyone states it.** She prices the field as someone who has handled the best magic the continent has to offer and grew up around the capital. The reader can infer a merchant house from how she talks about cost and material. (invented staging of canon)

**Why the laundry scene works** (craft notes; invented):

- **It introduces magic through need.** The reader sees what the field is for in the hands of someone who can't manage without it, before anyone explains how building works.
- **It shows the class order through an inversion.** The noble can't do laundry and the baker's son can, which sets up the culture clash without a word of exposition.
- **Davie and Tor are both outsiders, in opposite ways.** Davie hides that he doesn't know something; Tor hides that he knows a great deal. Both fear being laughed at.
- **Reaction rules collide.** Davie asks for help sideways and won't admit ignorance; Tor says yes before the sentence ends and calls the field "not really anything". Neither will say what's actually happening, and the scene's humor and tension come from that.
- **It shows who Tor is before it shows what he can do.** He helps with the soap before he tests the magic, so the reader meets his character first.
- **The laundry is a setting the reader can see:** wet wool, cold water, a bar of soap and the suds it makes (or doesn't), a tub, steam, a painted square of cheap wood set down among the dripping shirts, and then a room too full of people in brown tunics.
