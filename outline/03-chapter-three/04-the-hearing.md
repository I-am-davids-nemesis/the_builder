# The hearing

- **Summary:** Tovey arrives with Kolb, embarrassed rather than angry, and apologizes first to Trice, then to Tor. Tor's "what are friends for?" turns, by noble custom, into a declared friendship, and Trice makes sure it sticks. Wensa calls the assault treason; Tovey and Trice say Tor only came to Trice's aid. Tor admits he didn't know they were cousins, or that Trice was noble; he helped because she's his friend. He's done fifty fields; Tovey is astonished. Wensa reveals she's a royal guard, draws a cutter hilt, and demands Tor be held; Tor stands to go, falling into Kolb's guard, and Kolb laughs the room back down.
- **POV / mode:** Rotating third: Trice (user, October 17, 2026); she sets up the friendship and realizes why Tor helped her. She doesn't know her own field, so she hides nothing from the reader. Camera: the room, wide.
- **Location:** The warded room in the administration basement.
- **Present:** Tor, Trice, the headmaster, Wensa; Tovey and Kolb arrive together. Campbell's presence after the door is (assumed).
- **Reader learns:** Tovey hates his rage and is ashamed of it; Trice and Tovey are first cousins; noble declared friendship and what it means; the assault is treason in law; Tor didn't know Trice was noble; Tor's production rate is extraordinary; Wensa is a royal guard; nobles are falling into rages more and more often.
- **Hidden (and how):** Whom Wensa guards (she says "royal guard," never whose); Trice's field (she's the victim here, and nobody suspects her); the cause of the rage epidemic (Wensa suspects Tor; everyone else just doesn't know).

## Beats

All beats canon (user, October 17, 2026) unless labeled.

1. **Tovey arrives, with Kolb.**
2. **Tovey apologizes to Trice first:** is she hurt, did he...? Trice mocks the idea that he could hurt her, admits he did a number on the side of her head, and credits Tor with distracting him; it got closer than she liked. The **right side of her face is swollen**, already bruised spectacularly.
3. **Tovey apologizes to Tor.** Tovey isn't angry; he's **embarrassed** at the loss of control a battle rage implies, and he's bad at showing embarrassment.
4. **"What are friends for?"** Tor waves the apology off. He means it sarcastically and manages not to sound it.
5. **Trice seizes it.** She tells Tovey to be thankful they have friends like this, jokes that she'd have haunted him if he'd killed her, and teases him out of his gloom. She's holding **something wrapped in a soft light-blue cloth**. Among nobles, declared friends are rare and treated very differently from everyone else; Trice is making sure Tor's remark counts as a declaration. Tor believes "there are no debts between friends," and has no idea he's just become a count's close ally.
6. **The law.** Wensa: Tor assaulted a member of the peerage. That's treason.
7. **Tovey's counter:** he doesn't remember Tor assaulting him, only coming to Trice's aid, which Tovey would have begged him to do if he could have.
8. **Trice concurs:** Tor tapped Tovey on the shoulder to get his attention, then ran away.
9. **Why they were out.** Asked (by Wensa, most likely) why the two of them were in the courtyard at that hour: they'd gone outside to **argue about a family matter** where nobody would hear. (user, October 17, 2026; replaces the planned flashback)
10. **Tor didn't know.** He admits he didn't know Trice and Tovey were related. They're **first cousins** (close enough that marriage between them isn't a worry). Trice asks why he helped, then. Tor can't answer, because it's obvious: she's his friend, and they're in business.
11. **Fifty.** Tor yawns and admits he finished the **first fifty** drying fields.
12. **Trice understands.** He didn't know she was noble. He thought he was saving a friend from a noble giant in a rage, for no possible reward, out of friendship alone. She's **mildly smitten**.
13. **Tovey is astonished** at the number. As far as he knows, most manufacturers can't make **a hundred in a day** across the whole company.
14. **Wensa is undeterred.** Tor should be removed from the school, out of caution. She admits she's a **royal guard**, draws a weapon (it looks like a **cutter hilt**), and demands Tor go to a **holding cell** until people come to their senses.
15. **The stand-off.** Tor gets to his feet, ready to go with her, but falls into a **defensive stance Kolb taught him**. Wensa sees it; her suspicion climbs and she readies to attack.
16. **Kolb laughs.** He taught Tor all of that, and Tor takes direction well: he ran away from Tovey, as taught.
17. **Tovey politely asks for restraint.**
18. **Trice jokes** that if Wensa keeps this up, Tor might break off his business with her and Sara.
19. **What's at stake.** Nobles have been falling into combat rages more and more often lately, and nobody knows why. Everyone fears a plot. (It is one, but a different one than anyone expects, and it won't come clear for a long time.) Wensa's case is that Tor might be behind it.

- **Placement of beat 19.** The summary gives the rage epidemic as background. It can come out in Wensa's argument (beat 14) as the reason her caution isn't absurd, or in a line from the headmaster. Keep her voice: trade-and-debt words, rhetorical questions that are instructions. "Friends like this" can be where she sets the declaration in public.

- **The friendship is the scene's pivot, and nobody explains it to Tor.** The reader should catch the room's reaction: Tovey going very still, the headmaster's eyebrows, Wensa's mouth tightening. In Trice's viewpoint, she sees the opening and takes it in a heartbeat; that's her reaction rule (`patricia.md`: new value → secure it).
