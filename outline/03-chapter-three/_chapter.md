## What the chapter must do

1. **Pay chapter two's cliffhanger.** Tor survives Tovey's rage by doing exactly what Kolb taught him: hit once, then run. Then he gets the real bill: striking a noble is a high crime, punishable by death.
2. **Turn the danger from a fist into the law.** The threat moves from a giant in the dark to a warded room full of powerful people, and to Wensa, an accounting instructor who turns out to be a royal guard and decides Tor is a threat.
3. **Reverse the perception gap on Tovey.** Tor expected a noble to destroy him; Tovey apologizes. Tor's offhand "what are friends for?" becomes, by noble custom, a declared friendship, which he doesn't understand he's made.
4. **Tell Tor (some of) who his friends are.** Trice is a duke's daughter and Tovey's first cousin; Sara is a Debri; the special school trains people who might be assassinated. Rolph explains it all and stays hidden himself, even while telling a story about himself.
5. **Open the book's mystery plot.** Nobles have been falling into combat rages more and more often, nobody knows why, and people are afraid of a plot. Tor is the convenient suspect.

## Tone: light and funny

The chapter starts with Tor running for his life and ends with breakfast. Keep the comedy in the gap between Tor's certainty that he's about to be executed and the room's slow, embarrassed, very aristocratic tidying-up.

- **Tor's last will and testament** is the opening comic beat: bruised, filthy, sure he'll hang, he hands Rolph the order for his mother and asks him to sell the rest and send the money home. Rolph is barely awake.
- **The hearing is a farce of good manners.** A count apologizing, a duke's daughter with half her face swollen making jokes, a combat instructor laughing, and one deadly serious woman with a cutter hilt who is the only one taking Tor at his own estimate.
- **"What are friends for?"** is Tor's dry, self-mocking humor, said straight. The reader sees the room take it literally.
- **Escalating numbers continue.** Tor yawns that he's done fifty. Tovey knows whole companies can't do a hundred in a day.
- **The danger stays real:** Wensa really would cut him down, and the chapter's last word on her is Rolph's warning that royal guards don't play by the rules.
