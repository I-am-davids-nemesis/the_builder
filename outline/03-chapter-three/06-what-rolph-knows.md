# What Rolph knows

- **Summary:** Tor flops on his bed. Rolph, told only that Tovey was attacked, asks if he's even alive. Tor tells him the whole story and asks how he could have survived it, let alone not be in trouble. Rolph explains Tovey, Trice ("different"), the special academy, and Sara, and tells Tor what he told Sara about him. He packs four fields in an ornate box for his mother, tells Tor to take his time on the food dryer, and learns about Wensa. Tor's joke about her makes him laugh; the accusations against Tor make him furious. They go to breakfast, Rolph warning that royal guards don't play by the rules.
- **POV / mode:** Rotating third: Rolph (user, October 17, 2026); secret-keeper rule: explicit action, with word choice the only hint at his family. Camera: the room.
- **Location:** Tor and Rolph's room, then out toward breakfast.
- **Present:** Tor, Rolph.
- **Reader learns:** Who Trice is (the Ducharina Patricia Alyson Morgan, a duke's daughter) and how she came to be "Trice"; that Sara is a Debri; what the special academy is for (as Rolph tells it); that Sara checked up on Tor through Rolph; Tor's baking; Tovey hates his rage; Tor was accused of being an Austran spy.
- **Hidden (and how):** Rolph's royalty (he tells the "royal brat of a Prince" story about himself as if about someone else; his gift will beat even his father's; he wraps it so his mother's friends won't be mean); Wensa guards Rolph (Rolph's anger at the news is the only sign: he'd suspected someone, and now knows who); whether Trice's "different" means more than being a target (Rolph doesn't say).

## Beats

All beats canon (user, October 17, 2026) unless labeled.

1. **Flop.** Back in the room, Tor flops onto his bed, trying to find the energy to get breakfast before class.
2. **Rolph knows nothing** except that Tovey was attacked. He asks if Tor's even alive.
3. **The whole story,** from Tor's side. He attacked a count in a battle rage, survived, and doesn't even seem to be in trouble. He hopes Rolph can explain how.
4. **Tovey.** Rolph knows him well enough to think he won't try to get back at Tor, especially if he thinks Tor did it to help his cousin. **Tovey hates combat rage.**
5. **Trice is "different."** Even though she's a noble, she's different, which is why she's spent **years at the special academy**.
6. **The academy.** Rolph asks if Tor knows what goes on there. Tor thinks it's a school for spies and assassins. Rolph: it trains people who might be **targets** of assassination, nobles and the children of high merchants. That's why Trice and Sara are there: Trice is a **duke's daughter**, and Sara is a daughter of the **Debri** house.
7. **Sara will change the deal.** Tor guesses Sara doesn't know who he is and will want to change their deal when she finds out. Rolph disagrees: as a merchant she had to know about him before she took a risk on him. She **came by the evening before asking about him**.
8. **What Rolph told her:** Tor's family, his work ethic, and that he can **bake well enough to open a bakery**, a fallback career if building doesn't work out. That really impressed the girls. Tor doesn't get it. Rolph explains he can't even make toast, and **most people he knows can't either**.
9. **Out of his league.** Tor sees Sara as pretty and rich, which only depresses him: another person too good for the likes of him.
10. **Who is Trice?** Rolph: the **Ducharina Patricia Alyson Morgan**. Known to Tor as Trice; **Tricia** in polite circles; only close friends call her Trice. It's a childhood name: some **royal brat of a Prince** couldn't pronounce her real name as a baby, and it stuck. She seems fine with it.
11. **The gift.** Tor suggests Rolph send some fields to his mother now; if he hurries, they'll arrive in time. Rolph brings out a **very fancy, ornate box** and puts **four** devices in it (four of the ten; the other six go separately, as gifts for her friends). He has to wrap it well or his mother's friends will be a bit mean about it. At least the present will be beyond anything anyone else gives her, **including his dad**.
12. **The food dryer.** Rolph, distracted, asks when the food drying field will be ready. Tor can't do it now: he needs to focus, and he's wiped out by the stress and no sleep. Rolph, surprised and afraid he's pushing Tor too fast, tells him to **take his time**.
13. **The week ahead.** Tor lists everything he has to get done in the next week, and says Wensa has it out for him. And by the way: did Rolph know she was some kind of **secret royal guard**? Rolph is **very angry**, and demands to know if Tor is sure.
14. **The joke.** Tor says it would be more fun if Wensa were a bit younger. Rolph pictures a smitten Wensa chasing Tor and laughs hard.
15. **The accusations.** Tor says he was accused of being an **Austran spy**, and somehow of trying to get Trice killed, maybe with some odd magic that makes nobles angry. He tells how Kolb came to his defense.
16. **Rolph's fury.** Tor accused of trying to harm anyone, when he was trying to protect Trice from Tovey in a rage: Rolph gets super angry and stalks out, and they go to breakfast together.
17. **The warning.** On the way, Tor anxiously makes sure Rolph knows he didn't try to hurt anyone. Rolph does. But royal guards don't play by the same rules as everyone else, and if Wensa is worried about Tor, Tor needs to be careful.

**Staging notes** (invented):

- **The scene is a ledger of reveals, so pace it as a conversation, not a briefing.** Rolph explains in fast, overlapping, self-answering runs (`rolph.md`), interrupted by Tor's questions and by the box, the wrapping, the hunt for string. Spread the reveals across business.
- **"Different" is a loaded word, and Rolph doesn't unpack it.** He means she's a target; the reader, who knows about Trice's field (`patricia.md`), may hear more. Keep it ambiguous.
- **The academy explanation is Tor's perception gap, inverted.** He assumed the worst (spies, assassins), and the truth Rolph gives is kinder and sadder. It's also only half: the school does train spies and killers (user, October 17, 2026). Rolph isn't lying, he's choosing. The reader, who saw the knife and the powder in chapter two, can feel the gap; Tor can't.
- **Sara checking up on Tor** is the reverse of Tor's fear: he thinks she'll pull out once she knows who he is; she already knew, and asked, and bought anyway. The reader sees it; Tor doesn't.
- **The bakery line is the funniest reveal.** In a school of nobles, a boy who can feed himself is exotic. Tor's bafflement should be total; Rolph's "I can't make toast" is a confession he's proud of.
- **The Trice name speech is the chapter's biggest dramatic irony.** The "royal brat of a Prince" is Rolph (user, October 17, 2026). He tells it as gossip about a stranger. The narration must not wink. In Rolph's viewpoint, the secret-keeper rule holds: one word choice at most (a fond "brat," a too-exact detail about what the baby said).
- **The box is description work and a hint.** Too fine for a merchant's son: inlay, a velvet lining, a clasp that's its own small piece of work. Rolph treats it as ordinary. Tor looks at it as Sara would, pricing it, and doesn't ask.
- **"Including his dad"** goes past fast, in the same breath as the wrapping.
- **Rolph backing off on the food dryer** is the chapter's empty engine step made visible (`_chapter.md`): no next idea, because Tor has nothing left. It's also a small, real moment: he sees Tor is spent, and is afraid he's been treating his friend as a supplier. Let it land in one beat of silence.
- **Rolph's anger is a voice signal; spend it here.** His "never" rule: he only goes formal when truly angry (`rolph.md`). "Are you sure?" can come out cold and exact. He'd suspected his family had someone watching him; now he knows who, and that she drew on his friend (user, October 17, 2026). He doesn't explain any of that to Tor. Then Tor's joke breaks it, and he's himself again, laughing. Then the accusations bring it back, harder. The double turn is the scene's spine.
- **Tor's Wensa joke is a rare one,** in the only company he jokes in. Keep it non-sexual: a comic image of the stern guard chasing him across the yard with a love letter instead of a cutter, which is what makes Rolph laugh (`AGENTS.md` §9).
- **"Stalks off" and "go to breakfast together"** happen at once: Rolph storming out the door and down the corridor, Tor trailing in his wake, still trying to get his boot on.
- **The warning is the chapter's last word.** Rolph, still angry, gets quiet and practical: royal guards don't play by the rules. It's both a friend's advice and, for the reader, a hint at how he knows.
