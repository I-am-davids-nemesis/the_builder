# The chase

- **Summary:** Tor has Tovey's attention and does what Kolb taught him: runs. Thrown, he lands far enough away to keep running; Tovey falls behind, then rises off the ground and flies after him. Tor hides in a wood until dawn, when Tovey comes to himself and flies back toward school. Alone, Tor works out that striking a noble can be punished by death, finds he's still carrying the sack, and decides to deliver Rolph's order and take the blame so Trice won't.
- **POV / mode:** Rotating third: Tor. Camera: follows Tor. (invented)
- **Location:** The courtyard, out across the school grounds, into a wood near the school; dawn at the wood's edge. (route and wood invented)
- **Present:** Tor, Tovey.
- **Reader learns:** Tovey's rage includes flight; Tor runs well and Kolb's advice works; striking a noble is a high crime, punishable by death; Tor will put himself between Trice and the law.
- **Hidden (and how):** Why Tovey is raging (Trice's field; nobody in the scene could know); why he stops at dawn (he just comes to himself; no explanation).

## Beats

All beats canon (user, October 17, 2026) unless labeled.

1. **Run.** With Tovey's attention on him, Tor follows his training and runs, but the numbing field makes him clumsy and slow.
2. **The blow.** Tovey lands a good hit that sends Tor flying. He lands bruised, not broken, and far enough away to get up and run.
3. **The pursuit.** Tovey is loud on his heels, then slowly falls behind.
4. **The silence.** After a couple of minutes the footsteps stop. Tor risks a look back: Tovey **lifts off the ground** and comes on, **flying**.
5. **The wood.** Tor runs harder and makes for a wood, where trees take away the flier's advantage.
6. **Dawn.** At first light Tovey seems to come to himself, and flies off toward the school.
7. **The bill.** Tor understands what he did: **striking a noble is a high crime, punishable by death.** He's terrified, and determined that **Trice won't suffer for it**.
8. **The sack.** He's still carrying the bag of drying fields, which he would have dropped to get away if he'd thought of it.
9. **The plan.** Go back to the dorm, make sure Rolph has the fields for his mother, then turn himself in and take the blame so Trice isn't blamed.

**Staging notes** (invented):

- **The numbness is his chase handicap.** Chapter two ended with it worst when Tovey looks at him; here it should ease as the distance opens (legs coming back to him, one stride at a time), which is how the reader feels Tovey falling behind before Tor looks.
- **The flight is the scene's big image.** Sparks lifting off the ground with him, the sound of running replaced by nothing, a huge shape against the stars. Tor's reaction can be one muttered word. The reader shouldn't be told that rage-fields can do this; it's simply happening.
- **The wood is physical comedy at the edge of terror:** branches, a stream he didn't see, the sack snagging, Tor apologizing to a tree. Above, sparks moving between the treetops, searching.
- **Dawn turns it.** Gray light, birdsong, Tovey's shape stopping in the air like a man who's forgotten why he came into a room, then going home. The fear doesn't leave with him; it changes shape.
- **The realization should be visible, not narrated.** In camera mode especially: Tor sitting in wet leaves, saying the law out loud to himself ("You hit a count, Tor"), counting the consequences on his fingers. Then the sack clanks when he moves, and he looks at it as if it's betrayed him. Laughing at himself would be in character.
- **The decision is his friend-threatened rule** (`tor.md`) turned on himself: he solves the problem with frightening clarity. Deliver the order, take the blame. He's settled before he stands up.
- **Kolb's lesson pays off here** and gets named in scene four (Kolb: "he did as he was taught and ran away"). Plant it: the run is good, steady, practiced, the one competent thing in the night.
