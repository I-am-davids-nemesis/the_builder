## Known elements

### 1. Wensa's shadow, and the introduction

All canon (user, October 17, 2026) unless labeled.

1. **Wensa follows Tor around.** She eats in the cafeteria when he does, is always close at hand, and is especially close when he's testing ideas for the shield.
2. **Dorgal corners him after dinner,** with two henchmen:
   - **Marco** (where he fits in is not yet decided);
   - **a frightening man slightly too old to be a student**, with eerie eyes: nearly all black, except for the irises.
3. **The accusation.** They accuse Tor of having a girlfriend: an older one, a member of the faculty.
4. **Tor's move.** While they're threatening him, he decides to push the two threatening groups together and hope they clash.
5. **He goes back into the dining room** and asks Wensa to come out and explain to Dorgal and his henchmen exactly why she's following him around.
6. **She does.**
7. **The introductions.** Tor introduces them to each other, very properly, but gives each the title that suits his view of them rather than their actual station.
8. **He walks away**, hoping they don't decide to gang up on him.

**Staging notes** (invented):

- **Location:** the cafeteria and just outside it: a passage, a stair, the yard by the kitchen door. The dining room has to be close enough that Tor can go back in and fetch her, and full enough that everyone sees him do it (`codex/locations/the-cafeteria.md`: whatever happens there has an audience).
- **Wensa coming out is the surprise.** The reader expects her to refuse or ignore him. She comes because it serves her: she wants to see who is threatening the boy she's watching, and to be seen watching. Her reaction rule (`wensa.md`): something she can't explain near Rolph → treat it as a threat. Dorgal's men are now that.
- **Tor's courtesy is the weapon.** He's polite to everyone, and the politeness is what makes it impossible for either side to back out. The reader should see him decide (a pause, a glance from Dorgal to the dining room door, a small "oh") before he moves; we don't narrate the thought.
- **The black-eyed man** is description work and a mystery, and something strange (user): his eyes mean something, explained much later. Show them through whoever holds the viewpoint, without explaining them. He has a **subtle influence on Dorgal** that isn't revealed until much later (user); on the page, let Dorgal glance at him before he speaks, or say something a little crueler than Dorgal usually is. Nothing more. Tor, who senses danger in people (`tor.md`; Douglas Sorvey, chapter two), should feel this man the way he felt Sorvey.
- **"Royal guard," out loud** (user). Tor names her station exactly, and gives Dorgal and his men titles from his own view of them (invented suggestions: "Master Sorvey, who would like to frighten me," "and his friends, whose names I don't know, who are helping"). Naming her is Tor's honest, literal habit (`tor.md`: reaches for the literal truth) used as a weapon, and it hands Dorgal's group a secret. Wensa's reaction should be visible and cold. Whether Tor realizes he's blown her cover, after Rolph's warning, is a choice for the draft; the comedy is better if he realizes a beat too late, while walking away.
- **The exit** is the last beat: Tor walking away, not looking back, muttering, and the reader not seeing what happens between them (cut, or a camera that stays with Tor).

### 2. The template, and the lonely board on the desk

All canon (user, October 17, 2026) unless labeled.

1. **The design is finished,** after testing against **cutters, force, fire, smoke, and hot dripping wax**.
2. **Ten of them.** As soon as the design is done, Tor sets out to make **ten** shields, for his friends: one for Kolb's test, the rest for people he thinks need one. (user, October 17, 2026)
3. **Template first,** as always. Making the template takes **another two days**, running over the **rest day**.
4. **The absence.** Tor asks Rolph to go and get his absences **excused** for the second day of building.
5. **He finishes.**
6. **Rolph is mortified,** thinking Tor has been pushing himself this hard for the **food drying field**.
7. **Tor laughs** and points at the lonely **wooden template** on the desk: the food dryer. He finished it **a couple of days ago**, and he's been working on the shield ever since.

**Staging notes** (invented):

- **The tests are a montage, and Wensa is in all of them** (element 1). Cutters (Kolb's, or one borrowed), force (the pell, the iron bar, Kolb), fire, smoke (the air-not-dust problem from chapter two), and hot dripping wax, which is the comic one: a candle held over the field, wax beading off it. Show each test from outside; the idea lands visibly when one fails.
- **The template build is a trance, like chapter one's,** not like chapter two's copying, in an **isolation chamber** (user). Show it through the chamber's keeper, Bet Haskins, and through Rolph: bells passing, the rest day's quiet, food left at the door and not eaten, Rolph sent off to the office. Then Tor back in the room with ten shields.
- **Asking Rolph to excuse him is a quiet change.** In chapter one Tor feared expulsion for missed classes and wouldn't ask anyone for anything. Now he asks, mid-build, without looking up. Rolph going to the office for him is friendship in action.
- **Rolph's mortification pays off chapter three.** He told Tor to take his time on the food dryer, and was afraid he'd been treating his friend as a supplier. Now he thinks he did it again, worse.
- **The laugh is the reversal.** The thing Rolph is guilty about was done days ago, alone, without fuss, and has been sitting on the desk the whole time, unnoticed. The template's loneliness on the desk is the image: one plain board, while ten shields stand finished. The joke is on Rolph's guilt, not on Tor.
- **The engine's step 3 from chapter three, paid late.** Chapter three left the next idea empty because Tor was too spent. The food dryer got done anyway, offstage. Revealing it here, in passing, is the payoff.

### 3. The night after, and the test

All canon (user, October 17, 2026) unless labeled. The user flagged possible plot holes here; see the open questions.

1. **Done.** The night after the second day of building, Tor announces to Rolph that the shield is finished (element 2's ending).
2. **Rolph gets him food and water.** Tor sleeps through the night.
3. **An ordinary morning.** They go to their regular classes and meet for lunch.
4. **To the testing ground,** with several people following along.
5. **A crowd is already there:** the headmaster, several faculty including **Wensa** and the **building instructor**, **Sara and Trice**, and **Tovey**. **Rolph** walks over with Tor. (user, October 17, 2026)
6. **Tor announces he's ready.** Wensa attacks with **special magic rods** almost before he can activate the shield.
7. **The first rod** seems to do nothing, until she turns it on the **pell**, which **explodes**.
8. **The second rod** does nothing, until she points it at what's left of the pell, and the **wall behind it bows out and cracks**.
9. **The third rod** sets **fire** all around Tor. Tor realizes it's actually dangerous: the fire could burn up all the air, and he has no way to breathe once it's gone.
10. **Wensa stops it** before it becomes a real problem, and he comes out unharmed. (user, October 17, 2026)
11. **Kolb claims the right to test.** He picks up a practice weapon and beats on the shield for **twenty minutes**.
12. When that does nothing but **crack the ground under Tor's feet**, Kolb draws his **cutter** and swings it at Tor. It does nothing either.
13. **Kolb laughs:** the test was supposed to be the shield on a **pell**, not on Tor.
14. **Wensa counters** that a builder needs to have faith in his equipment. She seems **happy**, for some reason.
15. **Kolb is satisfied.**
16. **Suggestions.** Several people suggest other things to try (drowning Tor in the nearby **pond**, for one). Nobody tries anything.
17. **The headmaster is enormously pleased,** and suggests Kolb beg Tor to provide a few for the school's use. Tor doesn't realize how valuable these things are.
18. **Sara asks** whether the shields are still included in their deal. Tor agrees, still at the **10%** level. He isn't sure how he could spend the money it would bring in.
19. **Trice goes further:** Tor should **sell the template to the Debri house**. He'd collect royalties on the shield without having to do the work, although Tor is much faster than other builders.
20. **The scale.** Tor is very naive about how many people will want this; it has to be **explained to him**, and even then he doesn't connect it with how much money he stands to make. That's where the chapter ends. (user, October 17, 2026)
21. **Version 2 is already under way.** Every way he now sees the shield could have failed (the air, the ground cracking under him, the delay before it was on, water) is an idea; he's thinking about the next version before anyone has finished congratulating him. (user, October 17, 2026)

**Staging notes** (invented):

- **Kolb's punchline is the perception gap's biggest payoff yet.** Since chapter two Tor has believed Kolb meant to test the shield on *him*, and he has walked onto the testing ground prepared to be killed politely. The test was always meant for a pell. Nobody told him because nobody imagined he'd think otherwise. Tor misunderstood all along (user, October 17, 2026).
- **The rods are unexplained magic seen from outside.** Each one's "nothing happens" beat is the shield working; the pell and the wall show what it stopped. Let the crowd's reaction carry the scale.
- **The fire is the one serious beat,** and the next idea. The shield passes air and keeps out smoke (chapter two's requirement, tested in element 2), but it can't make air. Tor's realization should be visible: a hand to his throat, a held breath, muttering. Wensa ends the fire herself (user, October 17, 2026), which matters to how the scene reads: she isn't trying to kill him, though Tor may come to believe she was.
- **The suggestions are a comic chorus.** Students and faculty offering ways to kill Tor more thoroughly, cheerfully, while he stands there apologizing.
- **Wensa's cover is gone** (user, October 17, 2026). Royal-guard rods in front of the headmaster, the faculty and half the school, after Dorgal's group already heard it from Tor. From here she stops pretending to be only an accounts mistress.
- **Wensa is testing, and happy for Rolph** (user, October 17, 2026). A shield like this could keep him alive. She can't say so; her pleasure is visible and unexplained. Tor reads her attack as an attempt on his life, and will go on believing she means to murder him, whether or not she does.
- **The stakes are hinted, never raised** (user, October 17, 2026). A shield that stops a cutter changes warfare. The headmaster's "for the school's use" and Wensa's pleasure are the only signs that the crown will care. Nobody says it; Tor doesn't see it.
- **The 10% stays ambiguous** (user, October 17, 2026). "Still at the 10% level" works whichever way round it is.
- **The money scene is the escalating-numbers joke at its peak.** Twelve gold a drying field; what is a shield that stops a cutter worth? Sara knows. Tor can't imagine spending it.
