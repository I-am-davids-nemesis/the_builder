# The tests

- **Summary:** Tor does exactly what the headmaster told him: his regular routine, every class, every bell. And in every spare hour he tests the shield design harder, because now there's someone who might really mean him harm. Wensa is always there: in the cafeteria when he eats, nearby when he works, closest of all when he tests. The design is tried against cutters, force, fire, smoke and hot dripping wax, and by the end it holds.
- **POV / mode:** Rotating third: Wensa (secret-keeper rule: explicit action; word choice the only hint at whom she guards). Her viewpoint shows the shield's value growing in front of a trained eye, which sets up her pleasure at the test. Camera: follows Tor, with Wensa always somewhere in frame. ## Beats

All beats canon (user, October 17, 2026) unless labeled.

1. **Routine.** Tor keeps to his regular routine, as the headmaster advised: building master early, meditation, theory, lunch, combat, to the letter.
2. **Ramping up.** In every spare hour he tests the shield design, harder than before, because now he has someone to fear who might really mean him harm.
3. **The shadow.** Wensa follows him around: eats in the cafeteria when he does, is always close at hand, and is especially close when he tests ideas for the shield.
4. **The tests:** cutters, force, fire, smoke, and hot dripping wax.
5. **It holds.** By the end, the design is finished. (The scene can end here, or run on into the dinner that opens scene two.) (invented)

**Staging notes** (invented):

- **The routine is a comic list kept with terrible seriousness.** The headmaster said act normally, so Tor is the most normal student in the history of Lairdgren: early to everything, every bell answered, homework in on time, while visibly not sleeping.
- **Wensa as a running gag.** At the next table. On the bench by the water butt. In the doorway of the workroom at the eleventh bell. With a ledger she never writes in. Tor greets her politely every time ("Good evening, Mistress Wensa"); she nods. The fear shows only in his courtesy (`tor.md`: threatened → extra courtesy).
- **The tests are a montage, each with a failure that teaches him something** (engine step 3 in miniature). Suggestions:
  - *Cutters:* Kolb, or a borrowed hilt, on a test square at the pell. The first version turns the blade; a later one stops it.
  - *Force:* the pell, the iron bar, Kolb's waster. Somewhere a square cracks and Tor writes a note.
  - *Fire:* a candle, then a brazier.
  - *Smoke:* chapter two's air-not-dust problem. Smoke clings to the outside in the shape of the field, like the powder did, while air passes. This is where he solves it.
  - *Hot dripping wax:* the comic one. Tor holding a candle over the field and watching the drops bead and roll off, delighted, muttering, while Wensa watches from the door.
- **In Wensa's viewpoint, the shield's value lands on her before anyone else.** She knows what a cutter does to a guard, and what it would mean if one couldn't. She can't say why it matters to her. Let her hand go still on her ledger.
- **What she's watching for** is a threat to Rolph, so she watches Tor's hands, his visitors, his materials. Every time she finds nothing, which bothers her more.
- **Tor never sees her as anything but danger.** The reader, in her viewpoint, sees a professional doing her job. That gap is the scene's irony, and it seeds Tor's belief that she means to murder him.
