# The proposals

- **Summary:** For once Tor lets himself celebrate. He goes to tell his friends he can fly. Sara immediately offers to marry him; Trice claims she saw him first. Tor thinks they're joking.
- **POV / mode:** Proposed rotating third: Sara (she makes the offer, and she hasn't had a scene where she's the one exposed). Alternative: Trice. Camera: the room or wherever they're found. (invented; to approve)
- **Location:** To decide: the cafeteria at breakfast, the practice yard, or Tor and Rolph's room. (invented)
- **Present:** Tor, Sara, Trice; Rolph (assumed), perhaps Tovey.
- **Reader learns:** Tor can be proud, briefly; Sara and Trice are interested in him, and both are serious under the joke; Tor can't believe it.
- **Hidden (and how):** How serious they are (they jockey for position in jokes; neither says it plainly); from Tor, everything (he hears teasing).

## Beats

All beats canon (user, October 18, 2026) unless labeled.

1. **Celebration.** This time Tor feels he can truly celebrate. He goes back to tell his friends about his success.
2. **Sara** immediately offers to marry him.
3. **Trice** claims she saw him first.
4. **Tor believes they're joking,** and doesn't take them seriously.

**What's really going on** (user, October 18, 2026): more than half serious. They mean it, in the way of merchant and noble marriages, and they're two friends **jockeying for position**, with jokes as the cover. Tor can't believe any woman is interested in him, largely because of **Maria**, who humiliated him when he asked her to a school dance before the book begins (`tor.md`, `supporting/maria.md`).

**Staging notes** (invented):

- **The release.** This is the first time in the book Tor lets himself be proud. Even now it comes out as news, not a boast (`tor.md`: never boasts aloud): breathless, grass-stained, wind-burned, talking fast, the handheld control in his fist.
- **Sara's offer is immediate and practical.** She's priced it before he's finished the sentence (`sara.md`). Her contract voice, her father's, slower and more even, would be the tell that she means it. She says it lightly.
- **Trice's claim** is trade-and-debt language: precedence, first refusal, an account opened at the laundry. "I saw him first" as a matter of record. She, too, says it lightly.
- **The jockeying** is a friendly contest between two people who are a pair (`patricia.md`, `sara.md`). They look at each other, not at Tor. The reader should be able to read it as a joke and as a negotiation at once.
- **Tor laughs.** He takes it as the kindest teasing he's ever had, and thanks them for it. The reader sees two faces fall a fraction, or not; keep it ambiguous. His reaction rules: flirted with → takes it literally, assumes a compliment is a mistake; a girl is friendly → kindness or a mistake (`tor.md`). The Maria rejection can surface in one line, through Rolph or through Tor's own deflection, without narrated thought.
- **Rolph, if present,** watches all three of them and says nothing, which is its own joke.
- **Non-sexual:** proposals as jokes and as alliances, nothing more (`AGENTS.md` §9).
