# The cold snap

- **Summary:** In the middle of the month a cold snap freezes the dormitories. Tor does a quick design to heat his and Rolph's room by pulling heat up from underground, and realizes the same design can run the other way: push the room's heat down into the ground in summer and cool it. Over a year it should come out about even, so the device hardly needs any power. Sara and Trice come in out of the cold. Sara sees what a room that's warm in winter and cool in summer is worth, and offers to marry him; Trice says she saw him first. Tor thinks they're joking. Rolph watches. Before the cold breaks, several of the devices are working around the school.

**Staging notes** (invented):

- **The cold is physical comedy first.** Breath in the corridor, frost on the inside of the window, the water jug with a skin of ice, Rolph in every blanket he owns plus Tor's coat, students sleeping in their tunics. The school's own heating (a stove at the end of the corridor, the cafeteria hearth) is nowhere near enough.
- **A quick design, not a trance.** This is the opposite of the four-day build to come: an evening at the desk, wrapped in a blanket, muttering, a scrap board and cheap paint. Tor builds because his fingers are too cold to hold the pen. The joke is the speed: Rolph complains, and by the time he's finished complaining the board is done.
- **Where the heat goes** (drafting choice; flag for the user): the board lies flat on the floor, and its field reaches straight down through the building into the ground below it. No pipe, no stake. Tor explains it with a baker's comparison: the ground a few feet down is the same in winter and summer, like the back of a cold larder; he's only moving warmth from where there's plenty to where there isn't.
- **The engine in miniature.** The build (heat) causes a complication (he overshoots; the room goes stifling, so they open the window in a frost), and the complication gives him the next idea: if it can pull heat up, it can push it down. The realization is visible: Tor stops with the window half open, says "Oh," and turns the board over in his hands.
- **Why it hardly needs power** is the bit Tor is proudest of, and he says it as news, not a boast (`tor.md`: never boasts aloud). Winter in, summer out: the ground lends and gets paid back.
- **Sara and Trice come in out of the cold.** Rolph fetched them, or word of the one warm room in the school reached them (the women's dormitory is as cold as the men's). They arrive in blankets, Trice already giving orders about the door.
- **In Sara's viewpoint, the price comes first, again,** and this time it's bigger than the shield, because it's for everyone: every house in Noram in winter, every merchant's warehouse in summer, food that doesn't spoil (the food dryer's sibling). She's priced it before he's finished explaining.
- **Sara's offer is immediate and practical.** Her contract voice, her father's, slower and more even, is the tell that she means it. She says it lightly.
- **Trice's claim** is trade-and-debt language: precedence, first refusal, an account opened at the laundry. "I saw him first" as a matter of record. She, too, says it lightly.
- **The jockeying** is a friendly contest between two people who are a pair (`patricia.md`, `sara.md`). They look at each other, not at Tor. The reader should be able to read it as a joke and as a negotiation at once.
- **Tor laughs.** He takes it as the kindest teasing he's ever had, and thanks them for it. The reader sees two faces fall a fraction, or not; keep it ambiguous. His reaction rules: flirted with → takes it literally, assumes a compliment is a mistake; a girl is friendly → kindness or a mistake (`tor.md`). The Maria rejection can surface in one line, through Rolph or through Tor's own deflection, without narrated thought.
- **Rolph watches all three of them and says nothing,** which is its own joke. He may say one thing afterward, to Tor alone, that Tor misreads.
- **The devices spread** as a short coda: Tor making copies through the cold snap, and the boards going where people are coldest. Suggestions: the scholarship boys in the east wing (again), Sara and Trice's rooms, Davie's floor, the infirmary, the washing house. Sara handles who gets them; Tor doesn't keep count.
- **Non-sexual:** proposals as jokes and as alliances, nothing more (`AGENTS.md` §9). Four students in a warm room in blankets; keep it a common-room scene, door open or students in the corridor.
- **Tovey isn't there** (user). He may be mentioned in passing: in his own rooms, which are probably warm already.
