# Waking Up

- **Summary:** Tor wakes in the palace after the rescue at the midden (chapter 8, scene 3), hurt, and starts work on the Ford water line from bed. Facts from the user's notes; canon unless marked.
  1. **The room.** Tor wakes in a room even fancier than his rooms in the guest house, in a bed that is even larger, or seems so to him.
  2. **The leg.** His left leg is in a splint: metal strips down the side, bandaged tight, with enough padding to keep it as comfortable as it can be. It throbs constantly. (This settles chapter 8's open question: the leg, not just the ankle, is badly hurt; whether it's a break is the question below.) He's dressed in light, loose linen. The room is cool; there's a cooling plate on the wall.
  3. **Thirsty.** He needs water and a privy and sees neither, so he has to work out how to hobble to the door.
  4. **The guards.** Two royal guards stand outside. For a moment he can't tell whether he's a prisoner. They're startled to see him up; one starts "Coun—", cuts himself off, and says "Master Baker." They're there so nobody disturbs him. Tor asks for water and a privy, and they're glad to help, so he isn't a prisoner.
  5. **The king.** Tor asks whether he can see King Richard, about the drought. The guard will pass the request on but can't promise anything.
  6. **His materials.** After a while Tor asks for his working materials from his room. It takes a while: the guards can't leave their post and have to send someone.
  7. **Talking at the open door.** The door stays open while he waits, and he talks (to the guards, or to himself; they don't answer) about the project for Ford County: he needs something big that won't be easy to move and that people won't meddle with. He mentions that the king asked him to get this done.
  8. **The falcons.** A couple of hours later, three large bronze falcons are brought in on Tor's own lifting plates: three plates under each, and even then they ride only a couple of inches off the floor. Tor is surprised they got them through the door.
  9. **Finely.** A royal guard named Finely comes in with them and asks whether they'll do. Tor is very impressed: they'll be perfect if the king allows the project. They're the most interesting thing he's ever put a field on, and their size and weight will make anyone think twice about stealing one; though you'd have to be pretty daft to steal anything with a giant sky river attached to it.

- **POV / mode:** Rotating third, in two sections (default; see the questions). **Tor** while he's alone: waking, the splint, the pain, the hobble to the door (alone and hurt; AGENTS.md §3). **The guard who said "Coun—"** from the door opening to the end: he watches the hero of the tanners' yard turn out to be a boy in a linen shirt asking politely for a privy, then listens to him plan a river aloud, and is there when the falcons come in.
- **Location:** A guest bedchamber in the palace, grander than Tor's rooms in the small guest house (`codex/locations/the-capital.md`): a huge bed, a cooling plate on the wall, a door onto a corridor with two royal guards posted. Water and privy are elsewhere (invented: the room has neither, so it's a sickroom chosen for quiet, not a suite).
- **Present:** Tor; two royal guards at the door (unnamed; one makes the slip); whoever is sent for his materials (offstage); the men who bring the falcons; Finely, a royal guard. Three bronze falcons.
- **Reader learns:** what Tor's injury is (the splinted left leg); he's being kept in comfort and under guard, for his protection; the guards are under orders and already think of him as something more than a commoner (the "Coun—" slip); Tor's first thought on waking is the drought, not himself; the court has quietly decided to help, sending the falcons without a word to him; the falcons are the anchors for the Ford water line, chosen for weight so nobody will steal or move them.
- **Hidden (and how):**
  - **What the guard was going to say:** cut off mid-word; nobody explains, and Tor doesn't notice or doesn't dwell on it. The reader may.
  - **Who sent the falcons:** the guards never answer Tor; Finely just arrives. Whether the king heard Tor's talk through the open door, or the guards passed it up, stays offstage.
  - **How much time has passed and what's been said about the rescue:** nobody tells Tor; the next scene (the king's visit) brings the news of the children and the story already growing.
  - **Who the girl is:** not raised here; the king tells it in scene 2.

## Beats

1. **Waking** (Tor, alone). Ceiling first: too high, painted. The bed goes on and on. He tries to sit up and the leg answers.
2. **The splint.** Metal strips, tight bandage, padding; the throb with every heartbeat. Linen he doesn't own. Cool air, and a cooling plate on the wall; he checks it's one of his.
3. **Water.** Dry mouth, a full bladder, no jug, no privy door. He mutters himself through it ("Think, Tor") and makes the trip to the door by furniture, chair to bedpost to wall.
4. **The door** (the guard). It opens on the two of them. The patient is up, gray-faced, holding the frame. "Coun— Master Baker." Their orders: nobody disturbs him.
5. **Prisoner?** Tor is very polite, which the guard reads as nerves. Water, please, and a privy. Both arrive at once, and Tor visibly relaxes.
6. **The king.** Could he see King Richard? It's about the drought. The guard says it'll go up; no promises.
7. **His things.** Later: could someone fetch his working materials? The guards can't leave the door, so they send a page, and it takes time.
8. **Thinking aloud.** The door left open; Tor in a chair by it with the leg up, talking: something big, heavy, that people won't touch; the king asked him to do it. The guards say nothing. The guard notices the other one is listening too.
9. **The falcons.** Two hours on: three bronze falcons, each floating a couple of inches up on three of Tor's own lifting plates, steered through the door with a hand's breadth to spare.
10. **Finely.** "Will these be appropriate?" Tor is already on his good knee beside one, hand on the bronze. The best thing he's ever put a field on, if the king allows it, and nobody will steal it; though you'd have to be daft to steal anything with a river attached. The guard decides he likes him.

**Questions:**

1. **The injury:** what exactly is wrong with Tor's leg?
   - Default: A broken shin and a badly wrenched ankle from the rope; splinted, weeks to heal, no healer can fix it at once.
   - Option: The ankle only, broken where the rope held it; the splint runs up the leg to keep it still.
   - Option: Torn ligaments, no break; he's walking with a stick by the end of the chapter.
2. **"Coun—":** what was the guard about to call him?
   - Default: "Count"; the court is already calling him that among themselves, ahead of anything official, and it pays off later.
   - Option: "Councilor," a guard's mangle of some honor the king has in mind.
   - Option: Leave it unexplained for this book.
3. **The viewpoint:** who holds the scene once the door opens?
   - Default: The guard who made the slip, from the door to the end.
   - Option: Tor all the way through (he's alone and hurt for most of it).
   - Option: Finely, from the falcons' arrival only.
4. **Time passed:** how long has Tor been unconscious?
   - Default: A night and most of a day; he wakes the afternoon after the rescue.
   - Option: Two or three days, under a healer's sleeping draught.
5. **Who sent the falcons:** did the guards pass on Tor's talk, or did the king order them himself?
   - Default: The guards reported it up; the king sent the falcons from the palace's own roof or gates without a word, which is his way of saying yes.
   - Option: Rolph heard and arranged it.
6. **Finely:** is he one of the two door guards, or someone senior?
   - Default: A guard sergeant, senior to the door pair, who'll recur as Tor's minder in the palace.
   - Option: Just a messenger for this scene.
