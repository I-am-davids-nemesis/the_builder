# Building a River

- **Summary:** Tor spends six days in a working trance putting the field on the three bronze falcons; on the seventh he sends them out; by the tenth the sky river is running into the Ford. Facts from the user's notes; canon unless marked. The bath and the dinner with Connie moved to scene 04 (`04-lunch-with-the-queen.md`; user, November 7, 2026).
  1. **Six days.** Building the field takes six days. It's perhaps the strongest field Tor has ever made.
  2. **Looked after.** Through those days the palace staff come in to feed him and lead him to the privy.
  3. **Going under.** Each day he withdraws further into the fields he's pushing into the birds. By the end of the second day he has forgotten the pain. On the third he doesn't see anyone around him. The last two days are only locking the fields in place, with nothing in his mind but the fields.
  4. **Sleep and eat.** Afterwards he wants only to sleep and eat, then sleep and eat again. He's ready to do something only at the end of the seventh day.
  5. **Sending them out.** He gives very clear instructions on where each falcon goes:
     - **The first, far up the river**, so it gets plenty of water and everyone downstream can draw off for their irrigation canals.
     - **The second, halfway to the ocean**, to keep the water on course and in the air.
     - **The third, as close to the ocean as it can be** without being washed away.
  6. **The guards.** A party of royal guards comes to take the falcons out and rides with the riggers to each site, as a sign of how much the job matters.
  7. **Tapping.** Starting it is like the clothes dryer or the food dryer: it only has to be tapped. Nobody quite takes in that tapping it *anywhere* will do, so he tells them to tap the bird in the center of the shield it holds.
  8. **Placing them.** It takes more than a day, even with the luggage floats carrying the birds. By the tenth day a large stream of perfectly pure water is flowing into the river and down to the farms.
  9. **No flood.** It adds a good deal of water but causes no flooding. That was one of Tor's chief worries: adding too much and making more trouble than he solved. The drought had already brought the river so low that his addition only lifts it a little over normal.
  10. **The off switch.** The coast falcon goes beside an ocean fort, right at the water, so the fort's regiment guards it (user, November 7, 2026; `codex/canon-notes.md`). If the weather turns and the rains come, they can turn it off. Tor hopes they won't need to often.
  11. **Rolph** drops by every day. From the third day on, Tor doesn't see him (user, November 7, 2026).

## Beats

1. **Day one** (Hale). Tor in the chair by the window with the leg up on a stool, copper and ink put away, hands flat on the nearest falcon. He still answers when spoken to, politely, a beat late. Wincing when he shifts the leg. Rolph drops by, and Tor talks to him.
2. **Day two** (the maid; invented). She brings soup and he thanks her for it twice. Rolph comes again; Tor's answers are shorter. By evening he's stopped wincing; she notices before Hale does.
3. **Day three** (Hale). Tor doesn't see them come in. Hale and the maid feed him with a spoon and steer him to the privy and back by the elbow; he walks where he's led and goes back to the bird. Rolph comes in as usual, talks to him, gets no answer, sits a while and goes; from now on Tor doesn't see him (user, November 7, 2026). The room smells of hot metal (invented), though the bronze is cool to the touch.
4. **Day four** (the maid). Feeding him has become routine; the staff work round him like furniture. Rolph still comes daily, and Tor doesn't see him. Something in the room has changed that she can't name (invented: the air feels heavy near the birds, the way it does before a storm).
5. **Days five and six** (Tor, alone). Nothing but the fields: locking them down one by one, testing each against the others. No room, no leg, no bell. This is the scene's only section in Tor's viewpoint.
6. **Day seven** (Hale). Tor comes up out of it, stiff and starving, and sleeps; wakes, eats everything, sleeps again. Toward evening he's himself, and asks for whoever is taking the birds.
7. **Sending them out** (Hale). The royal guards and riggers come in. Tor gives his instructions: one far upriver, one halfway, one as near the sea as it can stand; they settle on putting the last beside the ocean fort, where its regiment can guard it. Tapping it anywhere works; blank faces; "tap the middle of the shield, then." The falcons float out on the luggage plates.
8. **Day ten** (Hale). Finely, back from riding out with the party, reports: all three are set, and pure water is running into the river and down to the farms. No flood; a little over normal. If the rains come, the fort's guards can turn it off. Tor asks "No flooding?" more than once, then needs a bath.
