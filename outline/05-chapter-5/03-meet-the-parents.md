# Meet the Parents

- **Summary:**
- tor goes to Rolphs house and meets his mother but not his father (just the dog)
- **POV / mode:** Rotating third (proposed): Rolph for the arrival (a secret-keeper may hold the viewpoint if it stays on action and word choice; `AGENTS.md` §3); Connie for the gifts and dinner; Tor alone at night. Camera: follows Tor in, and stays at the dinner table. (invented; pending the user's review)
- **Location:** Rolph's family home in the capital (`codex/locations/rolphs-family-home-in-the-capital.md`): in through the garden side, "where we actually live" (chapter five, scene 1), never the front. A sitting room, the great hall with the old gods on the ceiling, a guest room over the city.
- **Present:** Tor, Rolph, Connie; Rolph's younger sisters; servants; Admiral the dog. Possibly Wensa; possibly Rolph's father (see the questions).
- **Reader learns:** Connie is warm, quick and fascinated by Tor, and wants his work before anyone else's mother has it. Tor's "homemade trinkets" are worth far more than he thinks. Tor can't cope with Rolph's sisters.
- **Hidden (and how):** That the house is the palace and Rolph the prince. Hidden by **scene selection** (the garden gate, never the front; no throne room, no court) and by **Tor's own explanation**: he decides Rolph's family must be very rich merchants who live and work *beside* the palace, perhaps supplying it, which accounts for the guards and the forks. The reader sees every clue; Tor files each one under "merchants." Also hidden: how much of Connie's warmth is strategy (`rolph.md`, external tension).

## Beats

All beats (invented; pending the user's review) unless labeled.

1. **Rolph goes quiet.** In the carriage from the Thompson house, Rolph talks too much, then not at all. He straightens his collar twice. Tor notices and assumes Rolph is worried Tor will embarrass him, and promises, gravely, to talk to people.
2. **The garden gate.** They go in by a small door in a long white wall, "the back way, the front's all stairs." Guards in red and white at the door who very nearly salute Rolph. Tor decides the guards are there because the house is so near the palace.
3. **The trunks come too.** The luggage plates follow them through the garden in a line; gardeners stop work; a peacock (or a goose) objects. Tor's heavy trunk sinks into a flower bed and he apologizes to the gardener.
4. **Admiral and the stairs.** Rolph's father's dog, afraid of the stairs (scene 1), sits at the bottom of the garden steps and whines. Tor picks him up and carries him, without thinking, before anyone has said hello. (Reaction rule: small practical need → stops to help.)
5. **Connie.** She meets them on the terrace, not in a receiving room: no ceremony, gardening gloves or a book in hand, as if she just happened to be there (she didn't). She calls him **Torrance**, has questions ready from eleven letters, and listens to every answer. Tor says "Good evening," as rehearsed, and then has to keep going. (Connie, canon; the rest invented)
6. **The ornate box.** She already has the four drying fields Rolph sent for her birthday (chapter three), in the red box with the leaves, and has been using one; she tells Tor which of her friends cried. Tor is mortified that she used the good box for laundry.
7. **The trunk.** Tor opens his trunk on the terrace, apologizing: "they're only homemade." A cooling plate; a heater for the winter; a food dryer; a shield; drying fields on copper; a jug purifier. He explains each one plainly. Connie doesn't price anything aloud, but her questions get sharper: who else has one? (Nobody.) She wants new magic before anyone else's mother (`rolph.md`).
8. **The cooling plate goes up.** It's stifling, as the whole capital is. Tor asks for four nails and a hammer and puts the plate up in her sitting room himself while the servants try not to look appalled. The room goes cool. Connie sits down in it and says nothing for a long moment, which Rolph has never seen her do.
9. **The sisters.** Rolph's younger sisters arrive and play the court's game at its highest level: compliments, double meanings, mock offers (`rolph.md`; non-sexual, flirtation as a game of wit). Tor answers every line literally and sincerely, and politely declines a courtship nobody has offered. The sisters are delighted; Tor is in agony; Rolph watches and doesn't rescue him for a while. (sisters and their game, canon, user; names and number to be decided)
10. **Dinner and forks.** A long table, more forks than Tor has ever seen; he watches Rolph's hands and copies them. The great hall's ceiling of old gods: Tor looks up, counts, and says aloud that one of them has the wrong number of fingers, which Rolph warned him you're not allowed to say. Silence, then Connie laughs. (Rolph's warning, scene 1)
11. **Talking to people.** Somebody at the table asks Tor how the cooling plate works, and he forgets to be shy: one long, precise run-on about cellars and buckets. The table goes quiet to listen. Connie watches the table watching him.
12. **Connie and Rolph.** After dinner, mother and son alone, from Connie's viewpoint. She asks whether Tor knows; Rolph says he thinks they're merchants. Neither says what "it" is. Connie: "Good. Let him like us first." Rolph hears the strategy in it and isn't sure he minds (`rolph.md`, external tension). (invented; the line is the hint, nothing more)
13. **Tor alone.** A guest room larger than his family's whole house. He can't sleep in the bed; it's too big and too soft. He opens the window on the city lights and sees, close by, a great lit building he takes for the palace. He writes to his mother: the house is very nice, Rolph's mother is very kind, and he thinks they must supply the king with something, there are so many guards. Then he takes out a sheet of copper and starts on the ice box. (Tor's viewpoint: he's alone; `AGENTS.md` §3)

**Staging notes** (invented):

- **The perception gap carries the scene.** Every royal clue (guards, forks, the ceiling, the sisters' poise, Connie's ease) is visible; Tor explains each one away, sincerely and plausibly. The joke is never on his intelligence: his explanations are reasonable given what he's been told.
- **Connie should be the most skilled player in the book so far**, and genuinely warm. Both are true at once; the reader shouldn't be able to separate them.
- **Tomorrow's luncheon at the Debri house** (set up at the end of scene 2) hangs over the evening: Rolph reminds Tor of the time, and Tor sets something to wake him.
