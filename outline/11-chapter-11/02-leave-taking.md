## Beats

1. **The farewell.** They all gather at the palace for a farewell and a joint carriage ride to the city edge.
2. **Burks.** Tor sees Burks with a platoon of servants behind him, dressed even nicer than normal.
3. **The present.** Tor has prepared a present for Burks, in simple wrappings: a temperature equalization amulet, a cooling plate, a shield and a flying rig.
4. **Goodbyes.** Somehow they get through the goodbyes without Tor realizing that Burks is Green, Count Lairdgren.
5. **Two carriages.** The kids in one, the parents in another, all the luggage tied on. Everyone wears nice travelling clothes, silk shirts and leather trousers, just the thing for flying: it wouldn't do to fly in a dress.
6. **The Baroness.** Tor's chance to ask questions. First: why is Mercy a Baroness instead of a Duchess? She was born first and inherited the Barony from her parents, so she has a stronger claim to it than to the title she has by marriage.
7. **Richard.** Which leads to the revelation that Mercy was meant to marry Richard, but somehow it didn't happen. Trice isn't sure exactly what happened, but it seems to have worked out. Tor is a bit hesitant to dig into why.
8. **Rolph's knowledge bomb.** Back when the parents were young they were all at school together: Connie, Mercy, Richard, Eric, someone named Serge Glost (who is apparently someone important in the Austran government, of all places), and the count's own daughter, Laurali. Despite the arranged marriages, they fell in love in different pairs. The old king was set on forcing them to marry as promised, but Count Lairdgren decided it was a bad idea and that they should marry for love.
9. **An Ancient.** Most interesting of all: Lairdgren is actually an Ancient.
10. **What's an Ancient?** Tor and Sara have no idea what that means. They know the word, but it makes no sense to them. Sara asks.
11. **Trice explains.** It's common knowledge among nobles, not so common among merchants or commoners. Long ago a vast technological civilization ruled the world, but used its power to make the land suit whatever it desired rather than to protect it. Eventually this caused a great catastrophe, and the civilization collapsed. The world broke into regions, each run by a different philosophy: Noram had magic; Soam, harmony with nature; Austra, technology; Antar was organized around manipulating family heritage lines (user, November 10, 2026). A group of people was there to guide each civilization, amazingly long-lived through some technology or magic since lost, and named, for some reason, after colors: Red, Green, Brown, White, Gray, Black. Green is the Ancient who is supposed to rule Noram.
12. **The walls.** By this point they're outside the city walls; no more time to continue, or for Sara and Tor to ask questions.
13. **Take-off.** They all get out, load the trunks onto the luggage plates, put on their new flying rigs and shields, and take off.
14. **The new rigs.** It takes a bit to get used to them, but within a few minutes they find they fly much faster than before and are much more maneuverable.
15. **Four bells.** It takes only about four bells to fly to Two Rivers.

Silk shirts: Eric white, Mercy pigeon gray-blue, Trice black. Heather on the trousers: "Who's flying?" "Everyone, Mother."
- **The household:** about thirty, lined along the shady arcade, starched and polished beyond the dinner, the cook with a flower in his buttonhole, Hale at the end. Sara assumes it's for Tor ("he'd cooled their kitchens").
- **The present:** brown paper and the white baker's string Tor brought from home. Sara knows the contents by the corners and stops her sum at the rig. Tor: the rig is for "all the stairs," left-handed; the shield because flying needs one; a cooling plate "if you've got a room"; a new amulet because his first one (chapter 5) had "terrible" lines. He doesn't know where Burks lives.
- **Burks's reaction (the only hint):** he doesn't take it at once; takes it in both hands; his thumb rests on the knot without pulling; Sara, who reads buyers' faces, can't place the look: a man given many things over many years who can't remember the last one that was wrapped.
