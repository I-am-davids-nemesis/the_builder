## Scenes

### dinner at the Morgans
He brings fabulously expensive presents -- enough cargo shipping to start the largest national shipping business, enough dryers to make the largest preservation business, and enough flying gear to equip a small army.  The Morgans were already ready to let Tor marry their daughter, title or not, and this kind of present seems way over the top -- a grooms present doesn't normally involve gifting an entire industry.  Tor is a little embarrassed that all the equipment is a bit worthless without people to run them, but the Duke is pretty confident that he can round up plenty of people; they will have to be oath-bound to the Duke, otherwise it would be too much of a risk to give anyone a shield and flying rig.
They have a pleasant dinner, much more modest than a feast at the palace, but much more elaborate than anything Tor had before coming to the capital.  There is a bit about Tor not drinking wine, and why, and then they give him some apple juice, which he really appreciates.  Turns out that it is a blend of multiple apples; the same kind of technique they use for cider, but with less sweet apples since there is no need for fermentation.

Rolph, it turns out, has a writ from the king signed by three counts promoting Tor to Knight Esquire.  They hold a mini ceremony in the dining room after dinner.  It turns out that there were a line of nobility willing to sign the papers; the ones that ended up signing are pretty impressive: Tovey, of course, as he would do anything to make these two happy; Ford, obviously, given how much Tor has done for the county; and, most specially, Count Lairdgren, Tor's own count.  Rolph said he flew in specially to sign the papers.  He didn't even know that the count had flying gear.  Rolph suggests that Tor would like the count -- a relatively short fellow, just a few inches taller than Tor, and they look surprisingly similar.  Something to talk about later.
This makes the marriage technically allowable, even if the Morgans were prepared to ignore the normal rules before.
Turns out that Rolph is a knight already, and has been since he was 13, but this is different because Tor earned it and did not inherit the title.

Tor is somewhat overwhelmed and is having a hard time figuring out all the planning, but Mercy interrupts and demands that she and Tor's mother be the ones to plan the wedding.  So, they agree that they should accompany the kids back to school, so that they can stop off at Tor's house and meet his family.

They plan to leave in two days.
Tor takes the time to build even more equipment for the Morgan's -- 50 more flying rigs, 100 new shields to go with them, and 20 temperature control plates.  He probably would have made more, but he ran out of time.

### leave taking
They all gather at the palace for a farewell, and a joint carriage ride to the city edge.  Tor sees Burks with a platoon of servants behind him, dressed even nicer than normal.  He has prepared a present for Burks, in simple wrappings, but a temperature equalization amulet, a cooling plate, a shield, and a flying rig.  Somehow they get through the goodbyes without Tor realizing that Burks is Green/Count Lairdgren.

They leave in a couple of different carriages -- the kids in one, and the parents in another, with all of the luggage tied on to the carriages.  Everyone wore nice traveling clothes -- silk shirts and leather trousers -- just the thing for flying, as it wouldn't do to fly in a dress.
