# Dinner at the Morgans

- **When:** the evening of the Morgan dinner, straight on from chapter 10, scene 5 (Duke Eric taking them in to dinner), then the two days before they leave.
- **Summary:** Tor's presents (cargo floats, food dryers, flying rigs) amount to whole industries, far beyond any groom's gift; the Morgans had already accepted him, title or not. Dinner is modest by palace standards and lavish by Tor's; he doesn't drink wine, and they give him a blended apple juice he loves. After dinner Rolph produces a writ from the king, signed by three counts, making Tor a Knight Esquire, and they hold a small ceremony in the dining room. The marriage is now technically allowable. ## Beats

1. **The presents.** Tor's gifts are fabulously expensive: enough cargo shipping to start the largest national shipping business, enough dryers for the largest preservation business, enough flying gear to equip a small army. The Morgans were already ready to let Tor marry their daughter, title or not; a groom's present doesn't normally involve gifting an entire industry.
2. **People to run them.** Tor is a little embarrassed that the equipment is a bit worthless without people to run it. The Duke is confident he can round up plenty, but they'll have to be oath-bound to him; anything less would be too much of a risk with a shield and flying rig.
3. **Dinner.** Pleasant, much more modest than a feast at the palace, much more elaborate than anything Tor had before coming to the capital.
4. **No wine.** A bit about Tor not drinking wine, and why: it clouds the mind he builds with (codex reason). At this table he says so plainly, or nearly (user, November 11, 2026). They give him apple juice instead, which he really appreciates.
5. **The blend.** It's a blend of several apples: the same technique they use for cider, but with less sweet apples, since there's no need for fermentation.
6. **The writ.** Rolph has a writ from the king, signed by three counts, promoting Tor to Knight Esquire. They hold a mini ceremony in the dining room after dinner.
7. **The signers.** There was a line of nobles willing to sign; the ones who did are impressive: Tovey, of course, who'd do anything to make these two happy; Ford, given how much Tor has done for the county; and, most specially, Count Lairdgren, Tor's own count.
8. **The count.** Rolph says the count flew in specially to sign; Rolph didn't even know he had flying gear. He suggests Tor would like him: a relatively short fellow, just a few inches taller than Tor, and they look surprisingly similar. Something to talk about later.
9. **Allowable.** This makes the marriage technically allowable, even though the Morgans were prepared to ignore the normal rules before.
10. **Rolph's knighthood.** Rolph has been a knight since he was 13, but this is different: Tor earned it and didn't inherit it.
11. **The planning.** Tor is somewhat overwhelmed and having a hard time with all the planning. Mercy interrupts and demands that she and Tor's mother plan the wedding.
12. **Travelling together.** They agree the parents should accompany the kids back to school, stopping at Tor's house to meet his family. They'll leave in two days.
`* * *`

13. **Two days' work** (Hale, in the orangery). Tor uses the time to build more for the Morgans: 50 more flying rigs, 100 new shields to go with them, and 20 temperature control plates. He'd have made more, but he ran out of time. The 50 rigs are the fifty left lying in his palace room (chapter 10, scene 5), now given to the Morgans; the 100 shields and 20 plates are newly built (user, November 11, 2026). The hundred undecided cargo floats stay in his room (invented).

## Draft notes

Drafted November 11, 2026: Mercy from the chest to the end of dinner (with `* * *` breaks inside her part: chest, dinner, writ, ceremony, signers, planning), then Hale. Inventions (pending review):

- **The chest opened before dinner:** Eric can't walk past a closed box. Mercy adds it up as she was taught at her father's quarter days: fifty rigs is "a regiment" (about a hundred and twenty in the kingdom, all spoken for), the floats a carrying trade nobody owns, the dryers the Debris' whole year. Eric's men will swear "on the stone in the chapel, the old way": "I'll not hand that to anyone I couldn't hang." Mercy decided at Connie's breakfast (chapter 10, scene 4) to accept Tor, title or not; Eric: "The little fellow with the leg? Good."
- **The dining room:** too small for Mercy's grandmother's hall table; a darn in the good cloth under the salt. Dinner: leek soup, barony trout, saddle of mutton with red currant sauce, new potatoes, carrots, poached pears, cheese. Tor: "Mum would boil it."
- **The wine:** the barony's south-slope red, the last cask. Tor covers his glass: "it makes my head go soft… I need it, my head, for building." Trice: "It sounds like you." Rolph lost that argument long ago.
- **The juice:** four apples (two sharps from the top orchard, a russet, "the old yellow"); Tor tastes the blend and asks about pressing; Mercy talks apples through the fish and enjoys it.
- **The writ:** brought out after the cloth is drawn (no business at table, `codex/canon-notes.md`). Rolph has Eric read it ("If I do, I'll laugh"). It names three deeds: the Ford water, the midden children, and "works of his own invention"; Trice spots that the third covers the masterwork without choosing one. The king: "tell him to pick one." Tor: "But I didn't *ask*." Rolph: "That's rather the point of you." Text of the writ is ours.
- **The ceremony:** Eric's grandfather's sword from over the sideboard; Rolph dubs Tor; Tor insists on kneeling, on the good knee, the splinted leg out on the footstool. Rolph: knight since thirteen, "it'd come with the rest of me. Like the ears… You did three. Four. I've lost count." "Get up, Sir Tor." Mercy: Sir Barnaby was made in a chair (he had the gout).
- **The signers:** a queue of nobles; the king chose three. Tovey; Count Ford, who signed so hard he went through the paper; Count Lairdgren's seal in **green** wax. Lairdgren flew in from the north two evenings before, signed, had a glass with the king, left. Tor: nobody at home has seen him; "Mum says he's very old."
- **The hint:** Mercy and Eric exchange a small look at Rolph's description; Trice sees it and files it; the boys don't. Mercy knows the king's seal from her marriage lines and "a letter she'd burned" (her broken match, unexplained; scene 02).
- **Planning:** Tor spirals (banns, the Elder at home, Mum thinks he's doing his accounts, the post takes a fortnight, the bakery before harvest, Teira); Mercy sets down her glass as she taught Connie to: "A wedding is the one thing in the world that isn't any business of the people getting married." Trice sets the two days (Tovey to come up, luggage plates, rooms aired at school) while folding her napkin under the table to a sugar lump. Mercy will go by carriage "like a grown woman"; the flight question is left to scene 02.
- **Hale:** Tor decides on the fifty rigs the night he's back ("They'll need them"), sleeps on them; Hale carries them to the orangery himself, counting. Copper appears overnight. Shields copied from his head (sixty-three by the first dark); the plates take a short trance on the second morning (Hale stands inside the door for it), then nineteen copies. The plates are for the barony's fruit sheds (invented reason). Tor's left hand tilts toward the carriage idea; "Next time." Hale will see them off.
