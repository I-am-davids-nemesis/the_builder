# Home Sweet Home

- **When:** arrival at Two Rivers, straight on from scene 02's four-hour flight. Scene 02 ends at noon with the party hovering over the village before landing: Tor stops in the air, "That's it. That's home." The landing belongs to this scene. Scene 02 also sketched the village from above (two rivers meeting, a square chapel tower, a green, the bakery chimney smoking too much for noon); invented, and still an open question in scene 02's outline.
- **Summary:** They haven't even finished landing when the mayor comes out to meet them, speaking the local dialect, and Tor has to translate both ways. The mayor recognizes Tor and wonders if he's working for the lords as their personal baker; Tor laughs and introduces his friends, and, saving the best for last, his fiancée Patricia. At the bakery three of his younger siblings burst out to hug him, then go wary at the strangers; his father comes out in his apron, and, since Tor's mother can't leave her berry pies, offers cider at an outdoor table. In the cellar, his father teases Tor about the "merchant girl" and asks only whether he loves her: love makes odd matches, he says; he married Tor's mother, after all. Tor's mother comes out, hugs him, and walks toward the table, looking younger and more regal with every step. Duke Eric stares, stands: "Laurie?" "Hello Eric. Mercy. How have you been?" Mercy runs to hug her: "We all thought you were dead!" End of chapter, on the cliffhanger. From the writer's chapter notes (user, November 10, 2026); canon unless marked.
- **POV / mode:** rotating third (user, November 12, 2026): **Trice** for the landing, the yard and the table, since she is meeting her future family, knows her parents' faces and heard Laurali named in scene 02; a `* * *` break to **Tor's father** for the cellar, the one stretch Trice can't witness; back to Trice for the ending. Tor isn't alone, and the reunion isn't the kind of trauma that gives him the viewpoint (AGENTS.md §3). Tor's mother, as the count's daughter, is not an Ancient, but she keeps the viewpoint closed anyway: her secret is the chapter's hook.
- **Location:** Two Rivers (`codex/locations/two-rivers.md`): the landing on the green, the walk to the bakery, which faces the green (user, November 12, 2026), the bakery door and yard, the cellar under the house (canon: warm in winter, cool in summer, the butter kept there), and an outdoor picnic table (user). The place squeezes Tor's past (§6). Scene 02's picture from the air stands (user, November 12, 2026).
- **Present:**
  - **Landing:** Tor; Rolph; Trice; Sara; Mercy, Duke Eric and Tovey (user, November 11, 2026); the mayor, **Tom** (user, November 12, 2026).
  - **Bakery:** the above, less the mayor, who leaves them at the bakery to spread the news (user, November 12, 2026); the twins **Terry** and **Tara**, and **Taman** ("Tam"), the youngest of all the children (user, November 12, 2026); Tor's father, **Alan** (user, November 12, 2026); Tor's mother: **Lora** in the village, though almost everyone calls her Mistress Baker or Mother Baker, and Alan just calls her **Lor**; "Laurie" to Mercy and Eric (user, November 12, 2026). The rest of Tor's siblings are at work or on errands round the village, and turn up in chapter 12 (user, November 12, 2026).
  - **Cellar:** Tor and his father only.
- **Reader learns:**
  - Two Rivers speaks its own dialect, fluid enough that the nobles can't follow it, and Tor translates both ways (user).
  - Tor's family is real, warm and busy; the bakery runs at noon (user).
  - Tor introduces Rolph as "my roommate," and Mercy by her barony (user's notes: "the Morgans (Duke and Baroness), Count Tovey, and his roommate Rolph, as well as merchant Sara"). He leaves out the prince out of habit and to spare the mayor; Rolph is delighted to be nobody for once (user, November 12, 2026).
  - Tor's father asks only whether he loves her; Tor does, and says the marriage was Trice's idea (user).
  - "Love can make odd matches": his father married Tor's mother, a hint that something strange happened there (user).
  - Tor's mother is **Laurie**, known to Mercy and Eric from their youth, and believed dead (user). She is the count's daughter **Laurali** (user, November 12, 2026); nobody says so in this scene. The reader, who heard in scene 02 that Laurali was at school with the parents and that her story was "another story," makes the link from the name.
  - She can change how she looks: as she walks to the table she drops twenty years and the village bearing together, and people can see it (user, November 12, 2026).
- **Hidden (and how):**
  - **Why Laurie left, and why everyone thought her dead:** the chapter ends on Mercy's cry; the scene cuts before any answer.
  - **That Tor is the count's grandson:** the reader can infer it from the name; Tor, Trice and the other students learn it, and the reader has it confirmed, in chapter 12 (user, November 12, 2026).
  - **Tor's clone secret** (`codex/characters/supporting/green.md`): Tor is both grandson and clone (user, November 12, 2026). The clone part isn't part of the plot until later books; "grandson" is enough for now, and covers it.
  - **How Laurie looks younger:** she is extremely long-lived, as the daughter of an Ancient, and can subtly control her features; she has been slowly aging herself to keep pace with her husband (user, November 12, 2026). Not talked about on the page; seen only as the change.
  - **How much Tor's father knows:** everything; he has always known who she is (user, November 12, 2026). Shown only by his one line in the cellar, said lightly to a son who misses it. In his viewpoint section the narration stays on explicit action and word choice, so his knowledge shows without being stated (AGENTS.md §3, secret-keeper rule).
  - **Why Eric takes minutes to know her:** she was presumed dead and looks like a village baker's wife until she changes; Eric is facing the bakery and Mercy has her back to it (user, November 12, 2026).

## Beats

1. **The landing.** They haven't even finished landing when the mayor comes out to meet them. Nobody in Two Rivers has ever seen a noble (canon); seven people and ten trunks come down out of the sky onto the green (invented detail). He greets them in the local dialect. (user)
2. **The dialect.** The dialect is fluid, constantly changing with who is talking, their mood, and who they're talking to. There is supposed to be a linguistic underpinning that makes it a better base for learning other languages; Tor is smart and adaptable enough that this isn't really apparent from his abilities, but perhaps it helped a bit (it fits his shifting accents, `codex/characters/tor.md`). The first meetings should be a bit awkward in dialect, and Tor has to translate. (user)
3. **"Lords and Ladies."** Tor translates the mayor: "Welcome Lords and Ladies to Two Rivers. We are happy to have you." (user)
4. **The personal baker.** The mayor stares at Tor and suddenly recognizes him, and wonders if he's working for the lords as their personal baker. (user)
5. **Introductions.** Tor laughs, and says they're his friends from school and, if the mayor can believe it, his fiancée and her parents. He introduces the Morgans (Duke and Baroness), Count Tovey, his roommate Rolph, and merchant Sara; and then, saving the best for last, his fiancée Patricia. (user)
6. **Both ways.** He has to translate it all back to the nobles, and tell Tom he's looking for his parents, to introduce them to the Morgans. (user) Tom leaves them at the bakery and goes to spread the news (user, November 12, 2026).
7. **The luggage.** They head over to the bakery and make a pile of their luggage. (user) The luggage plates set the trunks down by the door (invented).
8. **The children.** Just before Tor opens the door, three kids burst out and grab him in a big hug: his brother Terry, his sister Tara (the twins), and his youngest sister, Taman ("Tam"). (user) Tor is still on his stick with his leg splinted (scene 02), so the hug nearly knocks him over (invented).
9. **Wary.** As soon as they see the strangers, they pull back, suddenly wary, wondering who they are. (user)
10. **Father.** Tor starts to explain, but his father comes out, wearing his apron, with a bit of flour over his clothes. Tor introduces his friends and his fiancée's family. (user)
11. **The pies.** His mother is just finishing some berry pies, and can't come out yet or they'll burn. His father, everything in a thick accent, offers cider and leads everyone to an outdoor picnic table, and he and Tor go to fetch the cider from the cellar. (user) His father carries the cider; Tor, on his stick, brings the glasses on a luggage plate, which his father eyes and doesn't remark on (user, November 12, 2026).
12. **The merchant girl.** *(Viewpoint: Tor's father, after a `* * *` break.)* While they get the drinks, his father teases him about bringing home a merchant girl from the city to marry. Tor denies she's a merchant: she's a *ducharina*. He asks his father not to tell Trice, or her parents, that she shouldn't marry a simple country boy like him (user's note reads "not to tell them not to marry"; settled, user, November 12, 2026). His father just laughs, and asks only whether he loves her. (user)
13. **Trees.** Tor says yes. Her name fits in well there, since *Trice* sounds a bit like *trees* in the local dialect. He's friends with her, and he loves her enough to marry her. She's the one who had the idea, but it seems pretty great to Tor. (user)
14. **Odd matches.** His father agrees that love can make odd matches: he's married to Tor's mother, after all. A hint that something strange happened there. (user) He has always known who she is, and says it lightly; Tor misses it (user, November 12, 2026).
15. **Mother.** *(Viewpoint: Trice again, after a `* * *` break.)* They bring the cider and glasses to the table, and Tor ends up standing between the bakery and the table, so he's the first person his mother sees when she comes out. She hugs him and tells him the family has been missing him. (user)
16. **The walk.** Tor starts to introduce her to his friends, but she looks over at the table and starts walking toward it, and as she walks she seems to grow visibly younger and more regal. (user) Mostly bearing, seen by Trice: the apron comes off, the village stoop drops, and she changes from a village baker's wife into a countess. Under it, a little magic: she lets go of the age she has been slowly putting on to keep pace with her husband, and people can see it, though nobody names it (user, November 12, 2026).
17. **"Laurie?"** Duke Eric looks at Tor's mother oddly for a few minutes, then stands. "Laurie?" (user) He's facing the bakery; Mercy has her back to it (user, November 12, 2026). The village knows her as Lora, mostly says Mistress Baker or Mother Baker, and Alan calls her Lor; Tor doesn't connect "Laurie" with his mother, so the name is the first sign to Tor that the Morgans know her (user, November 12, 2026).
18. **"Hello Eric."** She replies: "Hello Eric. Mercy. How have you been?" (user) She says it in clean Hill speech, out of the dialect: the second sign, and the one that tells Tor something is wrong (invented; accepted by the user, November 12, 2026).
19. **"We all thought you were dead!"** Mercy cries "Laurie!", runs over to hug her, and says, "We all thought you were dead!" (user)
20. **Cut.** End of chapter, on the cliffhanger. (user)

## Draft notes

Drafted November 12, 2026: Trice, then Alan (cellar), then Trice, with `* * *` breaks. Inventions (pending review):

- **The village from the ground:** see `codex/locations/two-rivers.md` (twenty-six roofs, the chapel's open bell frame and crack, the walnut tree, the green door, the cellar). The cow watches the landing and goes back to eating; Trice respects it.
- **Tom:** a cooper, mallet in hand, which he hides behind his back; bows "in sections." He apologizes for the cow (Tor's translation). Leaves shouting the news house by house; by the chapel there are faces in every window and a boy on the pump.
- **The dialect, through Trice:** she has four accents of Noram (Hill, court, river cant from Sara, the northern stewards' speech) and catches only *lords*, *ladies* and names. She notices it changes with the listener: upright for the duke, warm and leaning for Tor. Tor speaks it "as if it had been waiting behind his teeth all summer." Alan's is thick and slow, "all the corners worn off."
- **Introductions:** Tor remembers "Baroness." There's no village word for prince except the hymns' word for the king, so Rolph is "my roommate"; Rolph shakes Tom's hand, "Just Rolph. Roommate. Delighted"; Tom says he's very big. Trice asks "And me?"; Tor: "I told him last. On purpose… Because you were the best part." Tom's eyebrows at the word for fiancée.
- **Eric:** "Could we? Hire him? For the bread?" "Eric." Shakes Alan's floury hand anyway and leaves the print on his cuff.
- **Trice's shame:** the moon on the privy door brings back the Gilder's Row house she was ashamed of (chapter 7).
- **The children:** they hit Tor at once; he tips back over the doorstep and Rolph catches his collar without appearing to move. The twins, at twelve, already reach Tor's shoulder (a quiet hint that Tor isn't built like his family). Tam, purple-smeared, hides behind his good leg.
- **The cellar (Alan):** the plate waits on the third step "as though it had been told to"; the good glasses have green in the foot. Alan: "he'll go to that school and come back with something, and it won't be learning." His hints, all said or done, none narrated as knowledge: he shows no reaction to the Morgans' names; he laughs for a long time at Tor's plea and says "That's not mine to tell her"; "Married your mother, didn't I"; "Your mother'll want to *see* them," not meet.
- **The ending:** Laurie hands Tor her folded apron without looking, and he holds it through the rest. Trice judges her fifty at the door, forty halfway, under thirty at the table, and first blames the shade. Eric looks "the way a man's face goes when he reads a letter in a hand he knows." Tor's polite laugh, about to say "No, that's my mother." Her Hill speech has a lift at the end of *been* that Trice has only heard from her mother's oldest friends. Mercy knocks over the cider jug and runs, "who had not run anywhere in Trice's whole life." The scene ends on her line.
