- **Summary:** Tor is seated a ways down from the king and queen, beside Varley, properly Veronica. The court around them brings him its problems (a woman's damp, moldy house) and its flirting, which Varley heads off: all proposals go through her mother, and her mother has already turned one down. Dessert is shaved ice too cold to melt, and the queen seems to thank him for it. Then a border argument flares, and Duke Winchester, in a combat rage, strangles Lady Priscilla while everyone backs away. Tor drops his stick, punches, then kicks, and keeps the duke on himself for twenty minutes until the rage runs out. Sure he'll be executed, he jokes, hands Rolph his shield and limps into a side garden. There a royal guard asks whether he means to pursue it; Rolph settles it, Connie brings Priscilla's thanks, and Rolph explains: Tor wears the king's crest, so what he did the king did; everyone saw Tor go into a rage of his own; and nobody, not even a duke with 20,000 men, wants Tor as an enemy. Connie sends him back in to show he's in control. The duke kneels for forgiveness; Tor gives it as he did to Tovey. Count Ford thanks him for the river and the crops. Trice steers him through two more hours of introductions, reminds him she hasn't forgotten his "station," and blushes when he kisses her hand. Burks undresses him and he sleeps. ## Beats

1. **Seated** (Varley). Tor is seated at the table a ways down from the king and queen, and finds he's beside Karina's sister, Varley, or more properly Veronica. She looks familiar, but the nobles all seem to be related to one degree or another, so that isn't surprising, and she was in his room the other day (chapter 9, scene 2; see Questions).
2. **Introductions.** They do a "formal" introduction: Tor says his name is Torrance, but his friends call him Tor.
3. **The plates.** Varley is happy to talk to him, since it's his fault the summer is bearable: he gave her two cooling plates. She confesses her sister almost didn't give them to her, but realized they were so good she didn't even need more than two.
4. **The warning.** She warns Tor about her sister: Karina will likely just try to use him for more magic trinkets.
5. **Doretta.** She's very friendly, and Tor, remembering the story of Doretta (chapter 8, scene 1), realizes he has to be friendly back, and not distracted and aloof, as he apparently comes across.
6. **School.** So he talks to her of school. She goes to school at the palace; they bring in tutors for her.
7. **Flying.** She wants to learn to fly. The council hasn't forbidden her to fly as it has the king, but there aren't enough rigs after the military; nobody else is getting any. That's why everyone is so envious of the gifts he gave the Morgans. Then a little light flirting, to see if he has any extra he could give her.
8. **The left-hand rig.** Tor admits he has no more, but says he needs to make some new ones, steered more by the left hand than the right, so you could use your right hand for a weapon of some sort. He makes a lot of hand gestures as he says it, and the people around him can clearly hear him and understand he's talking about flying rigs.
9. **Earmarked.** Varley promises to pay for the rig. Tor thinks that's ridiculous, since she's his best friend's little sister, and promises to earmark one for her. He also promises her and her sister shields, since he has extra of those, and she'll need one if she's going to fly.
10. **Gossip.** Varley keeps flirting, moves closer, and tells him court gossip.
11. **Demanding, not using.** Tor thinks Karina certainly isn't using him; she's just demanding things from him, which he sees as a separate kind of interaction. He hopes Varley doesn't start acting like that with him; that would be disappointing.
12. **Courses.** The meal has lots of courses, and he and Varley both eat only bits of each, being much smaller than the average noble: he because he's a commoner, she because she's still young.
13. **Varley at work.** Varley is skilled at dinner conversation and makes a point of talking with each person around them. A lot of people want to talk to Tor, and a lot of them want to talk about their problems.
14. **The mildew.** One woman has mildew and mold in her home because it's far too damp. Tor realizes it would be simple to dry the air, even easier than the drying field or the river fields, since it's only a little water in the air. He declines to do anything at once, but says he'll definitely work on it and send her something. She tries to pass it off; Tor insists she send him a note about it so he can be sure to get it to her.
15. **Flirting.** Some of the other women around him flirt, which makes him very uncomfortable: he doesn't know them, and they're much older or married.
16. **Through her mother.** Varley helps a bit: all proposals have to go through her mother (her brother made that a rule), and her mother has a high bar and has already turned down her older daughter.
17. **Playing along.** Tor is flabbergasted, but plays along, as if the queen were the proper person to manage offers of marriage for a country boy.
18. **Dessert.** It finally arrives: shaved ice so cold it isn't melting, so fine it melts on the tongue. The queen looks down the table at him and seems to be thanking him for it. Tor is sure he had no hand in it, though maybe his new cold box helped a bit (chapter 9, scene 5).
19. **The border.** After dessert, another shouting match erupts between two men seated near each other. Tor is too far away to hear exactly, but it sounds like a border dispute: where a county line is drawn, and what's on each side of it.
20. **The strangling.** Tor wants to ignore it, but then one man grabs the woman with the other man and starts strangling her. Tor is shocked nobody intervenes, but he gets the feeling at least one of them is in a combat rage, so the people backing away are likely protecting themselves from the combat aura, whatever this one is.
21. **Him, then.** If anyone is going to save the woman, it will have to be him. He stumbles over; she's already turning blue. He drops his walking stick, so he won't have a weapon, makes sure his shield is on, and hits the man in the kidney as hard as he can, which isn't hard enough for the man to notice.
22. **Again.** Tor thinks this will probably get him executed, but tries again, because he has to do something to save her.
23. **The kick.** Finally he kicks the man (awkward, with one leg in a splint). The man drops the woman, turns and tries to kill him instead. Tor has to goad him a few times to keep his attention, and the man finally runs out of steam after about twenty minutes.
24. **Coming to.** The man seems suddenly to come to, and asks Tor if he's all right: he was discussing border issues with Baron Rochester, and the next thing he knew he was hitting Tor.

`* * *`

25. **The joke** (Rolph). Tor, sure he's going to be killed since everyone saw what he did, makes a joke: they got into a fight over a girl, and better yet, he got the girl in the end (see Questions). He smiles at the man, who looks confused and like a killer headache is coming on.
26. **The shield to Rolph.** Rolph has walked up beside Tor; Tor sighs and hands his shield over. After a couple of minutes of nothing happening, he realizes his punishment will likely come after the party, or at least not in front of the guests.
27. **The garden.** He limps out to a side garden; Rolph follows. Two royal guards come out too, looking a bit nervous.
28. **"Something you need?"** Rolph asks if there's something he needs, still holding Tor's shield amulet uncertainly.
29. **The guard's question.** The guard asks Tor if he intends to pursue this further. Tor doesn't understand. The guard needs to know whether he's satisfied or there will be further conflict with the duke; the king's forces will need to know how to respond.
30. **Rolph settles it.** Rolph intervenes. The user's draft of the speech, kept as notes for its content (see Questions on wording and voice): "Oh. I see... This issue is over as far as Tor is concerned, I believe... It was never personal on his part, and he only sought to protect Lady Priscilla when he saw her attacked. I do not believe that Duke Winchester means harm toward Tor either, or Lady Priscilla. This was just a potential disaster thankfully avoided. These things happen. Do let us know if the Duke changes his mind, however. I don't like his chances if he decides to go that route against Tor, but that's up to him, as his honor demands. Still, I know him to normally be cool headed and just, so expect this to be over in truth now." He hands the amulet back to Tor and motions him to put it on.
31. **Shaking.** Tor is shaking with fear.
32. **Connie.** Connie comes out after a bit and hugs Tor. Lady Priscilla asked her to thank him: luckily her natural shield came on enough to keep her alive until Tor came and distracted the duke. Connie commends his bravery.
33. **"Why haven't I been arrested?"** Tor, still confused, asks.
34. **The crest.** Rolph asks if he's joking: he's still wearing the king's crest over his heart. That means Tor wasn't the one doing anything; as far as everyone is concerned, it was the king. Which explains the guard's question. A good thing Tor doesn't hold grudges.
35. **The rage they saw.** Does Tor understand? People saw him go into a battle rage himself, and worried he wouldn't recover at all (that can happen the first couple of times). And the duke hasn't the manpower, or the magical help Tor has or could give the king's army; even his army of 20,000 men would be no match for Tor if he were determined.
36. **The shield off.** Rolph and Connie are a bit confused about why Tor turned his shield off. Tor is confused again: he felt as if he had a shield on. It worked just like his normal one; he could feel the field and how the forces moved round him, draining into the ground.
37. **Back in.** Tor just wants to go back to his room, if he's still welcome in the house. The queen: of course he's welcome, but he has to go back to the party to show he's in control of himself. It will terrify those smart enough to understand what's happening. So he has to go in, smile, and pretend nothing happened.
38. **The hush.** He walks back in and the room goes quiet. At once several people converge on him: the duke he just fought, Tovey, and another big man, even taller than the king. They're much faster than Tor, hobbling on his splinted leg.
39. **The knee.** The duke reaches Tor first and, instead of resuming the fight, goes down on one knee and asks forgiveness for the rage and for attacking him.
40. **No debts between friends.** Tor bows and tries what he did with Tovey (chapter 3): it was nothing, and should be nothing, since they could be friends, and there are no debts between friends, as long as he's asked forgiveness of the person who was actually hurt.
41. **To Priscilla.** The duke thanks him, rises, and goes to see to that directly.
42. **Count Ford.** The big man looks satisfied and holds out his hand to shake Tor's. He's Count Ford, and wants to thank Tor for all his help: the river already made, and, as he heard from the queen, Tor's plan to make up some of the lost crops by giving away more of his own work, all without asking anything of the count (chapter 9, scene 4). If there's anything he or his people can do for Tor, he's at Tor's service.
43. **The king's river.** Tor tries to turn the praise to the king; he's just happy he could play a small part.

`* * *`

44. **The rounds** (Trice). Tor has to stay another couple of hours making the rounds and being introduced. Luckily Trice is beside him, keeping track of everyone, since Tor is certainly not going to remember them all.
45. **Leave to go.** The queen finally indicates he may leave.
46. **His station.** Walking toward the door, Trice tells him she hasn't forgotten what they talked about earlier (scene 01): they just need to get Tor an improvement in his station.
47. **The hand.** Tor blushes, but tries to be courtly and gallant, bends over Trice's hand and kisses it. It's Trice's turn to blush, and she walks away, leaving Tor on his own.

`* * *`

48. **Burks** (Hale). Back in his room, Tor has to have Burks help him undress. Then he falls asleep, too tired even to dream.
