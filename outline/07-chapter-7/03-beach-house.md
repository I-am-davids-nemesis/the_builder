# Beach House

- **Summary:**
- The next morning, they take the requisite carriage ride out to the edge of the city, just beyond the wall. they arranged to meet the girls at the city gate, so they didn't have long to wait before the four of them were back together and ready to leave.  Tor realized that Trice was a bit embarrassed by her house in the city -- not nearly as grand as Tovey's place, or even Debri house, and really didn't want them to pick her up.  Tor realized that this was a kind of social embarrassment that he would just never understand.
- it took less than two hours to get to the beach once they got into the air.  Rolph had to hunt a little bit on the coast before he found a landmark that led them directly to the sand in front of the house.  While a royal retreat, it turns out not to be as grand as Tor was expecting after experiencing the capital.  It has a lot of land around it, and the nearest village is quite a trek away, but the house itself is only big enough for a dozen or so people to stay in.  It was nice enough, but fit in with Tor's feeling of what a nice house should be; he wondered if he could just stay there for the rest of the summer break -- far away from the crowds and the people problems of the capital, it almost seemed ideal to him.
- The first thing they did was go down to the beach -- this made sense to Tor since that was why he came here.  Once on the beach, the true scale of the ocean finally hit him.  When flying it, it was just color on the ground far below, but standing on the beach and hearing the waves crash on the shore and feeling the wind blow salty air through his hair, he could finally feel the immense vastness of the ocean.  he could walk up to the water, feeling the spray from waves crashing on rocks wash over him, and taste the salt in the water -- so much salt that he could feel it start to crust over everything -- at least that is what the fields felt like, even if the others were just happy to splash through the revenants of the waves as they washed back out, skipping back as new waves came crashing in.
- It was one thing to hear about it in school, but spending his whole life in the forests of Two Rivers, the only thing he had experienced was small lakes, tiny rivers, and ponds like the ones at school or in the palace grounds.  The experience of taking in the whole ocean was almost overwhelming.
- While the others played in the water on the beach, Tor got to work, trying to understand the ocean on a deep level to feel the field of that much water, that much salt.  He was so far under, in his trance, that he didn't even notice Trice coming up and dumping a full bucket of water over his head, until he started choking and coughing up the water.  On the plus side, it did give him a feeling for what dissolved salt in water felt and tasted like.  He already knew what pure water was, since he had that several times a day; although now that he thought about it, he could remember that there was some minor things about the contents of the water he drank that was a little bit similar to the sea water.
- he sat with his eyes closed for a couple more hours, and eventually he had the field firmly imprinted on a couple of small copper plates, both the stream in the air, as well as the purification -- this one a much stronger, focused field, that kept all of salt, and all of the tiny animals and plants, out of the stream in the first place. He had to concentrate extra hard because he could feel the idea of stone and sand that he also wanted to keep out of the stream.  But finally, he was done, happy with his new device, even though he thought it would only last a few months.  It was firmly planted into two copper plates, and he took the them out and placed them on the beach so the there was a great curving stream of water a foot thick arcing out over the beach, and back into the ocean.
- he tried putting his hand in the stream and it was quickly pushed out, with a little splash of water.  Rolph and the girls find this to be a great sport, splashing each other with the pure clean water.  The first part was just sticking their hands in to splash water into their mouths, to quench their thirst, but then Rolph uses his massive size and strength to send great waves of water out, soaking the girls.  Sara, the target of a lot of Rolph's splashing, gets a bit fed up with this, and cleverly goes over to one of the copper squares and turns off the device.
- Immediately all of the water falls out of the air and crashes down on the beach.  There was enough water in the air, to create great rivulets that washed away huge trenches of sand as they flowed back to the ocean.  The trio laugh as they watch the water fall.
- Tor immediately sees the problem and becomes terrified.  He thinks that he was just about to put a giant river hundreds of miles across land, where anyone could just walk up and turn it off causing an environmental catastrophe.  It takes him a couple of minutes to calm down enough explain the problem to the others.
- Rolph's nervously chuckles and congratulates Sara for saving part of the kingdom.  This is a great win, and probably the most productive play of the year.  But, now that they had pointed out the tiny flaw to Tor, he would figure it out and fix it.
- This helps Tor immensely -- fix the error and move on, don't focus on the negative what-ifs. Rolph reminds Tor of something he said once: everything has flaws, its just a matter of finding them and fixing them if you can.
- They finally go back to the house, and pull out the basket that Ada had given Rolph before they left that morning.  They realize that it isn't nearly enough for the full group for the evening and the next morning before they have to go back.  Tor laughs at them, finding something where he is clearly better at then they are (finally) -- he can cook, and bake, and he can go to a pantry and find things that can be made into food given a bit of time and effort.  The problem with the nobles is that they have never needed to think about making themselves food, and they are useless without prepared food.  Tor enlists their help, what little they can provide, to turn some flour, baking soda, and salt into biscuits - they have to build up a fire for this, and the nobles try to help with that, but find they are also a bit at a loss there too -- not knowing the difference between green wood, and dry wood; sticks that will burn up in a minute and real logs that will give heat for hours.
- Eventually, they get enough food for the night, and Tor plans a different combination for breakfast.
- Rolph looks around with a hopeful look on his face, and asks what's next on the agenda.  Trice throws a couch pillow at him and reminds him that there are other things that they can do while here -- homework from school, and other stuff (She doesn't go into details, because there are spy assignments that would be perfect now that they have the flying rigs, and the cover of being on a distant vacation -- they can get to places in just a couple of hours that no one would expect them to be at, and they could get away before being noticed.  It is one of the major summer homework activities that all the students at the 'special' school are expected to accomplish, and now is the perfect opportunity.  They don't want to let Tor in on the work because he is a builder student; Rolph is also not technically part of the special school, but does have a lot of the same training, being heir to the throne).
- Trice and Sara, giggle and go off to their room; Rolph sighs, realizing that he has to make sure Tor gets lost in his building project again so that he can sneak out without Tor noticing.  Tor gets entirely the wrong idea about what's happening, afraid that it is some depraved noble activity; Rolph assures him that it is not, and starts asking him about the building project to get him thinking about work and falling into a trance

## Beats

1. **The west gate** (Sara). Sara and Trice wait on a mounting block at dawn. Sara stayed the night at the Morgan house (three windows wide, a worn stair carpet; Trice: "Don't"). The plain crestless carriage brings Rolph (with Ada's basket) and Tor.
2. **The amulets.** Tor hands out the three amulets on Karina's silver chains (Trice: "Karina"). "Where were you staying? I'd have liked to see it." "It's very small." "Oh, I like small." He half notices, mutters "*Small*," and lets it go.
3. **The flight.** Brown country, Ford worst of all; Tor flies in front, lips moving. The sea from the air; Rolph hunts for the beach ("it's *moved*") and finds it by **the Teeth**, a line of old stumps running into the surf.
4. **The house.** Sara prices it, and the first number is too low: gray shingle, faded blue shutters, a terrace along the front, no other roof but Walt's cottage by the gate. Tor: "It's a proper size." He asks to stay all summer. Rolph felt the same every year as a boy.
5. **The sea** (Rolph). Down the dune path with eyes on his feet; then "Now." Tor just looks. "It doesn't *stop*." He stands in the spray, tastes it: "It won't run out." "I was worried about the *fish*." The girls shriek into the waves; Tor sits down in the trance.
6. **The bucket** (Trice). Two and a half bells later the tide has reached him. She pours a bucket over his head; he chokes, then: "That's what it's like *in* the water." It's a help; it's in the well water at home too ("why Mother's kettle goes white"). She sits beside him, looks north along the coast, and does a sum.
7. **The loop** (Sara). Two squares: one on a rock in the surf, one wedged in the sand forty yards up. A foot-thick loop of perfectly clear water stands over the beach. It pushes hands out. They drink; Sara can't make the price come out.
8. **The splash war.** Rolph sweeps sheets of water over Sara four times. She walks to the rock, puts her hand on the square and thinks *stop*.
9. **The fall.** The loop drops; trenches gully the beach. Rolph and Trice laugh. Tor goes white: "Anyone." He explains the hundred-mile line: a child, a goat, a man from Austra, water down on a village, no water downstream.
10. **The fix.** Rolph laughs nervously, congratulates Sara ("You've saved a county. Possibly two"), then reminds Tor of his own words from second year, over a broken lamp: "Everything's got a flaw. You just have to find it. And then fix it, if you can." Tor: "It *only* listens to—"
11. **The basket** (Trice). Ada packed a luncheon, gone by mid-afternoon. The pantry: flour, salt, soda, honey gone solid, beans, oil, a mousetrap. Tor laughs till he leans on the doorpost: "It's *biscuits*."
12. **The fire.** Trice brings driftwood (burns blue and green with the salt), Rolph a live tree off the dunes. The logs were under the eaves all his life. Tor cooks; Sara cuts in the butter; Trice watches the oven.
13. **Walt.** He comes up at dusk with eggs and milk, knew Rolph as a boy ("Nobody sent"), eats a biscuit ("You'll do"), and goes back to his porch and his pipe: he sleeps light. Trice files the porch, the gravel path, the distance.
14. **"What's next?"** Biscuits and honey on the rug by the fire; Rolph eats nine. Cushion. "Homework." "The summer kind." Tor: "What sort?" "The dull sort."
15. **The kiss told as a joke.** Tor tells them about Karina; Sara and Trice exchange a look ("Karina doesn't run").
16. **Trice's lecture.** Nobody says no; it's manners, not lying (Sara: "It's why they never pay their bills on time"). A no is a debt, and people collect. "Count Wylde." "Who's Count Wylde?" "Not tonight."
17. **"I don't mean your squares."** Trice tells him he has a very good face; half the girls at the Ward dinner were looking at him. Her ears burn.
18. **Tor laughs, kindly.** The smallest person at school; strangers think he's fourteen; "Nobody's going to want me like that." Sara: "Tor. Look at me. She's right." He thinks they're the ones who don't understand.
19. **"He's had his Doretta."** Rolph, on his back, with no nickname: "I know why he thinks that." Trice connects it all. Tor: "Sorry. Who's Doretta?" Rolph puts a log on: "Sit down, Tor. It's a story. And you're in it."
