The door at the foot of the east stair opened, and Tor came out into the court with the sack on his shoulder. It clanked. He took two steps and stopped.

He stood on one leg and held the other up in front of him, and looked at it. He shook it. He put it down. It landed a little to one side of where he'd aimed it, and he lurched.

"Come on," he said to it. "Please."

He slapped his thigh twice with his free hand, and took another step. The leg swung out stiffly from the hip. He flexed the fingers of his free hand, and they closed slowly, and only partway.

"Oh, no," he said quietly. "Oh, no, no. What did I do?" He looked back at the door he'd come out of, and at the dark windows of the workroom above it. "Sat on it. I sat on it wrong. That's all. Walk it off. Walk it off, Tor."

He started across the court on the diagonal, toward the dormitory, very slowly, looking at his feet.

At the far side of the court, beyond the bell tower's black bulk, there was a flash.

It was blue-white, a short crackling spill of light, low to the ground, gone almost as soon as it came. In it, for an instant, there were two figures. One was huge. The other was smaller, though still taller than Tor, and held something out at arm's length toward the big one. A sound came across the stones, a dull *whump*, and the big figure went back three steps as if shoved.

Then it was dark again.

Tor stopped. He stood swaying, the sack on his shoulder, looking toward where the flash had been.

Another spill of sparks. The big figure was coming forward again, with sparks coming off its head and shoulders as it moved, lighting the stones around its feet. The smaller one backed away, stumbling. It held its arm out. *Whump.* The big one rocked back a step, and came on.

The smaller figure was staggering badly, one hand pressed to its side.

Tor took a step backward. Then another. He turned toward the dormitory door, and took two lurching steps toward it.

In the dark across the court, there was a heavy sound, flesh on cloth, and a cry: high, sharp, a girl's voice. It came back off the wall of the long hall.

Tor stopped.

He stood with his back to the fight. His head came slowly around.

"Miss Trice?" he said, under his breath.

From across the court, hoarsely: "Baker. *Run.*"

He turned all the way around, and came toward them.

He couldn't run. His left leg had stopped bending; he swung it from the hip, and lurched, and swung it again. The sack slid off his shoulder, and he caught it by the neck in both hands, and carried it in front of him like that, low, banging against his knees. He was talking, low and fast.

"—better have a very good reason. Out at this hour. Very good reason. Fighting. At this hour. Miss Trice. Honestly."

The smaller figure was on one knee on the flagstones. The big one stood over her. The sparks came off it in a long crackling sheet now, and in their light Trice's face was turned up, white, her mouth open, her arm half raised with something short and dark in her fist. The arm stopped halfway and stayed there, shaking. The big figure's face, above the sparks, was in shadow.

Tor came up behind it.

He planted his feet wide, swung the sack back with his whole body, and brought it around into the middle of the big figure's back.

*Clank.*

The big figure didn't move.

*Clank.* The squares inside the sack slid and chimed together. "Sorry," said Tor. *Clank.* "Sorry. Leave her alone. Sorry."

On the ground, Trice made a sound that might have been a laugh.

*Clank.*

The big figure turned around. It turned slowly, the whole body coming around after the head.

Trice's raised arm dropped. She put both palms flat on the stones and pushed herself up an inch.

Tor's knees gave. He didn't fall. His legs simply stopped holding him properly, and he sagged forward, and the sack fell out of his hands and hit the flagstones with a crash that rang all the way around the court. He put a hand out to nothing. He stayed up, bent over, swaying, looking up.

The clouds moved off the moon.

The light came down gray across the court and onto the big figure: a broad jaw, dark brows, a plain dark cloak with a brooch at the shoulder. The face was entirely still. There was nothing in it at all.

Tor's mouth opened. It made the shape of a name, without sound.

*Tovey.*

Tor's face went white, and still, and then, all at once, very polite.

On the ground behind them, Trice shook her head, once.

Tovey looked down at Tor a moment longer. Then his eyes moved off him, past him, unfocused.

And he swung.
