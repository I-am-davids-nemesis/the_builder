# Canon notes

Verified facts about *The Builder* and its world, kept separate from invention. Character-specific canon also appears, labeled, in each character's file.

## Publication

- *The Builder* (2012) is **book 1 of The Young Ancients** series by P.S. Power.
- Following books include *Knight Esquire* (2), *Knight of the Realm* (3), *Ambassador* (4), *Counselor* (5). *Ancient Kings* (9) reportedly closes Tor's first arc; later books follow Tor's younger siblings (e.g., Timon Baker).
- An "Author's Edition" reissue exists: *Builder: His Magic Will Change the World, His Power Will Save Yours...*
- Book 1 reportedly **ends abruptly** and leads directly into book 2.

## Premise (from blurbs)

- Tor Baker is a **builder**: someone who creates the magic that makes the kingdom of **Noram** function.
- He is the son of a baker from the deep woods, studying magic on a **special scholarship from the king**.
- His first project might fail, and he risks **expulsion for taking unscheduled time off** to work on it. In our book this is **Tor's fear, not a real risk**: nobody else thinks it's worth mentioning (see How magic works). (blurb canon; reading per the user)
- The project draws the attention of very powerful people; the world he thought he knew is a lie.
- He is caught in noble intrigue and plotting that **began before he was born**.
- His innocence is a liability: he could end up on the gallows for a few simple mistakes.

## World

- The setting is **far-future post-collapse Earth**. The book does **not** use American state names for counties or houses (corrected by the user from chapter one; an earlier research note claimed otherwise). The real house names are not yet recorded; the bible uses placeholders **House A** (Tovey's family) and **House B** (Davie's family). Common 20th-century names abound (Laura, Connie, Patricia, Sandy).
- The world is shaped by the **Ancients**: long-lived, genetically modified humans who name themselves after colors (Green, White, Red, Gray, Black, Brown). Tor eventually earns the nickname **Purple**.
- **King Richard Cordes** ordered Tor's scholarship but doesn't know him. He is also the one Ancient who didn't take a color name.
- **Culture clash:** Tor's backwoods, humble, prudish upbringing vs. the cosmopolitan, aggressive, libertine culture of his noble friends.

## The school

- The school is the **Lairdgren School**.
- A **tower bell** by the main courtyard rings the hours day and night; time is counted in bells.
- Students wear **brown trousers and tunics**. There is a **student store** and **isolation chambers** for building work.
- **Combat class is mixed** boys and girls.
- Daily **meditation classes** train lighter versions of the building trance; taught by **Doris**. (teacher canon, user)
- Tor's instructors: his **building instructor**, who seems to be a **special projects** instructor; **Doris** (meditation); and a **general magic** instructor (unnamed). (canon, user)
- **Afternoons are combat instruction**, led by head instructor **Kolb**. (canon, user)
- Students eat in a **cafeteria**. (canon, user)
- Instructors **don't praise work explicitly**, even when they think it's top-notch. Whether that's school culture or Tor not hearing it is left ambiguous. (canon, user)
- The school week has **rest days** with no classes. Tor's recovery day in chapter one is one. (assumed; decided by the user for our book) Students do their laundry then, which is why the washing house is crowded. (invented; accepted by the user)
- **Soap** is a gray, cracked lump that smells of ash; Tor buys his (likely at the student store). (invented; accepted by the user)
- There is a **washing area** with washing tubs; the laundry scene and the instructor's test both happen there. (canon, user)

## How magic works

- **Copying** from a template: about 1 in 60 people can do it, even untrained. Copy work runs the realm's infrastructure.
- **Novel builds** are rare; even builder students seldom try; professionals may need dozens of attempts.
- Advanced building like Tor's is so rare that producing **even one such builder from a whole cohort** counts as an amazing accomplishment for the school. Everyone at the school knows this instinctively; a student doing it is in no danger of expulsion for missed classes, and nobody thinks that's worth saying. (canon, user)
- The builder must understand the idea very clearly. A **sigil on a focus** (cheap ink on unfinished wood) is a mental map that holds focus.
- Building takes a **deep working trance** that can last days and scrambles perception afterward.
- **Ink drying unusually fast** is a good sign.
- **A build's value depends on its material and how long it lasts.** One on cheap wood and paint is worth much less than one on good material. Tor's drying field, on cheap wood and paint, is still worth **30 gold**, a significant sum. (canon, user, chapter one)
- A student's novel build (the prototype) **must be turned in for testing**. (canon, user, chapter one)
- **Vitality fields.** All living things have some kind of vitality field; the more complex the living thing, the stronger and more complex its field. **Human vitality fields naturally resist simple fields**; damaging a person directly takes more focused, specific effort. (canon, user)
- **Distinguishing living from non-living tissue** is one of the fundamental parts of a builder's education. (canon, user)
- **Using the drying field:** Tor starts it by touching the center of the sigil. Water wells out of the weave in beads and runs off the cloth onto the floor; the cloth is left dry and slightly stiff. Clothes on people within range dry too (Davie's wet sleeves). (invented; accepted by the user)
- **Copying in practice:** Tor and Rolph's door lock is a copy Tor made from the lock on Rolph's lockbox. It identifies the room's occupants and magically lifts a simple door bar on the inside. (canon, user, chapter one)
- **New magic as prestige:** among nobility, having new magic before anyone else is a mark of significant prestige. (canon, user, chapter one)

## Tor's first project

A field on a pine square, a hand across, painted in cheap blue house paint, that makes water leave cloth and fibers in an area, so laundry dries in moments. He spent **thirty hours** in trance (duration decided by the user) and missed about a day and a half of classes; it is untested at the opening. He first tests it at the laundry, about a third of the way into chapter one (see `outline/chapters.md`). The user describes the focus as cheap wood and **paint**; earlier notes said ink. (canon, user)

## Tor's second project (idea only, chapter one)

A **food drying field**: the drying field adapted to fruit and vegetables, against crop losses. Prompted by Rolph; sketched the same day and dreamed about that night. (canon, user)
