# Rolph

Rolph is Tor's loud, loyal shield who is quietly afraid he'll only ever matter as Tor's friend.

## Facts

- Spelled **Rolph** (canon; confirmed by the user).
- Tor's **roommate for two years**. (canon)
- Listens when Tor talks about magic; not frivolous. (canon)
- Tor "had been told" Rolph is from a **merchant family**. (canon) That story may be a cover. (assumed)
- His mother is **Connie**. (canon)
- Owns a **magic lockbox full of gold**, kept in their room. Tor copied its lock to make the room's door lock. (canon, user, chapter one) It's a hint about his family, never an admission.
- Very interested in Tor and his magic, in what seems to be innocent envy; he once **stared at the room lock for hours**. (canon, user)
- Wants a copy of the drying field **for his mother**. (canon, user)
- Tells Tor that **among nobility**, having new magic before anyone else is a mark of significant prestige. (canon, user)
- **Crop losses** are a frequent topic of conversation at his home. His wish that the field worked on fruit and vegetables gives Tor the idea for a food drying field. (canon, user)
- Patient and amused when Tor drifts off into work mid-conversation; keeps him company afterward, reading. (canon, user)

## Profile

- **Role:** Tor's closest friend and roommate; supposedly from a merchant family. His mother, Connie, takes an interest in Tor. (Connie and roommate are canon; the rest is assumed)
- **Want:** To be the one who protects Tor and shows him how the world really works.
- **Need:** To find a talent or purpose that is his own, not borrowed from his family or his friend.
- **Internal tension:** Genuine love for Tor vs. envy of how easily Tor surpasses everyone. He'd die before admitting the second.
- **External tension:** He hides his family's real standing, and isn't sure how much of the friendship was arranged. He suspects Connie's warmth toward Tor is partly strategy, and he's not sure he minds.
- **Hidden aspect:** His true family. The reader can learn this early, as dramatic irony, while Tor doesn't.

## Reaction rules

- **Tor drifts into a build mid-conversation** → waits, amused, and doesn't take offense. (canon, user)
- **Tor stuck in a social situation he can't handle** → arrives, takes over the conversation, and gets him out. (canon, user: the end of the laundry scene, chapter one)
- **Tor insulted** → steps physically in front of him, big and grinning, before anyone else reacts.
- **Tor achieves something huge** → celebrates loudest in public; goes quiet for a while in private.
- **Asked about his own magic or future** → jokes, changes the subject, or starts a fight.
- **Family politics surface** → plays dumb, but catches every detail and reports it to Tor later.

## Voice

- **Diction:** casual, nicknames for everyone ("Little Tor," "Baker"). Merchant talk on the surface, with noble manners slipping out.
- **Rhythm:** fast, overlapping, answers his own questions.
- **Tics:** teasing Tor about his prudishness; claps shoulders; "Trust me"; asks what Tor's builds would sell for.
- **Never:** speaks formally unless he's truly angry. Formality from Rolph is a red flag.

## Sample lines

- "Trust me, Baker, nobody here is as scary as they want you to think. Well. Almost nobody."
- "You built that in a week? Of course you did. Of course you did."
- "My lord, I believe my friend has finished speaking with you." (cold and formal; he's about to swing)

## Series slice

Book 1 proves Rolph's loyalty under pressure. It leaves open his envy, his own purpose, his true family, and whether the friendship was arranged.
