# Clark, son of Count Missouri

Clark is an honorable antagonist: he opposes Tor on principle, and his principles eventually force him to Tor's side.

## Profile

- **Role:** Heir to the county of Missouri; top of the combat class and the school's social order. (assumed)
- **Want:** To uphold his house's name and the proper order. Commoners on scholarship upset it.
- **Need:** To separate honor from rank. The most honorable person he meets is a baker's son.
- **Internal tension:** A rigid code of fairness vs. a class prejudice that code can't justify. Every time Tor behaves well, Clark's worldview cracks a little.
- **External tension:** His father's long rivalry with Mississippi (invented), and pressure to win the school's social contests as a proxy for that feud.
- **Hidden aspect:** Doubts about his own prejudice that he would never voice; and whatever Missouri's role in the older plot turns out to be. (Not yet defined.)

## Reaction rules

- **Tor breaks etiquette** → corrects him coldly, in public.
- **Someone cheats against Tor** → intervenes on Tor's side, furious that he has to.
- **Loses to Tor** → formal acknowledgment, no excuses; then trains twice as hard.
- **Mississippi's people are involved** → suspects them first, sometimes wrongly. That's his blind spot.

## Voice

- **Diction:** formal, military, full titles; uses "one" instead of "I" when making rulings.
- **Rhythm:** short declaratives; no filler words; long pauses.
- **Tics:** "That is not done."; addresses Tor as "Scholar Baker."
- **Never:** jokes at someone else's expense, or lies outright.

## Sample lines

- "Scholar Baker. That is not done. One does not address a countess's daughter by her given name."
- "The match was fair. You won it. I have nothing further to say."
- "Step away from him. If this is settled, it is settled with me."

## Series slice

Book 1 shifts Clark from contempt to grudging respect. It leaves open whether he'll break with his father, and what Missouri's role is in the older plot.
