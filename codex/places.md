# Places as pressure

Each setting squeezes a different tension, so moving the cast changes what the scene is about. Canon details about the school are in `canon-notes.md`.

| Place | What it pressures | Who it tests most |
| --- | --- | --- |
| The school (Lairdgren, canon) | Rules, rank, and grades; Tor's accidental rule-breaking | Tor, Tovey, the headmaster |
| Tor and Rolph's room (canon) | Privacy: the one place Tor isn't performing; the door bar shuts out the school's demands. Rolph's lockbox of gold sits in plain view. | Tor, Rolph |
| The washing house | At the back of the dormitory court: a long low room of wet stone, one pump, a copper over a banked fire, a drain down the middle. Old white tubs, glazed inside, on iron feet cast like an animal's paws; nobody knows why there were once so many. (invented; accepted by the user) Busy on rest days. Where Tor first tests the drying field and the building instructor dunks him. | Tor, Davie |
| The practice yard (canon, chapter two) | Bodies and rank stripped away: Kolb judges only what a student can do. The pell, the cutter, the dust. Where Tor is assigned a shield that could kill him. | Tor, Kolb, Trice, Sara |
| The building instructor's office (canon) | Where the school meets money: the instructor brings a merchant here to buy Tor's work. | Tor, the building instructor, Douglas Sorvey |
| The courtyard at night (canon, chapter two) | The school's public center emptied out; the bell tower overhead and nobody to see. A clouded moon, a lamp at the gate lodge with nobody awake, and the long hall's wall throwing sound back. (invented; accepted by the user) Where Tor finds Trice losing a fight to Tovey. | Tor, Trice, Tovey |
| The cafeteria (canon) | Public, everyday, and inescapable: everyone eats there, so whatever happens there has an audience. Chapter one ends as Tor leaves it for combat. | Tor, Rolph, Kolb |
| Hollin, the town below the school | Money and ordinary people; where Tor's work first reaches real lives. Five miles down a long slope; a square with a well (Kolb's runners slap its stone), a pump, a chandler's, a little chapel whose bell strikes the half hours, and a mill road out the far side. From the road the school's bell tower stands on the ridge like a finger. A mile short of town stands an old marker stone, gray and smoother than stone should be, with letters nobody can read. The east road from there runs into the trees toward Two Rivers, four days on foot. (invented; accepted by the user) | Tor, Miriam, Sara |
| Two Rivers (Tor's hometown, canon) | Tor's past and family secrets; the humble self-image he clings to | Tor; any friend who visits sees a side of him they didn't know |
| The capital | Open politics and the crown; nobody is off duty | Patricia, Count B, Tovey |
| Rolph's family home, in the capital (canon) | Tor is invited for the next school break. The private face of the capital's politics: whatever Rolph's family is, it's at home here. | Tor, Rolph, Connie |
| The beach resort house | Leisure among nobles; the culture clash between Tor's prudishness and his friends' worldliness | Tor, Rolph, Sara |
| A city of Gallaia | Foreign rules and outside threats; Noram's rivalries look small from here | Everyone; alliances get tested away from home |

The beach house is where characters say what they can't say at school. Two Rivers is where Tor's story about himself stops fitting.
