## Description

Hollin is a small town five miles from Lairdgren (three runs there and back make thirty miles), an hour each way at a run. The school road leaves the main gate, rocks down the hill in ruts, bends round a stand of trees and runs down a long slope that levels out at the bottom. A mile short of town an old marker stone stands at the verge, gray and smoother than any stone should be, with letters cut in it that nobody can read. There the east road branches off into the trees toward Two Rivers. Looking back from the stone, you see the school's bell tower standing on the ridge like a finger. From the air the valley between is gray-green fields, walls, a wood and a stream, and Hollin comes up over the edge of the next hill. Tor can take it in at a glance. It's smaller than a single noble town house laid end to end.

The town is built round a square. In the square stands a well with a worn stone lip, polished pale on one side where Kolb's runners slap it before they turn for home. There's also a pump where women draw water, and a chandler's. Carts unload and townsfolk buy twists of salt there. The mill road leaves from the far side of the square and runs out to the mill and its slowly turning wheel. The little chapel has a stubby tower and a lead roof, and its bell strikes the half hours. At dawn on a still morning the lead shows silver, mist lies in the folds of the valley, and smoke from four or five chimneys goes straight up. A dog crosses the square and sits down to scratch. The town has a pub, the Plough. Its traffic is carts: a mule cart of cabbages whose near wheel squeals like a pig, a carter's cart with two horses, and the bakers' boys who carry bread up to the school before the fifth bell. Its people include a miller, a cabbage carter, a boy named Sam Carter, and a woman who takes in laundry, the mother of Hob from the school kitchens.
