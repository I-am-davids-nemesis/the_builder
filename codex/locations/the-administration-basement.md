# The administration basement (canon, chapter three)

Under the administration building: a **very cold** corridor that feels **empty**, and wrong, because the mind won't stay on it. Its doors can't be focused on without a deep trance or an **access token** pressed to a **pad** beside them. Behind one is a room where a **roaring fire** gives no warmth, and **one side can't be focused on**: wards on the room and on its **chairs** hide who is sitting there. (canon, user) The cold may be the wards' doing (assumed).

- **What it pressures:** Secrets and authority. It's where the school deals with what can't be seen in public, and where Tor first meets power that hides itself on purpose. His trance-sight breaks the room's one rule: you don't see who you aren't meant to see.
- **Who it tests most:** Tor, Wensa, the headmaster.
- **Details to reuse:** the token and pad; heat on the face with no warmth in it; breath showing beside a fire; attention sliding off walls; chairs that hide their sitters. (first two canon, user; the rest invented)

## Description

A stair of thirty-one worn stone steps goes down from the back hall of the administration building. At the bottom a lamp burns in a niche, already lit, and beyond it runs a long stone-flagged corridor. Under the wards it won't stay in the mind: the eye slides to the lamp, the boots, the stair, and comes back with "a wall, and more wall, and a floor that was a floor." Seen plainly, it has oak, iron-studded doors set into its walls at intervals, dark with age, with an iron ring for a handle; the warded room's door is seventeen flagstones from the bottom step, on the left, with a small brass plate set into the stone at shoulder height beside it. A disc of dark metal with a frost-like pattern, worn on a cord, pressed to the plate, makes a sound like a held breath let out, and the door shows itself for a breath. The cold is the one thing that stays: a dead, still cellar cold in deep winter that comes up through boot soles and lies along the backs of the hands, while outside it is barely autumn. Breath shows white.

The room behind that door is small, with a low whitewashed vault and a stone floor with a worn rug. A great fireplace fills the far wall, its fire roaring with logs as thick as a man's leg banked high; orange light moves over the walls and heat lands on the face, but the room is as cold as the corridor ("The room is always cold"), and breath still smokes in front of the fire. Wet clothes steam faintly in the firelight. Two chairs stand on the near side of the fire with their backs to the door; to the left of the fire, three more stand in a half circle, among them a big leather armchair, and one has a brass stud set into its arm that switches off the wards hiding whoever sits there. Until then that side shows only firelight and wall. A loud laugh goes off the vault "like a dropped pan."

The scene runs from the middle of the night to dawn; climbing back up, people find the back hall full of pale morning light through opened shutters and a smell of porridge, and their hands warm again. Coming up out of the room feels like finding the world has gone on without you.

### Look

A narrow stone-flagged corridor below ground, lit only by one lamp in a wall niche at the foot of a worn stone stair, with dark, iron-studded oak doors at intervals and a small brass plate beside one. Behind it, a small room under a low whitewashed vault, stone floor and a worn rug, a great fireplace on the far wall roaring with huge logs, throwing orange light over the walls. Armchairs, one of big leather, stand around the hearth.
