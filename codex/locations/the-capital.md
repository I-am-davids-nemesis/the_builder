# The capital

- **What it pressures:** Open politics and the crown; nobody is off duty
- **Who it tests most:** Patricia, Count B, Tovey

## Description

The capital sits on a wide, slow, gray-green river crowded with barges, and from the air it shows first as a long, straight line of white in the haze. The line grows towers and roofs that run up and over the hills on both banks: white stone walls (white because the local quarry is white; the old part is nearly all stone), red tile, green copper, gold, banners, chimney smoke and the glitter of a thousand windows in the afternoon sun. A great white wall, thick as a house, rings the whole city and runs down to the water on both sides, with a flagged tower every hundred yards; the river gate opens through a shaded arch. Close to, the white fills with color: blue, saffron and green awnings over the streets, window boxes, washing strung across the alleys, and a whole hillside on the far bank where the dyers live, painted pink, yellow and sky-blue. The approach is a new-graveled road along the river, clean-ditched, marked with waist-high white chamfered milestones cut in black (*THE CITY — 22*), through farms, then walled market gardens, then houses built on the walls.

Inside, the streets hold fountains (one lucky, one only said to be), a stone horse in a square, a corn exchange, river stairs where eels are sold, the chandlers' hall, a river temple with vaults beneath it, glass halls on the river where fine fields sit on ironwood and velvet under lamps, and merchants' wharves and counting houses. The Hill is the noble quarter: short, steep streets of large white houses with porticos, stone dogs on the steps and shutters closed against the heat. The palace stands behind a long, windowless white wall higher than a house, on a quiet tree-lined street, entered by a plain oak-and-iron gate; it has a dome and a bell tower. The city guard wears red and white in summer (white tunic, red sash, red cap with a white cockade) and now flies; flying inside the walls is forbidden for now.

Summer in the capital is fierce. Out of the wind, inside the walls, the heat comes down like a lid and the stones give it back; the air smells of river, horse, hot tile and frying, and the river stinks in the heat. Shutters stay closed all afternoon, nobody eats hot soup, luncheon is at the third bell after noon, and babies cry all night. The city's bells ring the hours: a thin one down the hill, then the great one in the palace tower that makes windows hum.

### Look

A huge walled river city of white stone climbing the hills on both banks, roofed in red tile, green copper and gold, with towers, banners and smoke. A massive white wall with flagged towers every hundred yards runs down to the water. Inside, streets shade under blue, saffron and green awnings, with washing across the alleys, fountains and a whole far-bank hillside painted pink, yellow and sky-blue. Bright, hard summer sun.

### Image prompt

A vast walled city seen from the air on a hot summer afternoon, spreading along both banks of a wide, slow, gray-green river crowded with barges. White stone buildings climb up and over the hills on either side, roofed in red tile, green copper and gilded domes, with towers, fluttering banners, chimney smoke and the glitter of a thousand windows in the sun. A massive white wall, thick as a house, rings the city and runs down to the water on both banks, with a flag-topped tower every hundred yards. Nearer, the streets are shaded by blue, saffron and green awnings, washing hangs across narrow alleys, and one far hillside is painted in pink, yellow and sky-blue houses. Warm haze, bright hard light, a sense of heat rising off stone; a grand, sunlit, faintly old-world capital with odd survivals of an older age.

### Open questions

- The city is never named; the milestone says only “THE CITY.” Does it have a name?
- Travel time from Lairdgren: “six days in the coach” (ch. 5, sc. 1) vs. Tovey's “a week of dust” on the road (ch. 5, sc. 2); the express takes three days (ch. 2). Probably compatible, but worth pinning.
- Tovey insists “There's only one river,” yet they follow a second river north first. Is the capital's river named, and which bank is the palace on?
- Where the palace sits relative to the Hill and the river (on a hilltop? its own hill?) is not settled; a skyline picture needs the dome's position.
- Winter appearance, night light, and rain are never shown; every manuscript view is summer afternoon.
- Proposed fixed trait: white stone buildings and walls (the quarry is white), with red tile, green copper and gold roofs.
- Proposed fixed trait: a white city wall, thick as a house, with a flagged tower every hundred yards, running down to the river on both banks.
