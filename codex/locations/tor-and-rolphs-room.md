# Tor and Rolph's room (canon)

- **What it pressures:** Privacy: the one place Tor isn't performing; the door bar shuts out the school's demands. Rolph's lockbox of gold sits in plain view.
- **Who it tests most:** Tor, Rolph

## Description

The room is the fourth door along the top corridor of the men's dormitory: a narrow room with two beds facing each other, a desk, and one window looking out over the dormitory roofs, with the bell tower standing up over the court and the sheep field below the wall. The door is plain oak with a plain iron latch and no keyhole. Inside, a bar of ash, dark with handling, rests in a pair of iron brackets under a pattern painted on the planks in cheap blue (lines, angles, a ring); it lifts itself with a soft wooden knock when either of them comes within a stride, and drops home with a *clunk*. A bent nail on the inside of the door takes Rolph's coat. The walls are plaster, the floor bare boards, cold underfoot in the dark of a winter morning. Seen from the door in the camera drafts, Tor's half is on the left and is all paper: paper heaped on the desk and under it, pine offcuts on the sill with half-painted patterns, more offcuts and, later, dead planks under his bed, a jar of brushes, a saucer crusted with dried blue, the cheap gray paper from the store, candle stubs, a lamp with a brass foot. Rolph's half, on the right, is a made bed with a bedpost and a creak in it, a shelf of books (and string), and at the foot of the bed his chest: the size of a bread bin, dark wood bound in darker iron, with a keyless lock plate cut with a very fine pattern, full of gold. From chapter four Tor keeps a small plain oak box with iron corners under the foot of his own bed, its lock replaced by the same click.

The light comes in long and gray over the roofs in the morning and is dark blue before dawn; at night a lamp turned low or a candle on the desk, and from the court the room is the one window on the top floor still lit. The weather comes in through that window: the wet-grass smell of the sheep field and the lambs in a warm spring week, with the window open all day; frost in feathered fronds on the inside of the glass in the cold snap, until Tor's copper plate (book-sized, roughly sheared, nailed above his bed at heart height with four ordinary nails) makes it "summer," with the frost running down the black glass, the candle leaning, the plaster ticking, and the corridor wall outside warm enough for half the floor to lean on; and in early summer, with the window shut and the plate forgotten, an oven that smells of hot copper and hot ink, until he turns the plate round and the cool settles in like cellar air. The room carries the marks of the builds: the sour green smell of etching acid and cut-penny copper squares laid in rows of ten on the floor between the beds; a long pale scrape in the ceiling plaster above the window and a ring of plaster dust under the middle of the ceiling that never quite sweeps up; a hand-shaped scorch on the corner of the desk; a crack across the lower windowpane, papered over. Its sounds are the bar's knock, the tower bell, Tor's pencil biting the paper at night, his muttering, knocks and voices and feet in the corridor, a door slamming and someone shouting about a boot; in the long trance it smells of copper, sour ink and boy.

### Look

A narrow dormitory room with plaster walls and bare floorboards: two plain beds facing each other along the side walls, a wooden desk under the single small-paned window. A plain oak door with an iron latch and, inside, an ash bar in iron brackets beneath a blue-painted pattern. One side is drifts of paper, pine offcuts and brushes; the other a made bed, a book shelf and an iron-bound chest the size of a bread bin at the bed's foot. A book-sized copper plate nailed above the paper-strewn bed. Gray daylight by day; lamp or candle at night.

### Image prompt

The interior of a narrow, plain student dormitory room on the top floor of an old stone school, seen from just inside the door. Plaster walls, bare wooden floorboards, a single small-paned window at the far end looking out over gray slate roofs toward a stone bell tower. Two plain wooden beds face each other along the side walls. The left half of the room is chaos: a wooden desk heaped with loose gray paper, more paper under it and on the floor, scraps of pale pine board with half-finished blue line patterns propped on the windowsill, a jar of brushes, a saucer crusted with dried blue paint, a brass-footed oil lamp, a candle stub; above the rumpled bed, a rough-cut copper plate the size of a book is nailed to the plaster with four nails, etched with a few simple lines and a downward hook. The right half is tidy: a neatly made bed, a short shelf of leather-bound books, and at the foot of the bed a small chest of dark wood bound in darker iron, with a smooth iron lock plate and no keyhole. On the inside of the plain oak door, a dark ash bar rests in two iron brackets under a simple pattern painted in blue. Soft gray morning light falls long across the floor. The mood is quiet, cramped, lived-in and warm.

### Open questions

- **The chest and the lockbox:** in chapters one to three Rolph's chest *is* the lockbox (bread-bin-sized, keyless plate, full of gold). In chapter four, scene 7, his coat goes "into the chest at the foot of his bed on top of the lockbox," as if the lockbox sat inside a larger chest; and "Rolph moved the chest" to get it out from under the plank. One object or two?
- **One desk or two?** Chapter one says "two beds and two desks," but every later scene has "the desk," and the camera draft speaks of "Tor's corner of the desk." Shared desk, or does Rolph's desk just never appear?
- **Stool or chair?** Tor sits on a stool at the desk in chapter one; later he has a chair (chapters two and four), Rolph's coat goes over "the back of a chair," and Trice pulls "the stool" up beside his chair. Is there one of each?
- **Left and right:** only the camera draft places Tor's half on the left and Rolph's on the right (presumably from the door). Is that fixed?
- **The broken pane** (chapter four, scene 7) is papered over; was it ever mended?
- **Which era for pictures:** the copper plate is on the wall only from the cold snap (chapter four, scene 8) on; pictures of earlier scenes should leave it out. The prompt above shows the later room.
- **Size, window and fittings:** the room's length, the window's size, shape and side (it's opposite the door in nobody's words), whether it has shutters or curtains, any fireplace or stove (the cold snap suggests none), and what the beds look like are never given.
- Proposed fixed trait: a narrow room with plaster walls and bare boards, two beds facing each other, and a single window over the dormitory roofs.
- Proposed fixed trait: a plain oak door with an iron latch and no keyhole; inside, a dark ash bar in two iron brackets under a pattern painted in blue.
- Proposed fixed trait: Tor's half buried in paper, pine offcuts and brushes; Rolph's half tidy, with a shelf of books.
- Proposed fixed trait: at the foot of Rolph's bed, a chest the size of a bread bin, dark wood bound in darker iron, with a keyless lock plate.
- Proposed fixed trait (from chapter four, scene 8): a book-sized, rough-sheared copper plate nailed with four nails above Tor's bed, at about heart height.
