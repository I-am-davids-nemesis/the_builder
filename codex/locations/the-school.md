# The school (Lairdgren, canon)

- **What it pressures:** Rules, rank, and grades; Tor's accidental rule-breaking
- **Who it tests most:** Tor, Tovey, the headmaster

## Description

Lairdgren stands on a hill above a valley, walled, with an arched main gate (a yard just inside it, a gate lodge or porters' lodge by it, the gate left on the latch before the fifth bell for the bakers' boys from Hollin). Outside the gate the road runs down to the left toward Hollin, an hour's run away; to the right a path follows the wall down through long grass to the sheep field, and below that lie a ditch, a pond with ducks, and at the valley bottom Lairdgren's wood of oak, holly and old beech. Seen from the valley or the air the school is small on its hill, its roofs and bell tower above the wall, the sun rising behind it. Inside, the buildings are old stone round a main court of gray flagstones: the square, plain bell tower at one corner (open bell under a little peaked roof, a weathercock shaped like a fish, weathered gray upper courses over three bottom courses of smooth, seamless, poured gray that frost never touches); the dormitory with its stair and top corridor of oak doors; the east range, its undercroft holding the six isolation chambers and its stair holding small workrooms; the long hall, with steps down behind it to the practice yard, whose far wall of huge close-fitted blocks the color of dried bread is older than the school; the north side, with the building room and the building master's office; the administration building, with a back hall, a yard behind it, and a stair of thirty-one steps down to a warded corridor; a chapel; a cloister; a library; the student store; the washing house; an infirmary; and the kitchens, whose rain-running kitchen yard holds a woodpile, a scullery window and a water butt, with an arch through to the dormitories. Past the corner of the long hall the ground drops to a lower lawn and the low wall of a kitchen garden. The women's wing is a long cold walk across the court, with a stove at the end of each corridor; the scholarship boys of the east wing sleep four to a room without one. Rooms are plastered, with oak doors, iron latches and windows that stick in frost; corridors have windows at their ends.

The bell counts every hour, its strokes followed a beat later by an echo off the chapel roof, rolling out over the court and down the hill. The court throws sound back off the long hall's wall; boots ring on it in hard frost. Rooks come home over it at dusk, lambs complain below the wall in spring, a dog barks down the hill at night, a kitchen boy drags a pail across flagstones before the sixth bell, and somewhere a window bangs open or a door slams over a lost boot. The undercroft smells of stone and lamp oil; the back yards of wet stone; the steps up from below of bread; the morning administration hall of porridge; the building master's office of linseed and cold ashes; the cafeteria of the first-day stew.

The light and weather move through the year. Early mornings are gray and wet, with mist lying in the valley like milk in a saucer; frost whitens the court's flags (never the tower's poured base) and on one line brown tunics hang stiff between dormitory windows. In a spring cold snap the court turns iron under hard small stars, frost grows in fern-like fronds on the inside of the windows, and ice in the practice-yard butt will bear a student; then the wind goes south and it thaws by noon. In the hot spell the court goes white with glare at noon and the flagstones give the heat back through boot soles, while the library and the stone corridors stay cool. At night the court is dark under the black tower, with a single lamp at the gate lodge and most dormitory windows dark; at dawn the tower stands black against the sky, and the sun rising behind the hill catches it first.

### Look

A walled school of old gray stone on a hilltop above a green valley: plain stone ranges and wings with many windows, set round a wide court of gray flagstones. At one corner rises a square, plain bell tower with an open bell under a small peaked roof and a fish-shaped weathercock; its bottom three courses are smooth, seamless, poured-looking gray. A road runs down from an arched gate; behind one hall a sand yard backs onto a wall of huge, close-fitted, bread-colored blocks older than the rest.

### Open questions

- **Overall size and layout:** never given. How many buildings, how many floors, and where do the dormitory, east range, long hall, north side, administration building, chapel, cloister, library, kitchens, women's wing and gate stand relative to the court and to each other?
- **Building materials beyond the tower:** only the tower's gray stone, the practice-yard wall's bread-colored blocks, plaster walls and oak doors are given. Are the other buildings the same gray stone? What are the roofs (slate, lead, tile)?
- **Undercroft: east range or old wing?** Chapter one puts the isolation chambers under “the east range”; chapter four puts them under “the old wing.” Same building?
- **Gate lodge or porters' lodge?** Rotating-third says “gate lodge,” camera says “porters' lodge.” Same building? And is the main gate an arch (chapter four's “gate arch”)?
- **Where is the chapel?** Its roof echoes the bell from chapter one on, but it's never placed or seen.
- **Corridor floors:** a corridor's boards muffle a man's boots (chapter two), but a boot goes “skidding down the flags” of the corridor before theory (chapter four). Boards upstairs and flags below?
- **Where do scholarship boys sleep?** The east wing houses scholarship boys four to a room without a stove, but Tor, a scholarship boy, shares a two-bed room on the dormitory's top corridor with Rolph. Is that deliberate (the king's seal, Rolph's arrangement)?
- **Cafeteria, dining hall or hall?** The eating room is called all three; is it part of the long hall?
- **Which way does the school face?** The sun rises behind it as seen from Hollin's side; is Hollin west of the school?
- **The porter's “school's gray”:** do staff wear gray as students wear brown?
- Proposed fixed trait: old walled school of gray stone on a hill above a valley, reached by a road down from an arched gate.
- Proposed fixed trait: a wide main court of gray flagstones.

- Proposed fixed trait: the practice yard's far wall of huge close-fitted blocks the color of dried bread, older than the school.
- Proposed fixed trait: students in brown tunics and trousers.
