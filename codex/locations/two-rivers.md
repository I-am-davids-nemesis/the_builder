# Two Rivers (Tor's hometown, canon)

- **What it pressures:** Tor's past and family secrets; the humble self-image he clings to
- **Who it tests most:** Tor; any friend who visits sees a side of him they didn't know

## Description

The manuscript has never shown Two Rivers on the page; everything we know comes from Tor's talk and from what other people say. It is a small place in the forest, four days' walk east of Lairdgren down a road that goes "off into the trees", so remote that the nearest noble is four days off and nobody there has ever seen one. It is small enough that a letter addressed only to "Baker, at the bakery" will find its way, and so small that Tor reckons the town would fit ten times over into one corner of the capital. The biggest building there that Tor mentions is the chapel, and even that is smaller than the Ardens' "small house". When Tor asks whether the capital is "all stone", he implies that Two Rivers is not.

What we have is household detail from the Baker home and bakery. There is an oven, and the children carry hot trays in from it. A cellar under the house stays warm in winter and cool in summer, and Tor's mother keeps the butter there. The kitchen is big enough for the whole long line of children to stand in it by size on feast days. A purifier older than Tor sits on a bit of slate on the water jug. Out the back, by the woodpile, is the privy, with a moon cut in its door. His mother makes a sorrel soup and puts the cream in before it's cold. His father cut down the plain brown belt Tor wears. People there do up only the top button. Nothing in the manuscript describes the light, weather, sounds or smells of the place itself, though the cellar talk suggests real winters.

### Look

A small forest village of modest houses among tall trees, much smaller than any town nearby. A chapel is its largest building. Behind the houses are woodpiles and wooden privies with a crescent moon cut in the door. One house is also a bakery, with a working oven, a deep cellar and a kitchen big enough for a large family.

### Image prompt

A small, remote village deep in a forest, reached by a single dirt road that winds in through the trees. A cluster of modest, plain houses, with a small chapel as the largest building. Behind the houses are stacked woodpiles and a wooden outhouse with a crescent moon cut into its door. One house is a village bakery with a chimney and a bread oven, and a warm kitchen glows through its windows. Tall trees surround the village on every side. The mood is quiet, homely, poor but well kept, a place far from any city. Everyday life in a rural community, simple materials, no grandeur.

### Open questions

- Village or homestead? `codex/characters/tor.md` makes Tor a baker's son "from the woods around Two Rivers", and the opening has him coming "from the forests round Two Rivers". But the address "Baker, at the bakery, Two Rivers" puts the bakery in the town. Is the Baker house in Two Rivers itself or out in the woods?
- Building materials: nothing settles them. "Is it all stone?" suggests timber or a mix. Are the houses log, plank, wattle-and-daub, or stone-footed timber? What are the roofs: thatch, shingle or slate?
- Are there two rivers, and where? The name suggests a confluence, but no river has been shown.
- Number of houses, the layout (a single street, a green, a square?), and whether the chapel has a tower or a bell.
- Season, weather, light, sounds and smells are all unestablished. Should the forest be pine, oak, beech or mixed? Are there hills?
- Post-collapse texture (§6): are there any ruins or odd survivals at or near Two Rivers, like Hollin's marker stone?
- `outline/05-chapter-5/01-school-as-normal.md` reads "Two Bends" in a summary as Two Rivers. Is that confirmed?
