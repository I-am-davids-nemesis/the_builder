# Rolph's family home, in the capital (canon)

- **What it pressures:** Tor is invited for the next school break. The private face of the capital's politics: whatever Rolph's family is, it's at home here.
- **Who it tests most:** Tor, Rolph, Connie

## Description

The family calls it "the small house," and it is small only beside the palace. It stands behind a long white wall, higher than a house and without a single window, that runs the whole length of one side of a long, quiet, tree-lined street. Visitors come in by a plain gate set back in an arch: oak bound with black iron, a carter's gate, kept by four guards in purple and black. They work from a list, and an intruder once got as far as the fig walk. Over the wall shows a long gray-slated roof with a row of tall chimneys. Further off, above the trees and still inside the wall, rise more roofs, towers and a dome. Inside, it's the biggest building Tor has ever entered. Its second floor has a long corridor with a carpet down the middle so thick that boots make no sound, tall windows at the end, a linen press, and Rolph's room next door to the guest suite. A main stair leads down. Back stairs, close and hot, lead to the kitchens. At the bottom of the stairs a passage runs past a row of doors and smells of beeswax and boot blacking. It ends at a door onto a yard of pale flagstones and whitewashed walls, where the sun comes off every surface at once. Round the side of the house the fig walk runs between two rows of trees with broad dark leaves and fat green fruit. It is paved, edged with raked gravel, and dappled with sun.

The guest suite is several rooms. One holds only chairs. In the bedroom, a bed big enough for a family lying crossways is hung with pale green curtains, and the windows reach the floor. In the first room a great long table of dark polished wood carries a deep moss-green cloth. There are a rug, pictures, a bowl of white flowers on a stand, and a long glass between the windows. The washroom is tiled in white and blue, with a bath on four brass feet like a beast's and two brass taps, one cold and one warm, and a green-scented, expensive soap. The small dining room is the one the family uses when there's nobody to impress. Its table seats twelve, its open windows give onto the fig walk, and its painted ceiling of birds and vines has gone unlooked-at for thirty years. The queen's sitting room has windows that stand open on the dark, with moths coming in to the lamp. The kitchens at noon are like the inside of a bread oven, with ovens, a great range where the bread is baked, a yard door, and the smell of bread, smoke and onions.

In summer the whole house holds the capital's heat. It presses in through the tall windows in the afternoon, and by just past the fifth bell the long carpet holds it the way bread does. Until Tor's plates, there was no cool room in it, and the queen hadn't slept well there in summer since she married. Evening light comes in long and gold. At dawn the windows go from black to gray to white. The city's bells carry over the roofs: first a thin one down the hill, then the great bell in the palace tower, near and deep, that makes the window glass hum. Other sounds: a cock crowing far off, a rake on the fig walk, the kitchens faintly below.

### Open questions

- **Is the small house part of the palace?** Chapter 5 sets the dome "much further off," apart from the small house, yet in chapter 6 Tor thinks he's in "the palace, the actual palace, with the dome." Is the small house a separate building inside the palace wall, or a wing of the palace?
- **Family home or guest house?** The chapter 6 luncheon outline calls it "the guest house Tor is staying in," but Connie calls it the house she "married into," and the family sleeps and dines there.
- **Corridor temperature:** on arrival the second-floor corridor is "long" and "cool" (scene 3, both modes), but Karina finds it "like walking into an oven, all the way along," and by the next morning it's warm. Was it only cool compared with the street?
- **Exterior walls:** only the gray-slated roof and chimneys are described. What are the house's walls made of, and what color (white, like the city)? How many floors? Are there shutters?
- **Where is the palace tower bell?** It's "nearer" and makes the windows hum; how close does the palace proper stand to the small house?
- Admiral, the dog afraid of the stairs, and the carp pond the king fell into as a boy are mentioned but not placed. Are they at this house?
- The great hall with the old gods on the ceiling (outline, chapter 5) is held for later; is it in the small house or the palace proper?
