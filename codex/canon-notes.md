# Canon notes

Verified facts about *The Builder* and its world, kept separate from invention. Character-specific canon also appears, labeled, in each character's file.

## Publication

- *The Builder* (2012) is **book 1 of The Young Ancients** series by P.S. Power.
- Following books include *Knight Esquire* (2), *Knight of the Realm* (3), *Ambassador* (4), *Counselor* (5). *Ancient Kings* (9) reportedly closes Tor's first arc; later books follow Tor's younger siblings (e.g., Timon Baker).
- An "Author's Edition" reissue exists: *Builder: His Magic Will Change the World, His Power Will Save Yours...*
- Book 1 reportedly **ends abruptly** and leads directly into book 2.

## Premise (from blurbs)

- Tor Baker is a **builder**: someone who creates the magic that makes the kingdom of **Noram** function.
- He is the son of a baker from the deep woods, studying magic on a **special scholarship from the king**.
- His first project might fail, and he risks **expulsion for taking unscheduled time off** to work on it. In our book this is **Tor's fear, not a real risk**: nobody else thinks it's worth mentioning (see How magic works). (blurb canon; reading per the user)
- The project draws the attention of very powerful people; the world he thought he knew is a lie.
- He is caught in noble intrigue and plotting that **began before he was born**.
- His innocence is a liability: he could end up on the gallows for a few simple mistakes.

## World

- The setting is **far-future post-collapse Earth**. The book does **not** use American state names for counties or houses (corrected by the user from chapter one; an earlier research note claimed otherwise). Tovey's house is **Thompson** (canon, user, chapter two); Davie's is still the placeholder **House B**. Common 20th-century names abound (Laura, Connie, Patricia, Sandy).
- The world is shaped by the **Ancients**: long-lived, genetically modified humans who name themselves after colors (Green, White, Red, Gray, Black, Brown). Tor eventually earns the nickname **Purple**.
- **King Richard Cordes** ordered Tor's scholarship but doesn't know him. He is also the one Ancient who didn't take a color name.
- **Hereditary magic.** Most **high nobles** have some form of hereditary magic, which is why they're nobles. It usually comes out only when they're **enraged**. Tovey's makes people near him go numb. (canon, user) Nobles tend to be **big**: Tor is "too small to be a noble." (canon, user)
- **Rolph's family is royal.** Hidden from Tor, hinted to the reader. (canon, user)
- **The special school** is a **spy school, with a side of assassination training**. Trice and Sara attend it. **Not revealed in chapter two**: on the page, only the black uniforms, the fighting, and a department head. (canon, user) It also **trains likely assassination targets**, nobles and high merchants' children; both are true, and in chapter three Rolph tells Tor only the second. (canon, user, October 17, 2026)
- **The law on striking nobles.** Striking a noble is a **high crime**, punishable by **death**; Wensa calls assaulting a member of the peerage **treason**. (canon, user, chapter three)
- **Declared friendship.** Among nobles, **declared friends are rare** and treated **very differently** from everyone else; a declared friend becomes a close ally. Tor's "what are friends for?" to Tovey counts as one, with Trice's help. Tor's own belief, from home: **"there are no debts between friends."** (canon, user, chapter three) What the custom obliges, and how it's declared formally, is not yet defined.
- **Nobles intermarry** for alliances, so many are related; Trice and Tovey are **first cousins**. (canon, user, chapter three)
- **Noble titles:** a duke's daughter is a **Ducharina** (Trice: the Ducharina Patricia Alyson Morgan). (canon, user, chapter three)
- **The rage epidemic.** Lately, **nobles have been falling into combat rages more and more often**, and nobody knows why. People fear a plot. **There is one**, but it's different from what anyone expects and won't come clear for a long time. (canon, user, chapter three) Not yet defined; don't let drafts commit to a cause.
- **Austra** is a foreign power: Wensa suspects Tor of being an **Austran spy**. (canon, user, chapter three) Its relation to Gallaia and Noram is not yet defined.
- **Royal guards** guard the royal family, sometimes under cover (Wensa); they "don't play by the same rules as everyone else" (Rolph). (canon, user, chapter three)
- **Production scale:** most magic manufacturers **can't make a hundred devices in a day**, across the whole company (Tovey). Tor made fifty in one night. (canon, user, chapter three)
- **Culture clash:** Tor's backwoods, humble, prudish upbringing vs. the cosmopolitan, aggressive, libertine culture of his noble friends. **Our book replaces the libertine side** with flirtation as a non-physical game of wit (see `outline/series-direction.md`).

## The school

- The school is the **Lairdgren School**.
- A **tower bell** by the main courtyard rings the hours day and night; time is counted in bells.
- Students wear **brown trousers and tunics**. There is a **student store** and **isolation chambers** for building work.
- **Combat class is mixed** boys and girls.
- Daily **meditation classes** train lighter versions of the building trance; taught by **Doris**. (teacher canon, user)
- Tor's instructors: his **building instructor**, who seems to be a **special projects** instructor and has an office; **Doris** (meditation), who barely speaks and brings a new item each day for the class to focus on; and a **magic theory** instructor (unnamed). (canon, user)
- Tor's morning: building instructor (early), meditation, magic theory, then lunch in the cafeteria; combat in the afternoon. (canon, user)
- **Afternoons are combat instruction**, led by head instructor **Kolb**. (canon, user)
- Students eat in a **cafeteria**. (canon, user)
- Instructors **don't praise work explicitly**, even when they think it's top-notch. Whether that's school culture or Tor not hearing it is left ambiguous. (canon, user)
- The school week has **rest days** with no classes. Tor's recovery day in chapter one is one. (assumed; decided by the user for our book) Students do their laundry then, which is why the washing house is crowded. (invented; accepted by the user)
- **Soap** is a gray, cracked lump that smells of ash; Tor buys his (likely at the student store). (invented; accepted by the user)
- There is a **practice yard** for combat, with a **pell** (a great log buried halfway in the ground) for striking practice. (canon, user, chapter two)
- Students of the **special school** wear **black** instead of brown. Trice and Sara are among them; they have a department head of their own. What the special school is has not been stated. (canon, user, chapter two)
- The **nearest town** is about **five miles** from the school by road; Kolb sends students to run there and back, **ten miles** in all. (canon, user, chapter two)
- The **student store** sells **copper squares** (five inches on a side) and **acid** for etching. (canon, user, chapter two)
- **Express mail** reaches the capital in **three days**. (canon, user, chapter two)
- The **queen's birthday** is celebrated; Tor wonders whether students get the day off. (canon, user, chapter two)
- There is a **pub** students can go to (Rolph comes back from it late). (canon, user, chapter two)
- There are school **workrooms** where students can build, apart from the isolation chambers. (canon, user, chapter two)
- There is a **washing area** with washing tubs; the laundry scene and the instructor's test both happen there. (canon, user)
- **Staff, chapter three:** a **dean of students**, Campbell (`characters/supporting/campbell.md`); **Wensa**, who teaches accounting (`characters/supporting/wensa.md`). (canon, user)
- **The administration building** has a **warded basement**, with a room where the school holds the hearing after the courtyard. See `locations/the-administration-basement.md`. (canon, user, chapter three)
- **The school has holding cells**, or Wensa expects it to. (canon, user, chapter three)
- **The special academy** (the special school), as Rolph explains it: training for people who might be **targets of assassination**, nobles and the **children of high merchants**. Trice has been there for **years**. Tor had assumed it trained spies and assassins. (canon, user, chapter three) It's **both** (user, October 17, 2026): it trains people who might be assassination targets, and in practice that means spycraft and the means to kill. Rolph tells Tor the polite half. See World, above.

## How magic works

- **Copying** from a template: about 1 in 60 people can do it, even untrained. Copy work runs the realm's infrastructure.
- **Novel builds** are rare; even builder students seldom try; professionals may need dozens of attempts.
- Advanced building like Tor's is so rare that producing **even one such builder from a whole cohort** counts as an amazing accomplishment for the school. Everyone at the school knows this instinctively; a student doing it is in no danger of expulsion for missed classes, and nobody thinks that's worth saying. (canon, user)
- The builder must understand the idea very clearly. A **sigil on a focus** (cheap ink on unfinished wood) is a mental map that holds focus.
- Building takes a **deep working trance** that can last days and scrambles perception afterward.
- **Ink drying unusually fast** is a good sign.
- **A build's value depends on its material and how long it lasts.** One on cheap wood and paint is worth much less than one on good material. Tor's drying field, on cheap wood and paint, is still worth **30 gold**, a significant sum. (canon, user, chapter one)
- A student's novel build (the prototype) **must be turned in for testing**. (canon, user, chapter one)
- **Vitality fields.** All living things have some kind of vitality field; the more complex the living thing, the stronger and more complex its field. **Human vitality fields naturally resist simple fields**; damaging a person directly takes more focused, specific effort. (canon, user)
- **Distinguishing living from non-living tissue** is one of the fundamental parts of a builder's education. (canon, user)
- **Using the drying field:** Tor starts it by touching the center of the sigil. Water wells out of the weave in beads and runs off the cloth onto the floor; the cloth is left dry and slightly stiff. Clothes on people within range dry too (Davie's wet sleeves). (invented; accepted by the user)
- **Copying in practice:** Tor and Rolph's door lock is a copy Tor made from the lock on Rolph's lockbox. It identifies the room's occupants and magically lifts a simple door bar on the inside. (canon, user, chapter one)
- **Shields and cutters.** Magical **shield field devices** protect against blows; a strong blow can kill someone who has neither armor nor a shield. A **cutter** is a magic weapon: a **four-foot**, **infinitely thin blade** that passes through any physical thing **without resistance**, cutting it apart. The blade **extends instantly from the handle** and is gone when not in use, so it needs **no sheath**, and it never needs wiping. Kolb slices a pell clean through with one. (canon, user, chapter two) Armor is useless against it; only a field can matter.
- **The sigil is a guide, not the magic.** The builder imbues the object with the field's pattern; the sigil helps him keep his place and makes clear what the object is for. (canon, user, chapter two)
- **Metal focuses.** Designs can be **etched in copper with acid**. A drying field on a metal plate is worth more than one on wood (Sara: 20 gold vs. 12). (canon, user, chapter two)
- **Copy work is slow and tiring.** Tor copies the drying field in **batches of ten**, a couple of hours per batch, and it gets harder to concentrate as the work bores him and the night gets late. (canon, user, chapter two)
- **Tor can throw out a raw field** under stress, with no device: it pushes everything away from his body, blocks a knife and powder, and also blocks air. Sara takes it for a shield device. (canon, user, chapter two) It **looks like a noble's hereditary field**, though Tor is too small to be a noble, and **he controls it on purpose**, which very few people have ever shown. (canon, user) Nobody explains this to Tor in chapter two.
- **Wards on attention.** Mental protection **wards** can be laid on a place or an object (a corridor, its doors, a room, **chairs**) so that the mind won't stay on it: a corridor feels empty, a door can't be focused on, people in warded chairs can't be seen. An **access token** pressed to a **pad** beside a warded door makes it briefly focusable. A **deep trance** let Tor see through them, which surprised the dean and alarmed Wensa. (canon, user, chapter three) How rare that is isn't stated.
- **Tovey's rage includes flight.** In his rage he **levitates and flies**; when the rage ends, so does the flight, and he has to walk home. (canon, user, chapter three) Whether flight is part of his hereditary field, or of rage fields generally, is not defined.
- **New magic as prestige:** among nobility, having new magic before anyone else is a mark of significant prestige. (canon, user, chapter one)

### Magic details from the chapter two drafts (invented; accepted by the user)

- **The cutter** is carried as a bare **cord-wrapped hilt** a hand and a half long, with a line of small regular marks around the guard. Kolb wears his on his belt every day; Rolph took it for a tool for three years. The four-foot blade is dull gray, simply there when wanted and gone when not; seen edge-on it vanishes. It cuts in silence, without slowing, and can be swung one-handed like a switch.
- **The drying field's sigil** has a hook at the lower left, a doubled crossing near the center, and something like a fishbone along the top edge.
- **Etching:** the pattern is drawn on the copper in a waxy resist; the acid fizzes on bare metal and leaves the lines; the squares are rinsed in a jug. The smell is like a copper kettle boiled dry, or a cut penny. Faults: acid creeping under the wax (a wide, furry line), or a corner of the pattern not taking.
- **How Tor copies:** hand flat over the square, eyes half closed, lips moving; he traces the etched line with one finger like a child tracing letters, and it still works when his finger wanders off the line. "It's like a recipe. You don't need it to make the bread. It just helps if you're doing forty loaves."
- **How Dorgal was taught:** build the idea clear and whole, then press it into the board "through the sigil, the way you press a seal into wax." His perfect painted copy, drawn from memory, does nothing.
- **Trice's device:** a rod about the length of her hand with a thumb catch; each press bucks and pushes the target back several steps with a dull *whump*. It has a few charges. Built "for getting away from someone." Unnamed and unexplained on the page.
- **Tovey's rage, from outside:** blue-white sparks crackle off his hair and shoulders and light the stones; his face goes empty. His numbing field feels like cold going through you "like water through cloth"; hands go dull as if through three pairs of gloves, legs go first; it's worst at whoever he looks at and eases an inch when he looks away.

## Tor's first project

A field on a pine square, a hand across, painted in cheap blue house paint, that makes water leave cloth and fibers in an area, so laundry dries in moments. He spent **thirty hours** in trance (duration decided by the user) from late on fourth day to dawn on the rest day, missing all of fifth day's classes, including one afternoon of combat (timing invented to fit); it is untested at the opening. He first tests it at the laundry, about a third of the way into chapter one (see `outline/chapters.md`). The user describes the focus as cheap wood and **paint**; earlier notes said ink. (canon, user)

## Tor's second project (idea only, chapter one)

A **food drying field**: the drying field adapted to fruit and vegetables, against crop losses. Prompted by Rolph; sketched the same day and dreamed about that night. (canon, user)

## Tor's third project: the shield (chapter two)

Set by Kolb: a **shield** that withstands **both** a lethal blow and a cutter for **at least twenty minutes**, due in **two weeks**. Kolb will test it on Tor himself. Tor's raw field in sparring shows it can work, and that it must **let air in but keep dust out**. (canon, user) His first principle, from watching a carter's knife cut a cabbage stalk: a blow is a weight that can be spread, but an edge is a line that can't, so the field should **turn** a cut rather than stop it. (invented; accepted by the user)

**Tor's field, seen from outside** (invented; accepted by the user): sand slides away from him in a ring with a hiss; Trice is pushed back an arm's length; the knife stops a hand's breadth from his throat; thrown powder clings to the outside in the shape of an egg. He uses it once and not again that afternoon.
