## Profile

- **Role:** Tor's closest friend and roommate; supposedly from a merchant family. His mother, Connie, takes an interest in Tor. (Connie and roommate are canon; the rest is assumed)
- **Want:** To be the one who protects Tor and shows him how the world really works.
- **Need:** To find a talent or purpose that is his own, not borrowed from his family or his friend.
- **Internal tension:** Genuine love for Tor vs. envy of how easily Tor surpasses everyone. He'd die before admitting the second.
- **External tension:** He hides his family's real standing, and isn't sure how much of the friendship was arranged. He suspects Connie's warmth toward Tor is partly strategy, and he's not sure he minds.
- **Hidden aspect:** His true family is royal (canon, user). The reader is hinted toward it early, as dramatic irony, while Tor doesn't see it.

## Reaction rules

- **Tor drifts into a build mid-conversation** → waits, amused, and doesn't take offense. (canon, user)
- **Tor stuck in a social situation he can't handle** → arrives, takes over the conversation, and gets him out. (canon, user: the end of the laundry scene, chapter one)
- **Tor insulted** → steps physically in front of him, big and grinning, before anyone else reacts.
- **Tor achieves something huge** → celebrates loudest in public; goes quiet for a while in private.
- **Asked about his own magic or future** → jokes, changes the subject, or starts a fight.
- **Family politics surface** → plays dumb, but catches every detail and reports it to Tor later.

## Voice

- **Diction:** casual, nicknames for everyone ("Little Tor," "Baker"). Merchant talk on the surface, with noble manners slipping out.
- **Rhythm:** fast, overlapping, answers his own questions.
- **Tics:** teasing Tor about his prudishness; claps shoulders; "Trust me"; asks what Tor's builds would sell for.
- **Never:** speaks formally unless he's truly angry. Formality from Rolph is a red flag.
