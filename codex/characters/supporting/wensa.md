# Wensa, accounting instructor and royal guard

Wensa is the one adult at the school who takes Tor exactly as seriously as he takes himself: as an unknown, and therefore a danger.

## Facts

- **Stays at the school over the summer**; the palace has plenty of royal guards of the same mind (user, October 25, 2026).
- **Rolph's accounting instructor** at the school. (canon, user, chapter three) She is the accounting professor earlier notes called **Miriam**, a placeholder name (user, October 17, 2026).
- **Secretly a royal guard**, sent as the **lead bodyguard for Rolph**. She has to keep her cover as much as she can, but her first duty is to keep Rolph **physically** safe. She doesn't care who his friends are. (canon, user, chapter three)
- **Present in the warded room** after the courtyard fight, with Trice and the headmaster. (canon, user, chapter three)
- **Sees Tor pierce the wards** on the room and the chairs, and from then on reads him as an **unknown, dangerous person**. (canon, user, chapter three)
- **Raises the law:** Tor assaulted a member of the peerage, which is **treason**. (canon, user, chapter three)
- **Undeterred** when Tovey and Trice clear Tor: demands he be **removed from the school** out of caution, **reveals she's a royal guard**, draws a weapon that looks like a **cutter hilt**, and demands Tor be put in a **holding cell** until people come to their senses. Seeing Tor fall into Kolb's guard, she **readies to attack**. (canon, user, chapter three)
- Her case, as Tor reports it: he might be an **Austran spy**, or trying to get Trice killed with some magic that makes nobles angry. (canon, user, chapter three)
- Behaves very oddly for an accounting instructor, which is how Tor sees her: he finds her suspicious too. (canon, user, chapter three)
- **Tor believes she tries to murder him** in book 1. She may never actually attempt it (user, October 17, 2026); earlier notes said she "tried to kill Tor." Chapter three is where his belief starts, and chapter four's test feeds it.

- **From the chapter three drafts** (invented; accepted by the user): neat, sharp-faced, gray in her hair, a plain dark dress, ink on the second finger of her right hand. Sits with her weight forward and a hand by her belt. Has held her commission **nineteen years**. Teaches accounts to the third years in the cold room on the second floor; marks Rolph's sums with small neat ticks and never looks at his face for long. "I would like it noted that I objected."

- **Chapter four** (canon, user, October 17, 2026): **follows Tor around**: eats in the cafeteria when he does, is always close at hand, and is especially close when he tests ideas for the shield. When Tor comes back into the dining room and asks her to come out and explain to Dorgal and his men why she's following him, **she does**. Tor introduces her **as a royal guard**, aloud, so **her cover is blown** to Dorgal, Marco and the black-eyed man. What passes between them is never shown. (canon, user; the introduction's wording, user, October 17, 2026)
- **Chapter four, the test** (canon, user): attacks Tor's shield with three royal-guard **rods** almost before he can activate it (an explosion, a force that bows a wall, fire); **ends the fire herself** (user, October 17, 2026). Counters Kolb's "it was meant for a pell" with: a builder needs faith in his equipment. Seems **happy**, probably because the shield could protect Rolph (user, October 17, 2026). **Her cover is gone** after this (user, October 17, 2026).
- **Chapter four, after the test** (user, October 18, 2026): still teaches accounts, and still shadows Tor, now openly as a guard.
- **From the chapter four drafts** (invented; pending the user's review): her ledger is brown calf, ruled in red; she carries one like it as cover and writes nothing in it for nine days. Waits on the bench outside meditation, the one class she can't follow Tor into. Checks his purchases with the storekeeper. Once saw a cutter used on a man at a door, and pitch come down from a gatehouse. Notes, without comment, what Rolph eats. When the design is finished she writes her first line: *Ask for one.* Scene 9: sits on a rush-seated hall chair at the end of Tor's corridor through the four-day build, marking first-year accounts in pencil, day and night.
  - Scene 2: for the first time in nine days she stays in her seat when Tor leaves supper, because Rolph is still eating. Looks at the black-eyed man first and longest.
  - Scene 5: after the test she stops carrying the ledger and wears the hilt openly; students step round her. Watches from the far bank as Tor walks out of the pond, and from the gate as the templates leave.

## Profile

- **Role:** Rolph's hidden bodyguard; the chapter-three antagonist who is right to be careful and wrong about Tor. (first canon; second our reading)
- **Internal tension:** Her cover (an ordinary accounting instructor) vs. her duty (stop threats to Rolph, now). In chapter three, duty wins, and the cover is gone in front of a roomful of people. (canon seed; framing invented)
- **External tension:** A student she can't explain, whom everyone else in the room defends: a count, a duke's daughter, the head combat instructor and the headmaster. (canon, user)
- **Reaction:** Something she can't explain gets near Rolph → treats it as a threat until proven otherwise, whatever the social cost. (canon seed)
- **Reaction:** Someone shows training she didn't know they had → takes it as confirmation. (canon: Tor's stance, chapter three)
- **Hidden aspect:** Whom she guards. She says "royal guard" and never whose. (canon, user; hidden from Tor) Rolph suspected his family had someone near him but not who; Tor's news tells him. (user, October 17, 2026)
- **Voice:** (invented) Exact and procedural; names the law, the charge and the remedy, in that order. No jokes, no titles she doesn't owe. Never raises her voice.
  - "The boy struck a peer of the realm. The word for that is treason. I would like it written down."
