# Davie, fifth in line to a county

The first minor character the reader meets: a young noble discovering, at the laundry, that he has never had to do anything for himself.

## Facts

- Named **Davie** (canon, chapter one; earlier notes called him "David").
- **Fifth in line** to a county. (canon) That his father is Count B, of House B, is (assumed).
- At school he has to manage **on his own for the first time**, without the servants who did everything for him at home. (canon)
- He meets Tor about **a third of the way into chapter one**, when the drying field is first tested. (canon, from the user)
- He **doesn't know laundry takes soap.** He can't understand why his water won't suds up when he can see Tor's doing it. Tor teaches him basic laundry and lends him soap. (canon, from the user)

- Gets the first of Tor's crooked copper drying fields: "He can't keep his shirts dry, he's got no idea, it's awful." (chapter two) (invented; accepted by the user)

## Profile

- **Role:** First minor character on stage; the first real person Tor's magic touches, and so the first link in the book's engine (`outline/chapters.md`). Later, possibly the school's best source of gossip. (gossip role assumed)
- **Internal tension:** Grumpy resentment that someone of his birth has to wash his own clothes vs. fear that everyone will find out he doesn't know how. (canon traits; framing assumed)
- **External tension:** The school's social order. A noble who can't do what any servant can is an easy target. Possibly also a father who treats a fifth son as expendable. (first canon-adjacent; second assumed)
- **Reliable reaction:** Caught not knowing something → gets grumpy and blames the water, the tubs or the school before he'll admit ignorance. (from the user's chapter-one notes)
- **Voice:** Complaints aimed at the world, not at himself; a noble's expectation that things will be done *for* him, with nobody left to do them. Asks for help sideways, as if it were the other person's idea. (invented)
  - "Who designed these tubs? Nobody could get anything clean in these."
  - "I'm not asking how. I'm asking whether *you* happen to know how."

## Reconciling with earlier notes

The earlier profile (all assumed) made him a wry, relaxed gossip ("Nobody poisons the fifth son. Very restful."). Chapter one shows him frustrated, grumpy and afraid of mockery instead. One way to keep both (invented): he fears mockery because he knows exactly how gossip works at the school. He'll become a gossip later, once he's on safer ground and has friends to trade stories with. Until the user confirms the gossip role, chapter one should use only the canon traits.
