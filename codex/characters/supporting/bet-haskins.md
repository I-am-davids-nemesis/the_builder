# Bet Haskins, keeper of the isolation chambers

All details (invented; accepted by the user, October 9, 2026). She first appears in chapter one's opening, where Tor comes out of his trance.

## Description

Bet Haskins is a small, square, gray woman with pale, unbothered eyes, "like water in a pail." She has kept the isolation chambers for thirty-one years, long enough to have been a girl at the school when she first touched the warm stones at the foot of the bell tower. She wears an apron, and a gray shawl when she comes up from below, and carries a lamp, sometimes unlit, out of habit. She lives under the old wing and comes up for meals and little else.

Her days have a fixed rhythm. She gets up off her stool at every bell, slides back a chamber's shutter and puts her eye to it, closes it, and chalks a mark on the slate by the door. She counts the marks with her thumb and wipes a slate clean with the heel of her hand. She sits on her bench opposite the doors; when something astonishes her, she sits down on it and stays there. She knocks softly before going in. She watches closely and long (a brush, a sleeping boy, a line of floating plates) and lingers at a shutter longer than she needs to. Her face mostly does "nothing at all."

She speaks in few words, plain, dry and practical, often to nobody: "Well, then." "You'll want feeding." "Sign the slate." "He won't eat that." "Go to bed, young man." "Then you come and get me, and don't run on the stair." She keeps what she sees to herself on the spot, and then finds she has something to say at breakfast.

## Facts

- Keeper of the **isolation chambers** for **thirty-one years**. Unnamed in the camera-mode draft.
- Looks in on each occupant **every bell**, day and night, and keeps a **tally on a slate** hung by each door. Students chalk their own times in and out. Tor's entry: *BAKER, T. — In: late, fifth day* / *Out: first bell, rest day*, followed by *Thank you*.
- Knows the bell intimately: which stroke is the clapper and which the echo off the chapel roof.
- Can read students by how they come in: the ones out in an hour with nothing, the ones who sit a day and a night for a half-working lamp-square.
- Knows the **fast-drying sign**: she has seen paint dry like that "perhaps a dozen times" in thirty-one years.
- As a girl she once touched the poured courses at the bottom of the bell tower, and they felt warmer than they should.
- Tor thanks her every time he signs the slate; no other student ever has.
- **From the chapter four drafts** (invented; pending the user's review): checks Tor through the door shutter every bell and chalks a mark; tells Rolph "He won't eat that" and "Go to bed, young man"; says the ink is drying on him, a dozen times in thirty-one years; tells the staff table at breakfast, two days running. Scene 9: comes up to the dormitory for Tor's four-day build with an unlit lamp "out of habit"; "Don't pull him"; water every bell; broth, warm, not hot, salt in it; "Then you come and get me, and don't run on the stair."

## Profile

- **Role:** The school's quiet witness to building. She sees more novel work begin and end than any master, and the school's gossip about it often starts with her.
- **Internal tension:** Pride in what she's seen vs. the conviction that nobody listens to a keeper.
- **External tension:** Her place is low. Masters and students alike treat her as part of the furniture of the undercroft.
- **Reaction:** Sees something remarkable → tells no one directly; then finds she has something to say at breakfast. She's how the school learns, by the rest-day breakfast, that Baker came out with a board and went to the washing house.
- **Reaction:** A student says something foolish (e.g., Tor fearing expulsion) → says nothing.
- **Voice:** Few words, plain and practical; talks to herself as much as to anyone.
  - "Well, then."
  - "You'll want feeding."
  - "Sign the slate."
