# The building instructor (name TBD)

Tor's **building instructor**, who seems to be a **special projects** instructor. Tor brings him the drying field for testing on the morning after the laundry, at the start of chapter one's last third. (canon, user)

## Facts

- Has an **office**; Tor arrives there early to show him the field. (canon, user)
- When Tor hands over the drying field, he takes Tor to the washing area, **dunks him in a washing tub**, and shows that the field dries Tor's clothes without harming Tor. (canon, user)
- His **stated** reason, which the reader hears: the field has to be shown safe for use around people. (canon, user)
- His **unstated** reason, which the reader **never** learns: he was already reasonably confident it was safe, because Tor told him he'd tested it, and students are rarely as careful around new magic as they should be. (canon, user)
- The scene is played for comedy. (canon, user)
- His confidence rests on basic theory any builder learns: human vitality fields resist simple fields (`canon-notes.md`). The page may state the theory elsewhere; it never links it to him. (canon, user)
- **Keeps the device** after testing it, and sends Tor to classes with an instruction: explain the previous week's absence as **a building exercise for his class**. (canon, user) It's cover Tor didn't ask for, given as an order; the consideration under it isn't spelled out.
- **Never comments on the quality of Tor's work.** Like the other instructors, he assumes it's top-notch and doesn't say so. (canon, user)

- **Chapter two:** pulls Tor out of building class to meet **Douglas Sorvey**, a merchant he brought in to "do business" with Tor, in his office. Is clearly **unhappy** when Tor turns Sorvey down. Suggests it isn't a bad idea to **pay off whoever does the testing**; Tor hears a sensible suggestion, not a request for a bribe. (canon, user) Whether he's mercenary, or nudging Tor toward a safe buyer, is open; it's the first time his motive looks plainly bad. (invented)

## Profile

Personality details (assumed) unless labeled.

- **Role:** Master of the craft and a purist about method; oversees students' special projects, so novel builds like Tor's come to him.
- **Internal tension:** Pride in tradition vs. awe at Tor's impossible, untraditional work.
- **External tension:** Must certify Tor's projects, putting his reputation on the line.
- **Hidden aspect:** He's more careful and considerate than he looks. His methods look rough or reckless from outside; his reasoning is sound and he doesn't explain it. (canon, user)
- **Reaction:** Needs to test a new build's safety → tests it the most direct way available, without explaining, and lets people think what they like. (canon seed: the dunking)
- **Reaction:** Sees an unorthodox design → forbids it, then asks to see it again privately.
- **Voice:** Technical, grudging, precise.
  - "That shouldn't hold. Why is it holding? Don't touch it."
