# Varley

The younger princess, who blushes at everything except a dinner table. Every scene with her should test whether she wants Tor's things or Tor's company, and whether she can tell the difference.

## Facts

- **Princess**, Rolph's younger sister, **16**; Karina is 18 (user, October 25, 2026). Daughter of King Richard and Queen Constance (`../rolph.md`).
- **Given name Veronica**; "Varley" is the family's name for her, and she gives it to people she allows (invented; accepted by the user, November 8, 2026). "Veronica's for cards and for being told off" (chapter 10, scene 2 draft).
- **Two of Tor's cooling plates** came to her through Karina, who nearly kept all four (user's notes, chapter 10, scene 2).
- **Wants to fly** (user, November 8, 2026). The council hasn't forbidden her as it has the king, but there are no rigs to spare after the army and the city guard. Tor promises her the first left-handed rig, earmarked, and a shield; she offered to pay (chapter 10, scene 2).
- **Tutored at the palace** (user's notes, chapter 10, scene 2).
- **Good at a table** (user, November 8, 2026): taught by her mother at nine to "go round," and she talks to everyone near her in turn. She knows the court by sight and by gossip.
- **Holds the viewpoint** for the meal (chapter 10, scene 2) and the fight (scene 3). She sees Tor's gold sparks and hears Lady Arden call them "the old gold"; she half remembers a tutor's lesson about the first king's line and can't finish it.
- **From the chapter 5 and 9 drafts:** "sixteen and watchful" at the first dinner; slaps Karina's elbow for asking Tor about his rage ("You can't *ask* people that!"); comes to his bedside red to the ears and offers ice from the cellars and the good pillows; goes "the color of a radish" when her mother steers her out.
- **Chapter 10, scene 4** (user's notes, November 8–9, 2026): brings Tor the after-rage remedy ("drink it fast"); goes red when the go-between fishes about Tor's station (she saw the gold), and says nothing; proposes the knighthood (three heroic acts; the river and the midden count); argues against Karina that saving Priscilla counts.
- **From the chapter 10, scene 2–3 drafts** (invented; pending the user's review): her tutors are a geometry master on Tuesdays, Master Abelard for history (smells of cloves), a harp mistress and a theory man on Fridays. She tells Tor that Karina lay among four plates "like a dragon." She tries Karina's shy chin-dip and lowered lashes and does them badly; Tor doesn't notice either. She heads off his flirting older neighbors with the rule that proposals go through her mother, and tells the table that Karina asked for Tor at breakfast ("*Certainly not, darling, pass the marmalade*"). The left-handed rig, said aloud at table, gets passed down the room.

## Profile

All (invented; pending the user's review) unless labeled.

- **Role:** The royal child nobody watches, and so the one who sees. Through her the reader sees the court reacting to Tor before it has decided what to think.
- **Want vs. need:** Wants to fly, and to be first at something before Karina. Needs to be taken seriously as herself, not as the third child; Tor, who talks to her as an equal and won't take her money, is the first to do it.
- **Internal tension:** She disapproves of Karina's way of getting things, and she does it too, and she knows it.
- **External tension:** The third child, outshone by an heir and a sister two years older; a council that decides who flies; a mother who seats her on purpose.
- **Hidden aspect:** How much of tonight she tells, and to whom. She saw the gold and heard Lady Arden. (Open.)
- **Reaction rules:**
  - **Someone breaks manners** → corrects them on the spot, loudly, then goes red. (from the chapter 9 draft)
  - **At a table** → steady, practiced, goes round; shyness only comes back when someone speaks to her as herself.
  - **Caught doing a Karina** → her ears go, and she can't stop them.
  - **Something frightening** → backs away with everyone else, and keeps watching when they look away.
- **Where they break pattern:** In a crisis, or with something she wants badly enough, she comes forward anyway, red to the ears: "but she'd come" (chapter 9).
- **Voice:** quick, eager, a little too much; tumbling clauses joined with "and"; gossip told as fact; "Mama," "Papa," "Alphie." Says the thing before she can stop it, then goes on anyway. **Never:** sulks in public, or lets anyone be rude to a guest.
- **Sample lines:**
  - "I'm *sixteen*."
  - "I'll pay for it. I've got my own money, you know."
  - "You'll have to write to Mama. Everybody does."
- **Series slice:** Book 1 makes her Tor's friend in her own right, with a rig earmarked for her. It leaves open whether she flies, what she makes of the old gold, and whether she grows into a better player than Karina.
