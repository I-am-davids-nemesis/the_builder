# Karina

Karina is a princess who has learned that the right object in the right hands is worth more than a title, and every scene with her should test whether she ever wants anything for its own sake.

A main character from chapter 5 on, with more plot to come later in the series (user, November 1, 2026).

## Description

Karina is tall, like all her family, and taller than Tor. She has her father's red hair, her mother's dark coloring and poise, and thin, fine features (user). The manuscript itself shows little of her face. It shows her clothes and her manner: a summer dress that leaves her arms and the back of her neck bare, with something gathered at the shoulders that Tor can't name; a blue dress at dinner. In the heat she has her maid sponge her neck with rosewater, which does no good.

In the picture she has a mass of tight, curling red hair pinned up at the back with loose curls falling about her temples and ears. Her skin is a warm dark brown, with a faint flush on the cheekbones, and her features are fine and narrow: a straight nose, a small mouth, dark eyes under finely drawn brows. She wears small pearl drop earrings. Her gown is sage-green silk with an all-over floral embroidery in gold and dusky pink, cut square at the neck and gathered at the shoulders with embroidered flowers pinned to the sleeve caps; a band of the same embroidery runs at the waist. Her hands rest folded in her lap.

She moves gracefully and never hurries. She sits, arranges her skirt and folds her hands in her lap while thinking very fast. Every gesture can be a performance: a hand to her mouth, eyes let go wide, a head tipped, warmth let into her eyes "and put where she wanted it," and a shy dip of the chin she has practiced in a glass since she was twelve. She enters without knocking, so she can always claim the wrong door, and comes back to put her head round it. When she's pleased her eyes go bright, and the corner of her mouth twitches. When her voice comes out flat she hears it and fixes it at once. Others read her the same way: her mother sees "a girl doing sums," Rolph a cat in a dairy, and Tor, through her field, something quick and bright and pleased with itself, like a cat that has found the cream.

### Look

A tall young woman of eighteen, taller than a small young man beside her, with a mass of tight red curls pinned up loosely, warm dark skin and thin, fine features. She usually wears a light summer dress that leaves her arms and the back of her neck bare, with gathers at the shoulders; for dinner, a dress of blue. A small pearl drop in each ear.

## Profile

All (invented; pending the user's review) unless labeled.

- **Role:** The royal family's most worldly child, and the first person at court to treat Tor as currency. Through her the reader sees what Tor's work is worth in the capital's social market.
- **Want:** Standing among the young women of the Hill: to be first, to own what nobody else owns, to be the one everyone asks. (Favor with her friends, user.)
- **Need:** To value something, or someone, for what it is rather than what it gets her. Tor, who gives her things for nothing, is the first test of that.
- **Internal tension:** She plays the court's game better than anyone her age and enjoys it, *and* she's surprised to find she likes the boy she's playing it on. She tells herself the liking is part of the play.
- **External tension:** A princess's every move is watched and priced. She is younger than Rolph and outshone by him, and her mother sees through her ("Karina is going to be impossible"). Her friends' favor is only as good as her last coup.
- **Hidden aspect:** What she does with the favor she gains: whose circle it is, what it buys, and how far she would trade on Tor's name. **Left open until her later plot** (user, November 1, 2026).
- **With Tor in book 1** (user, November 1, 2026): a **flirtation she enjoys more than she admits**. No romance is possible in book 1 (and nobles marry within three steps).

## Reaction rules

- **Sees something new and rare** → wants it first, and works out who to show it to before she's finished admiring it.
- **Caught out** (someone reads her surprise, as Tor does) → goes still, denies it smoothly, and files the person as dangerous and interesting.
- **Given something freely** → is pleased, and a little uneasy: she doesn't know what's owed. Then she thanks prettily and finds a way to make it look like her idea.
- **Bored** → makes something happen, preferably to someone else.
- **Her brother is in trouble** → teases him in public, defends him in private. (assumed)

## Voice

- **Diction:** light, quick, polished; court phrasing worn lightly; says "Alphie."
- **Rhythm:** a breath of hesitation, practiced, before the line that matters; questions that are really bids.
- **Tics:** a shy dip of the chin; "Nobody has… except…"; the excuse ready before the entrance.
- **Never:** asks directly for what she wants in front of an audience, or admits she's been surprised.

## Series slice

Book 1 introduces her as charming, calculating and a little startled by Tor. It leaves open what her friends' favor is for, how far she'll trade on him, and whether she ever wants anything from him that isn't a thing.
