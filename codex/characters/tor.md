# Tor Baker (Torrance Green Baker)

Tor is a genius who believes he is a nuisance, and every scene with him should test that belief.

## Facts

- Full name **Torrance (or Torrence) Green Baker**; "Tor" becomes the name he's famous under. (canon)
- **20** at the start of our book 1, in his **third year** at Lairdgren. (decided by the user, October 9, 2026) Earlier research put him at about 16 at the start and around 19 by book 6; our book doesn't follow that.
- **Small** compared to almost everyone around him. (canon)
- Poor, a baker's son from the woods around **Two Rivers**. (canon)
- Studies at the Lairdgren School on a scholarship ordered by King Richard Cordes, who doesn't know him. (canon)
- Rarely smiles, keeps to himself, mutters to himself. (canon)
- Once babbled nonsense to test whether people were listening. (canon)
- Oldest sister: **Terlee**. (canon)
- In his first year he asked **Maria** out; she screamed that he was poor, ugly and above his station. He hid in his room for weeks. (canon)
- Believes **all nobles look down on anyone who isn't noble**, and is afraid of high nobles like Tovey, expecting them to lash out at any slight. (canon, user)
- Has hidden **mental controls**, revealed in later books; he isn't who or what he thinks he is. (canon)

## Profile

- **Role:** Scholarship builder student from Two Rivers; the kingdom's hidden piece in a game older than he is. (canon)
- **Want:** To be useful, to not cause trouble, and to make his family proud. (assumed)
- **Need:** To accept that his worth doesn't depend on being useful to others, and to ask who he is, not just what he can make.
- **Internal tension:** Conviction that he is poor, ugly, small and below his station vs. private pride in his intellect and mounting evidence that he is extraordinary. He reconciles them by assuming there's been a mistake. (canon-adjacent)
- **External tension:** Nobles and older powers maneuvering around him; school rules he keeps breaking by accident; a family history that doesn't add up. (canon)
- **Hidden aspect:** The mental controls. His humility, and perhaps his self-image, may have been put there by someone else. (canon, revealed later)

## Reaction rules

- **Praised** → deflects to luck, a teacher, or the materials; changes the subject to the work.
- **Asked for help** → says yes before the sentence ends, then overcommits.
- **Sees that people want something he can make** → silently takes it on as an obligation, without promising anything aloud. (canon: assumes he'll make drying fields for all his schoolmates, chapter one)
- **Someone near him has a small, practical need** → stops what he's doing to help, even when his own work is far more important to him. (canon: he teaches Davie laundry and lends him soap before testing his field)
- **Threatened himself** → goes quiet and polite; fear shows as extra courtesy.
- **A friend threatened** → the meekness vanishes; he solves the problem with frightening, disproportionate competence, then apologizes for the mess.
- **Handed a technical puzzle** → forgets food, sleep, and the people in the room; may sink into a trance lasting days. (canon) Even a passing wish from a friend counts as a puzzle: Rolph's remark about crop losses sends him straight to his desk. (canon, user)
- **Surfaces from a fugue and finds someone waiting** → apologizes for leaving the conversation. (canon, user)
- **Offered hospitality** → hesitates because he doesn't want to be a burden; accepts only if assured nobody minds. (canon: Rolph's invitation home, chapter one)
- **Needs to account for himself** → reaches for a literal, honest technicality rather than the easy story he's been given. (canon seed: he thinks his building trance should count as meditation, so he should be excused from Doris's class)
- **Asleep with a build in his head** → dreams it, wakes, and scribbles notes that make sense only to him. (canon, user)
- **Faced with tradition or an elder's rule** → follows it to the letter. (canon)
- **Facing a high noble** → assumes contempt; goes quiet, extra courteous and careful, as if any slip could provoke a blow. (canon: his fear of Tovey, chapter one)
- **Faced with noble social customs** → embarrassment, literal-mindedness, and a strong wish to be somewhere else.
- **A girl is friendly to him** → assumes it's kindness or a mistake. (rooted in the Maria rejection)
- **Small talk** → tunes out and privately judges it.

## The perception gap

There is a built-in tension between **how Tor thinks people see him and how they actually see him**. (canon, user) He believes nobles despise him; many don't. He believes he's a nuisance; people seek him out. Tovey is the first clear case: Tor fears him; Tovey is curious and wants a gift for his mother. The second is **expulsion**: Tor worries that missed classes could get him expelled, while everyone else knows a builder like him is the school's rare prize and takes his worry as a bad joke. (canon, user) The third is **praise**: his instructors know his work is top-notch and never say so. It's deliberately unclear whether that's the school's culture or whether Tor can't hear praise and doesn't understand that his work is better than average. (canon, user; keep it ambiguous)

Since we don't narrate Tor's thoughts (`AGENTS.md` §3), the gap is shown by contrast: Tor's behavior (fear, apology, distance) set beside what the other person actually says and does. The reader sees both sides; Tor sees one. Each time the reader catches the mismatch is a small dramatic irony, and it should recur all book.

## Voice

- **Diction:** plain, rural, respectful; "sir" and "ma'am" by reflex, even to peers who outrank him.
- **Rhythm:** short hedged sentences that stretch into long, precise run-ons the moment he talks about building.
- **Tics:** "I'm sorry," "I didn't mean to," "It's not really anything"; baking metaphors from home; mutters to himself ("Think, Tor. Focus now.") (canon); dry, self-mocking humor.
- **Never:** boasts aloud (the pride stays private), swears casually, or uses a title as a weapon.

## Sample lines

- "It's not really anything. The pattern just wanted to go that way, so I let it."
- "I'm sorry, sir. I didn't know that was against the rules. I didn't know there were rules about it."
- "Please step back from her. I'd hate for this to get worse." (said quietly, which is the warning)

## Showing him without his inner voice

The original narrates Tor's thoughts; we don't (see `AGENTS.md` §3).

- His **muttering** gives him an audible inner life. Use it sparingly.
- The **days-long trance** shows through the lost bell count, stiffness and hunger, ink dried too fast, or someone finding him.
- His **self-doubt** shows through apologies, deflections, overwork and extra courtesy.
- The **Maria rejection** can surface through gossip, through Rolph, or through Maria herself.
- The **nonsense experiment** works as a comic scene with a witness.

## Series slice

Book 1 answers "Can Tor actually build?" (yes) and "Is he in danger?" (yes). It leaves open who he really is, who arranged his life, and whether his humility is his own.
