# Tovey, son of Count A (name TBD)

Tovey is the noble Tor is most afraid of, and the one with the least reason to be feared. The distance between the two is his story in book 1.

## Facts

- Goes by **Tovey** (canon, chapter one), at least as a first name or nickname. Full name not yet confirmed; earlier notes called him "Clark."
- **Not disdainful of Tor, and not antagonistic.** (canon, user) Earlier notes made him an "honorable antagonist" with class contempt; that is withdrawn.
- **Tor is afraid of him**, believing Tovey might strike out irrationally at any slight. (canon, user) The fear comes from Tor's belief that all nobles look down on commoners, not from anything Tovey has done. See the perception gap in `tor.md`.
- **People follow him.** When Tovey goes to see something, a crowd comes along to see what he's interested in, and they want what he wants. (canon, user, chapter one)
- Addresses Dorgal by surname ("Sorvey"); one word from him ends Dorgal's teasing. Wears a plain dark cloak with a brooch over the school brown. (invented; accepted by the user)
- He comes to the laundry to see how the drying field works and **wants one for his mother**. (canon, user, chapter one) His mother is not yet named.

## Profile

- **Role:** Heir to the county of House A (name TBD), and the student the rest of the school takes its cues from. (heir assumed; social pull canon)
- **Want:** Concrete, small, and personal in chapter one: the drying field for his mother. More broadly, to take care of his family and do right by his position. (first canon; second assumed)
- **Need:** To be seen as a person rather than a rank, by Tor most of all. (invented)
- **Internal tension:** He wants things privately and simply (a gift for his mother), but nothing he does is private: whatever he looks at, the school wants. His interest turns a laundry trick into a market. (invented)
- **External tension:** His father's long rivalry with House B (invented); the school's habit of treating him as a signal; and the fear of a commoner he means no harm to, which he may not even notice. (invented)
- **Hidden aspect:** Whether he knows Tor is afraid of him, and how much it bothers him if he does (invented, undecided); and whatever House A's role in the older plot turns out to be. (Not yet defined.)

## Reaction rules

- **Hears of new magic** → comes to see how it works for himself. (canon: at the laundry, chapter one)
- **Sees something useful** → thinks first of who at home could use it. (canon seed: wants the field for his mother; generalization assumed)
- **Tor is overly formal or flinches** → takes it as courtesy, or doesn't notice; he isn't looking for fear because he isn't threatening anyone. (invented; feeds the perception gap)
- **Tor breaks etiquette** → corrects him plainly, as he would any peer. Tor hears it as a threat. (assumed)
- **Someone cheats or mocks** → stops it, briefly and without heat. (assumed)
- **Loses to Tor** → formal acknowledgment, no excuses; then trains twice as hard. (assumed)
- **House B's people are involved** → suspects them first, sometimes wrongly. That's his blind spot. (assumed)

## Voice

All (assumed); revisit against the user's reading of chapter one.

- **Diction:** formal, full titles; a noble's ease, not a noble's sneer.
- **Rhythm:** short declaratives; no filler words; comfortable silences that Tor finds frightening.
- **Tics:** addresses Tor as "Scholar Baker"; asks direct questions and expects direct answers.
- **Never:** jokes at someone else's expense, or lies outright.

## Sample lines

- "Scholar Baker. How long does the effect last? My mother keeps a large house."
- "I asked whether it could be bought. Not whether you would be in trouble. Why would you be in trouble?"
- "Enough. He's working."

The second line is the perception gap out loud: Tor's apology answers a threat Tovey never made.

## Series slice

Book 1 narrows the gap: by the end Tor fears Tovey less, though he may not trust that he's right. It leaves open whether he'll break with his father, and what House A's role is in the older plot. (assumed)
