# Dorgal Sorvey

Dorgal is the charming rival builder who needs Tor to be beatable, and gradually learns he isn't.

## Facts

- Full name **Dorgal Sorvey**. (canon, user) Earlier research listed a book 1 bully called "Sorvey"; that is very likely Dorgal, which fits his teasing Tor at the laundry. (assumed; confirm)
- Wears the school brown cut closer than everyone else's; leans in doorways. (invented; accepted by the user)

- Shares a family name with **Douglas Sorvey**, a dangerous merchant who tries to buy Tor's drying fields (chapter two). So the Sorveys are in trade. (canon, user) **Dorgal is Douglas's son.** (canon, user) It isn't obvious to Tor at all, and nobody else mentions it because it's common knowledge. His "sponsor" in the profile is, at least in part, his father. (assumed)

- **From the chapter two drafts** (invented; accepted by the user): lays out three pens and uses the middle one; splits a nib when he sees his father's mark on a card. Knows the Sorvey coachman by name. Teases Tor: "Off to be dunked again, Baker? Do try to float this time." Says "Interesting" to every version of the sand story. Works alone at night in the east-stair workroom on a perfect painted copy of the drying field that does nothing; hides it under a cloth when Tor comes in. Asked if he knows Sorvey: "Everyone knows him." Asked if Sorvey is nice: "He isn't. Particularly." Learns from Tor that the sigil is only a guide. Refuses a crooked copy out of pride, and stays in the workroom as long as Tor does. Afterward traces his own sigil and lets his finger wander off the line.

- **Chapter four** (canon, user, October 17, 2026): **corners Tor after dinner** with two henchmen, **Marco** and an older man with nearly all-black eyes (`supporting/minor-characters.md`). They accuse Tor of having an **older girlfriend on the faculty** (Wensa, who's been following him). Tor fetches Wensa, introduces them to each other, and leaves them to it. **Why:** wounded pride, his own (the workroom night curdled), with a **subtle influence from the black-eyed man** that isn't revealed until much later. (user, October 17, 2026)
- **From the chapter four drafts** (invented; pending the user's review): stopped using the east-stair workroom with the stove after finding Baker's slate on the room next to it every night. Corners Tor in the coat passage between the dining hall and the kitchen yard. Brings up **Maria** ("I heard she screamed the place down"), a line he didn't mean to say and never found funny: the black-eyed man's influence, shown only as Dorgal glancing at him first. Notices Wensa's hilt and thinks of Kolb's.

## Profile

- **Role:** A gifted, well-connected fellow builder student; the class star until Tor arrives. (assumed)
- **Want:** Recognition as the best builder of his generation, and the patronage that comes with it.
- **Need:** To value the craft more than the applause, which Tor, oddly, can teach him.
- **Internal tension:** Admiration for Tor's work vs. resentment of what it does to his own standing.
- **External tension:** A sponsor (house unconfirmed; House B is a strong candidate) who invested in him and expects results.
- **Hidden aspect:** He copies Tor's ideas and hates himself for it. And something is working on him through the black-eyed man, which he may not know himself. (user, October 17, 2026)

## Reaction rules

- **Finds Tor doing something beneath a gentleman** → teases him for it (canon: calls him a washerwoman at the laundry, chapter one).

- **Tor succeeds publicly** → gracious compliment in public; late night in the workshop, trying to replicate it.
- **Tor fails** → visibly relieved, then ashamed of the relief; may quietly help.
- **Offered a shortcut by his sponsor** → takes it the first time, refuses the second. That's his turning point.
- **Flattered** → soaks it up; flattery is the easiest lever on him.

## Voice

- **Diction:** polished, witty, a little theatrical; technical terms used to impress.
- **Rhythm:** balanced sentences with a punchline at the end.
- **Tics:** "My dear Baker"; backhanded compliments; dismissing things as "quaint."
- **Never:** admits he's confused. He says "Interesting" instead.

## Sample lines

- "My dear Baker, it's charming. Quaint, even. Does it do anything?"
- "Interesting. Walk me through the second lattice. Slowly. For the others."
- "I took it from you. The idea. I'm telling you because I'd rather you heard it from me."

## Series slice

Book 1 establishes the rivalry and one moment where Dorgal could betray Tor and doesn't, or does and regrets it. It leaves open his sponsor's hold on him and whether he becomes a true ally.
