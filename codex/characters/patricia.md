# Patricia ("Trice")

Patricia sees the political board more clearly than anyone Tor's age, and she can't decide whether he's a friend or her best move.

## Facts

- Patricia is a common name in Noram. (canon)
- Goes by **Trice**. (canon, user, chapter one) Later-series readers call "Trice" a divisive character.
- Usually found **with Sara**; the two are a pair. (canon, chapter one)
- Wears her hair pinned up close and plain; enters every room without hurrying and without stopping, and crowds make room for her without deciding to. (invented; accepted by the user)
- At the laundry in chapter one, she **gets Sara to estimate** what Tor's drying field is worth. (canon, user)

- Wears **black**: a student of the **special school**, with a department head of her own. Takes Tor's afternoon combat class. (canon, user, chapter two)
- **A skilled fighter, especially at kicks.** Opens sparring with a kick to the groin; overreaches with a showy **spinning wheel kick**, which Tor gets inside. Puts a **knife** to Tor's throat in "unarmed" sparring, then throws a **blinding white powder**. (canon, user, chapter two) Fights dirty and wins ugly; showiness is her weakness. (invented reading)
- Comes to Tor's room with Sara, dressed for an evening, to steer him away from the Sorvey house and toward the Debri house. Delivers the joke that the school rarely kills students because it's bad for enrollment. (canon, user, chapter two; our wording to be written)
- At night she's in the courtyard **fighting Tovey** with a device that makes him stumble back; she's losing, and Tovey knocks her down. (canon, user, end of chapter two) Why she and Tovey are out so late, and what set off her field, is not yet decided; probably shown in a chapter-three flashback. Whatever the reason, **she doesn't know she caused his rage.** (canon, user) Chapter two shows the fight mid-action from her viewpoint, with no cause.

## Profile

- **Role:** A sharp noble student fluent in court maneuvering; Tor's guide to power and occasionally its instrument. (assumed)
- **Want:** Leverage. To be the person who saw Tor's value first.
- **Need:** To trust one person without calculating the return.
- **Internal tension:** Real affection for Tor vs. a worldview in which affection is a liability. She tells herself the fondness is part of the strategy.
- **External tension:** A family that expects her to deliver an advantage, and rivals (Tovey's house, possibly House B) who see Tor as a prize to be claimed.
- **Hidden aspect:** Whose interests she ultimately serves. Even she may not be sure.
- **Hidden from herself: high noble blood.** Trice has a hereditary field, and hers **drives other nobles into a rage**. It triggers rarely enough that she hasn't noticed she's doing it. Something triggered it in the courtyard and sent Tovey into a blind rage against her. (canon, user, October 9, 2026) Her parentage is the secret behind the secret; not revealed in book 1's early chapters. Who her blood comes from is not yet defined.
- **Hidden from Tor: the special school** is a **spy school, with a side of assassination training**. (canon, user) **Not revealed at all yet**: the black uniform, the knife and the powder are all the reader gets.

## Reaction rules

- **Something new and valuable appears** → gets it priced, and knows who to ask. (canon seed: has Sara value the field)
- **New information** → files it, reveals none of it, and asks one more question than seems polite.
- **Tor is naive in public** → rescues him smoothly, then lectures him in private.
- **Caught in a manipulation** → doesn't deny it; explains why it was the right call.
- **Genuinely moved** → gets brisk and changes the subject to logistics.

## Voice

- **Diction:** precise, courtly; vocabulary of trade and debt ("owe," "cost," "position").
- **Rhythm:** clipped statements; rhetorical questions that are really instructions.
- **Tics:** calls Tor "Master Baker" with ironic formality; "Who benefits?" ("Think, Tor" is Tor's own canon self-talk, so it's not hers.)
- **Never:** says "I don't know." She says "Not yet."

## Sample lines

- "Who benefits, Tor? Why would a count's son eat lunch with a baker's boy? Because he wants something. Find out what."
- "Of course I used you. You were useful. That's not the same as not caring."
- "Not yet. Give me a day."

## Series slice

Book 1 establishes that Patricia will protect Tor when it counts. It leaves open whose interests she serves, and whether she'll ever stop calculating.
