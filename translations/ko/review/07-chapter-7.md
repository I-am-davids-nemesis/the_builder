검토해 주셔서 감사합니다. 이 번역은 초벌 번역입니다. 아래는 번역자가 특히 확신하지 못하는 부분들입니다. 각 항목마다 영어 원문, 현재 번역, 그리고 걱정되는 점을 적었습니다. 이 목록에 없는 부분이라도 어색한 곳이 있으면 자유롭게 고쳐 주세요. 말투(존댓말·반말)의 기준은 `glossary.md`에 정리되어 있습니다.

*Thank you for reviewing. This is a first-pass translation. Below are the passages the translator is least sure of, each with the English, the current Korean and the concern. Please also mark anything else that reads awkwardly. The speech-level choices for each character are in `glossary.md`.*

The chapter covers the Ward dinner and the two days after it. Tor recognizes Maria, drinks before the king, and stands inside his shield between the raging Rolph and Count Ward; watching Rolph's line of force, he gets the idea for carrying water to Ford. Ursula is to be married on paper to an empty barony. Next day Tor makes amulets for Karina and the palace kitchen, builds a fountain in his bath and then across the whole water garden, and learns he has never seen the sea. At the beach house he makes a loop of purified seawater, Sara switches it off, and Tor sees that anyone could stop his water line. That evening Trice explains why nobles never refuse anyone, and Rolph says Tor has "had his Doretta." New speakers: Maria, Countess Thorgood (more lines), Ada the cook, Walt the caretaker, Lady Arden. Viewpoints: Tor, Trice, Tor, Rolph (scene 1); Karina, Ada, Tor alone, Rolph, Constance, Rolph (scene 2); Sara, Rolph, Trice, Sara, Trice (scene 3).

## Across the whole chapter

1. **Wylde and Doretta** → 와일드와 도레타. A tale every noble child knows, never told here. Do the names sound like names from a story?
2. **Ward's rage field** ("the muddle") → 어리둥절함, and "like trying to think through wet wool" → 젖은 양털 속에서 생각하려는 것 같지. Is 어리둥절함 too mild for a field that makes a room go slack?
3. ~~**Trice's 당신.**~~ Answered by the author: keep 당신. In the evening talk (scene 3) Trice calls Tor 당신 several times ("당신 얼굴이 잘생겼어요, 토르"). She is earnest and embarrassed, not angry. Does 당신 read that way, or should she use his name or drop the pronoun?
4. **Units.** "six feet behind" → 여섯 자 뒤; "a foot deep" → 한 자; "hundreds of miles" → 천 리도 넘는; "a hundred miles of farms, two hundred" → 사백 리, 팔백 리; "forty yards" → 마흔 걸음 (he counts his paces).

## Scene 1: Dinner with Ward (Tor, Trice, Tor, Rolph)

5. **Maria's insults, remembered.** "*poor*, and *ugly*, and *how dare you*, and *above yourself*" → *가난뱅이*, *못생긴 게*, *감히*, *주제도 모르고*. Should sound like a schoolgirl shrieking.
6. **"No wonder she'd screamed."** → 비명을 지를 만도 했다. Tor's wrong conclusion; should be flat and sad.
7. **"You honor us."** → "자네가 우리를 높여 주는군." The king turns Tor's mistake into an honor. Does it sound like a courtesy, not irony?
8. **"*Clever girl*."** → *영리하네.* Trice's thought about Ursula drinking second.
9. **The queen's velvet voice.** "like velvet laid over a blade" → 칼날 위에 덮어 놓은 벨벳처럼. And "*Ours*… He is ours." → *우리 사람이야.*
10. **Quavel's answer.** "I've been as comfortable as on a spring morning … I have never once said that about the kitchens in summer." → 봄날 아침처럼 편안했습니다 … 여름 부엌을 두고 그런 말을 한 적은 단 한 번도 없습니다. Perfectly judged servant's flattery?
11. **Ward's insults** (reported speech): "a girl of low character" → 품행이 천한 아가씨; "some *schoolboy*" → 웬 *학생 녀석*.
12. **Tor's appeal to the walls.** "*Excuse me!* … Does anyone here know a nobleman? An unmarried one? … Ow. Sorry." → "*실례합니다!* … 여기 누구 귀족 아시는 분 있으세요? 결혼 안 한 분으로요? … 아야. 죄송해요." Polite, absurd, mid-punch. Funny?
13. **"*Howard Turnbull?*"** → "*하워드 턴불은요?*" Countess Thorgood's dry, shaky suggestion; the room nearly laughs. Then "as Howards did" → 하워드들이 늘 그러듯이.
14. **"But that's not *fair*."** → 하지만 그건 *불공평*하잖아요. And the king: "It isn't fair, Master Baker … I'll remember that you said it." → 공평하지 않네, 베이커 선생 … 자네가 그 말을 했다는 걸 기억해 두겠네.
15. **Tor on Wensa.** "I think she wants to kill me. Well. I don't *really* think so. Not really. Probably not. But I think it a bit. Sometimes. In the evenings." → 제 생각에 저를 죽이고 싶어 하시는 것 같아요. 아니. *진짜로* 그렇게 생각하는 건 아니에요. … 그래도 조금은 그렇게 생각해요. 가끔. 저녁에요. The comic rhythm matters.
16. **"Three days. Luncheon. Here."** → "사흘 뒤. 점심. 여기서."
17. **Rolph on the walk back.** "Somebody else is driving." → 딴 놈이 고삐를 잡고 있어. And Tor: "Rolph. We've lived in one room for two years." → "형. 우리 두 해나 한 방에서 살았어요."

## Scene 2: Vacation from the vacation (Karina, Ada, Tor, Rolph, Constance, Rolph)

18. **Tor waking to Karina.** "Princess. Your Highness. … Karina?" → "공주님. 전하. … 카리나 아가씨?"
19. **"Then there's lots of time."** → "그럼 시간 많아요." Tor reassuring Karina that noon is plenty.
20. **"think *right*"** → *알맞게*라고 생각하세요. As in chapters 5 and 6.
21. **Karina's calculated kiss.** "She'd judged it to the inch." → 한 치까지 재어 두었다. "put her face back on" → 얼굴을 도로 썼다.
22. **Ada's switch.** She speaks 반말 to the boy she takes for a garden lad ("뭐 해 줄까?", "가 봐라"), then 하십시오체 when she sees the amulets: "이건 못 받습니다. 도련님. 이건— 이건 그 *마법사*께서—". Does the shift land?
23. **The dried pear.** "it tastes more of pear than a pear does" → 배보다 더 배 맛이 나요.
24. **"Oh, there you are."** → "아, 거기 있었구나." Tor to the water in the bath.
25. **"cats sharpen their claws on each other's furniture"** → 고양이들이 서로의 가구에 발톱을 가는 걸 보는 것 같아.
26. **The closing exchange.** "A holiday from the holiday?" / "A vacation from our summer vacation." → "방학 중에 또 방학이요?" / "여름 방학에서 떠나는 방학이지." The English joke is Rolph restating Tor's phrase more grandly. Does the Korean pair work, or is there a neater one (휴가 / 방학)?
