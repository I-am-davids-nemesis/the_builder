# 제1장 검토 요청 (Chapter one: review notes)

검토해 주셔서 감사합니다. 이 번역은 초벌 번역입니다. 아래는 번역자가 특히 확신하지 못하는 부분들입니다. 각 항목마다 영어 원문, 현재 번역, 그리고 걱정되는 점을 적었습니다. 이 목록에 없는 부분이라도 어색한 곳이 있으면 자유롭게 고쳐 주세요. 말투(존댓말·반말)의 기준은 `glossary.md`에 정리되어 있습니다.

*Thank you for reviewing. This is a first-pass translation. Below are the passages the translator is least sure of, each with the English, the current Korean and the concern. Please also mark anything else that reads awkwardly. The speech-level choices for each character are in `glossary.md`.*

The book is light and funny (danger is real, but the tone isn't grim), and every character should sound distinct: if you covered the names, you should still know who is speaking. The most useful feedback is anything that sounds stiff, translated, too modern, or wrong for the speaker.

## Across the whole chapter

1. **Speech levels.** Does each pairing sound natural? Tovey (a young count) speaks 하오체: does it read as relaxed noble ease, or as a historical-drama (사극) parody? Dorgal (a student noble, about twenty) uses 하게체 and 자네 to Tor: is that believable as affectation from someone his age?
2. **Tor and Rolph.** Tor calls Rolph 형 and speaks 해요체 to him; Rolph speaks 반말. Does this read as warm friendship with Tor's habitual over-politeness, rather than distance?
3. **"Field" as 장(場).** Magic "fields" are 장, with compounds 생명장 (vitality field) and 식별장 (field of discrimination). Is 장 clear to a reader in a magic context, or would 역장, 마법장 or something else read better?
4. **Bells as hours.** The school counts hours by the tower bell: 첫 종 (the first hour), "몇 번째 종이에요?" (what bell is it?), 한 종 동안 (for an hour). Does this read naturally as a timekeeping system?
5. **School words.** 1학년, 3학년, 선배 and 선생님 are modern school vocabulary. Do they suit a fantasy boarding school, or do they pull the reader into a present-day Korean school?

## Scene 1: The opening (Bet Haskins)

6. **Two Rivers → 두물머리.** Tor's home district. 두물머리 is also a real place near Seoul. Is that distracting? Is there a better translated name?
7. **"which stroke was the clapper's"** → 어느 울림이 종 추가 낸 소리이고. Is 종 추 the right word for the clapper inside a Western-style bell?
8. **"She watched it come out wrong, and then come out right, which was worse."** → 셈이 틀리게 나왔다가, 이어서 맞게 나오는 것을 지켜보았다. 맞게 나온 쪽이 더 나빴다. Does the dry humor land?

## Scene 2: The laundry

9. **"The water lay flat and gray and unrepentant."** → 물은 납작하고 잿빛이고 뉘우칠 기미가 없었다. Funny, or just odd?
10. **"or it shrinks to fit a cat"** → 안 그러면 고양이한테 맞을 만큼 줄어들어요. Does the joke work?
11. **Dorgal's tic "Quaint. Very quaint."** → 예스럽군. 아주 예스러워. It should be faintly dismissive. Is there a better word?
12. **Tovey: "You built something. I'd like to see it work." / "Proceed."** → 무언가를 만들었구려. 작동하는 걸 보고 싶소. / 해 보시오. Is this too archaic?
13. **Rolph to the crowd: "Out of the way, if you'd be so kind, there's a love."** → 좀 비켜 주시겠어요, 착하지. He's breezy and mock-polite to fellow students. Does 착하지 sound right, or condescending in the wrong way?
14. **Sara's father: "Price the board first, then the work, then the years. The years are where they cheat you."** → *판값을 먼저 매기고, 그다음 솜씨, 그다음 세월. 속이는 건 세월에서야.* It should sound like a merchant's maxim.
15. **"He had wanted a gift. He seemed to have started a market."** → 그는 선물 하나를 원했다. 그런데 시장을 하나 열어 버린 모양이었다. This punchline ends the section.

## Scene 3: The room

16. **"Well. That's the door."** (after the magic bar locks out the crowd) → 자. 문은 됐고. Is the meaning "that takes care of that" clear?
17. **"Nobody would notice one baker."** Tor's surname is Baker and his family are bakers. Translated by meaning: 빵집 아들 하나쯤 아무도 눈치 못 채. Does it still sound like a joke?
18. **"Big. Draughty. Full of forks."** (Rolph's home is grand, with formal dining) → 커. 외풍 숭숭 들고. 포크 천지고. Is the hint about formal manners clear?
19. **CRUST vs. skins.** Tor's notes contain 겉껍질 (a crust that seals the outside, like bread) and 껍질 (fruit skins). Is the difference clear to a reader?
20. **"The rest of it might have been in another language. It might have been in several."** → 나머지는 다른 나라 말로 썼다 해도 이상하지 않았다. 여러 나라 말이었대도.

## Scene 4: The next day

21. **"Sir, you *drowned* him." / "I didn't drown him. He's standing there."** → 선배를 *익사시키셨잖아요*. / 익사시키지 않았다. 저기 서 있잖아. 익사 may sound too clinical for a panicking first-year. Is there something funnier and more natural?
22. **Kolb: "No, sir."** → 네, 선생님. This is Korean "yes" agreeing with "you weren't in my yard." Correct, but please check it doesn't confuse.
23. **The theory master: "that is why you can't kill your roommate with a lamp-square, however much you might like to"** → 등불판으로 같은 방 쓰는 친구를 죽일 수 없는 거다. 아무리 그러고 싶어도. A lecture joke; does the rhythm work?
24. **Dorgal: "On a good day, sir, me."** → 잘 풀리는 날이라면, 선생님, 저겠지요. It should be quick and witty.
25. **Kolb: "You owe it an afternoon. We'll start collecting today."** → 넌 거기에 오후 한나절을 빚졌다. 오늘부터 받아 내지. Quietly menacing, like a debt collector. Does it land?
26. **"He's all bark." / "Mostly bark."** → 저 양반 순 엄포야. / 뭐. 거의 엄포야.
27. **"liniment"** → 고약. Would 연고 or something else be more natural for a rub for sore muscles?
